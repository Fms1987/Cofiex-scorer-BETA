## Resumo
Investment loan to a capital municipality, direct delivery through the municipality's own infrastructure secretariat and a dedicated project-management unit. Finances urban-upgrading and mobility works (street repaving, drainage, public lighting, leisure/sports facilities, a tunnel and an overpass, and targeted bus-corridor interventions) in low-HDI neighborhoods, plus a much smaller urban-greening/tree-planting line, program management and financing costs. The urbanization-and-mobility component absorbs roughly 85% of total cost; counterpart is 20% of the total.

## Teoria da mudança e indicadores
A.1 Theory of Change: STRONG. Across all three NT reviews the diagnosis (urban precarity, flood exposure, limited paved/urbanized street network, mobility deficits concentrated in low-HDI neighborhoods) is scored consistently high (justification sub-criterion at roughly 94% of its maximum in every version), and the chain from problems to proposed solutions (urbanization/mobility, environmental sustainability, program management) to expected results is judged coherent and testable in every review. A.2 Results Framework: ADEQUATE. VERIFIED n_indicators=9; baseline_defined_share=1.0 and baseline_with_year_share=1.0 — every indicator carries a quantified baseline referenced to the same base year, with a matching target year roughly seven years out, each with a stated calculation method (GPS-based transit monitoring, a traffic micro-simulation model for emissions, utility billing data, tree-inventory–based carbon estimate). This is the strongest baseline discipline seen among the pleitos reviewed. Outcome-vs-output split is approximate but still output-heavy: the NT itself flags that the repaving/coverage-rate and tree-count indicators "merely replicate physical deliveries" and do not qualify as effectiveness indicators, while the flood-exposure, waterborne-disease, transit-speed/emissions and lighting-efficiency indicators read closer to outcome level. Verification mechanisms are present and indicator-specific (monitoring systems, model-based estimation, billed consumption data). Benchmarks used are limited to national census/urban-services statistical series; no wide-range or internally inconsistent indicators were identified in the final version, though the first two NT reviews noted indicators lacked explicit per-indicator deadlines — a gap the final version resolves by stating the same baseline/target years for all nine indicators.

## Sustentabilidade e riscos
A.5 Financial Sustainability: ADEQUATE. OPEX is quantified as an aggregate increment (about 2% per year of the amount invested in the two largest components), checked against expected growth in property-tax and services-tax collection, a recently created municipal waste-management fee, and more efficient (lower-cost) public lighting; the responsible maintenance agency is named, but the NT across all three reviews notes the cost breakdown lacks detail beyond this single aggregate figure. A.3 Risk Assessment: WEAK in the first two reviews, ADEQUATE in the final one. A risk matrix covers legal, procurement, land-acquisition, environmental and social risk categories; land-acquisition risk is treated with a dedicated survey of affected properties and a stated just/prior-compensation commitment, with no resettlement foreseen since interventions avoid the need to relocate residents. Environmental and social risk detail is judged insufficiently developed in the first two versions ("addressed generically, without adequate detail") and only reaches an adequate rating in the third; environmental licensing is PARTIAL — part of the physical sub-projects already hold licenses or are mid-process, while others have no engineering design yet and so no licensing has started. Counterpart (20% of total cost): TO BE SECURED throughout all three versions — the municipal-council bills creating the project-management unit and authorizing the external borrowing remain pending legislative approval even in the version that was ultimately approved; the NT treats this as a risk mitigated only by "close monitoring" by the municipal administration, not as a closed item. A.4 Implementation: ADEQUATE, improving to STRONG in the final version — a project-management unit under the infrastructure secretariat is already functioning, a multidisciplinary technical team is described in increasing detail across versions, and a SIGS portfolio-performance check of the proponent's own credit-operation track record improved from a "good" rating in the earlier versions to a higher "good" rating in the final one.
