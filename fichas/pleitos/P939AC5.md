## Resumo
- Modality: IDB contingent credit / payment guarantee (no disbursement, no counterpart) covering the state's recurring counter-payments to the private partner of an administrative-concession PPP for a high-complexity public hospital; fits the PPP-specific sublimit.
- The guarantee finances no works directly: private CAPEX (refurbishment, new building, capacity expansion, equipment) and OPEX (non-clinical "grey-coat" services) are paid through the PPP counter-payments; clinical services remain with the public side.
- Single component (guarantee of the state's contractual obligations); it would replace a cash escrow account held under the state's existing dual guarantee scheme (earmarked transfers plus escrow), freeing budget resources. Guarantee fee 0.8% p.a.; if called, a SOFR-plus-spread loan with remaining-guarantee tenor (25 years).
- Executed by the concessionaire; contract supervised by the state health secretariat with an independent verifier; strategic-partnerships office structured the PPP. No PMU.

## Teoria da mudança e indicadores
- A.1 Theory of Change: ADEQUATE. Clear logic for the guarantee (AAA-backed cover lowers perceived payment risk, cost of capital and bid risk; frees escrow cash) and for the hospital outcomes, but assumptions are not tested and the link from financial instrument to health outcomes is indirect; the CC itself states that programme indicators do not necessarily mirror the concession's performance indicators.
- A.2 Results Framework: ADEQUATE/STRONG. 13 indicators verified, approximately 9 outcome/service-level (institutional mortality, coronary-unit mortality, ICU lethality, emergency-room occupancy, turnover, satisfaction, caesarean share, capacity, throughput) and about 4 output/compliance-type (ESG label contributions, board gender share, workforce inclusion share, fiscal-cost saving).
- Baselines: stated for all 13 (share 1.0); with a reference year for roughly 10 of 13 (share 0.77). I counted the two zero baselines for the board-gender and workforce-inclusion indicators as defined because the concessionaire does not yet exist (new entity); these and the annual throughput baseline lack a year.
- Verification: every indicator has a formula, frequency and data source (hospital management reports, independent verifier reports, self-assessment form for the ESG label); targets are point values with dates, some staggered (earlier date for throughput, later for most).
- Weak spots: ESG-label indicator targets merely maintaining the current level (no stretch); the fiscal-saving indicator is a financial-engineering metric tied to a social-discount-rate assumption; a few dates are inconsistent in wording ("reduction" targets for increasing indicators).

## Sustentabilidade e riscos
- A.5 Financial Sustainability: ADEQUATE. Concession CAPEX and OPEX are borne by the concessionaire and repaid via state counter-payments over a long concession; payments backed by earmarked transfers from constitutional participation funds plus a liquidity cushion (escrow, to be replaced by the contingent operation). Mismatches between concession term and guarantee term, and a gap before the guarantee is signed, are acknowledged and covered by keeping the escrow in those periods. Exit-phase safeguards (higher performance bond, reinvestment) described. No explicit annual fiscal-cost table in the CC text.
- A.3 Risk Assessment: ADEQUATE. Four risks with impact/probability: deserted tender, procurement/labour charges (allocated to concessionaire), low institutional capacity of the grantor to supervise a first hospital concession (high impact, medium probability), and public payment default (the risk the instrument addresses). Mitigation includes lender institutional-capacity assessment, training, independent verifier, road show. Licensing is not treated as a risk.
- Counterpart: none required (guarantee, no counterpart); the earmarked-revenue and escrow legal framework (state laws and decree) is cited as already in force.
- A.4 Implementation: no PMU; concessionaire executes, health secretariat supervises with an independent verifier, technical governance committees (interface, hospital inputs, dispute resolution). Supervisory capacity is the stated weak point.
