## Resumo
- Investment project financing (IBRD) to a capital municipality for strengthening the social-assistance network and its service offer to highly vulnerable groups; ticket band 100-200M USD with counterpart at roughly 40% of total cost (well above the 20% minimum), Union guarantee, 5-year disbursement. Disbursement for the services component and part of the network component is to be tied to performance-based conditions agreed with the lender; technical assistance follows lender procurement and safeguards rules.
- Delivered directly by the municipal social-assistance secretariat through a dedicated UGP, with many internal coordination units as technical executors; extensive civil-society partnerships already deliver services.
- Component 1: service and benefit offer (street outreach, modular transitional housing for families, income transfer plus services to overcome child labour, protocols for migrants and older people, emergency and disaster response). Component 2: network strengthening (refurbishment of directly run and rented units, IT infrastructure, distance training, social-assistance surveillance and case-management systems, partnership-management system, project management and technical assistance).

- A.2 Results Framework: ADEQUATE. 13 indicators verified in the CC table (the header's 14 was a heuristic count); about 6 are outcome-type (rates of institutionalisation, reduction of street and child-labour populations, cadastral update) and the rest are output or process measures such as capacity, adequacy of units, training coverage and systems (approximate judgement). No baseline reference year or period is given (0.0). One rate shows 100% at both baseline and target, an apparent inconsistency or parse artefact.
- No separate verification-source column in the CC; the performance-condition protocol with the lender is the implied verification route. Some baselines come from municipal censuses (street population, child labour) mentioned in the diagnosis.

## Sustentabilidade e riscos
- A.5 Financial Sustainability: WEAK. The CC argues the design adds no budget pressure (expanded capacity is carried by municipal counterpart, efficiency gains from automation and energy-efficient refurbishment), but gives no maintenance or recurring-cost figures and no specific instruments; the NT scored this criterion zero. Counterpart is explicitly tied to operating existing services (street outreach, youth centres, social interviewers), i.e. recurring costs rather than project investments.
- A.3 Risk Assessment: ADEQUATE. Legal, procurement, environmental, social and expropriation items covered; risks include works in rented premises, electoral-calendar constraints in two municipal election years, delivery capacity of several concurrent system developments, public perception of street-population policy, and demand surge from better service. Environmental mitigation is deferred to lender preparation; no mitigation given for the electoral-priority-change risk in the legal section.
- Counterpart: aligned with the municipal PPA and sectoral plans; budget credits for procurements stated as already foreseen, but no LOA line or authorising act detailed ("to be secured"). The CC notes its own financing-section figures do not match the component and schedule tables.
- A.4 Implementation: STRONG/ADEQUATE. Dedicated UGP with exclusive-dedication staff and a bidding commission in the executing secretariat, a detailed responsibility matrix across many technical units, and long experience with external-credit operations.
