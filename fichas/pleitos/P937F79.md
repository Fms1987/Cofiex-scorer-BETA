## Resumo
- Investment loan from IDB to a capital municipality, executed directly by the municipal public-works secretariat through a project management unit (UGP); counterpart at the 20% minimum.
- Second phase of an integrated sanitation and urban-upgrading programme in precarious peripheral communities; the first phase is under way with the same lender and the same borrower.
- Four components: (1) environmental, urban, housing and sanitation works (roughly 75% of cost), (2) environmental/social sustainability and institutional strengthening (gender and diversity, job-skills training, social work), (3) engineering and administration, (4) concurrent costs (audit, evaluation, monitoring).
- Component 1 bundles water and sewerage networks, drainage, solid-waste collection points, slope containment, road works, an urban access/transport link, a park, community facilities and resettlement of at-risk households.

## Teoria da mudança e indicadores
- A.1 Theory of Change: ADEQUATE. Problem-solution-result narrative links each specific objective to indicators and to the SDGs, but there is no explicit assumptions chain; the integrated-works logic is asserted rather than tested.
- A.2 Results Framework: WEAK-to-ADEQUATE. 9 indicators (verified count), each tied to a specific objective; approximately 5 are outcome-type (coverage, flooding, waste points, recreational area, job insertion) and approximately 4 are output-like (road works, resettlement, training participation).
- Baselines: only 3 of 9 have a baseline value (water coverage, sewerage coverage, recreational area per inhabitant); none states a reference year. Flooding, waste-point, resettlement, roads and both social indicators are expressed as "100% / x% of" without a baseline value.
- Several targets are 100% by 2031 (full flood elimination, full resettlement, full coverage) which look aspirational; targets embed physical-asset quantities in the text. Verification is by surveys for the social indicators only; no formal verification mechanism for the works indicators.
- No recognised benchmark index used; no wide-range or deferred indicators.

## Sustentabilidade e riscos
- A.5 Financial Sustainability: ADEQUATE in the version judged (v3). Responsible operators are named per asset class (municipal maintenance and public-order secretariats for roads, drainage, parks, waste; the state water utility for water and sewerage under a convention still to be signed). An annual O&M cost table by component is given (roughly 1% of programme cost per year at full build-out), funded from the municipal budget. Earlier version lacked this table (see evaluations).
- A.3 Risk Assessment: ADEQUATE. 7 risks with impact/probability and mitigation (loan authorising law, procurement delay, expropriation, environmental management, urban violence, resettlement, low sewer connection uptake). Environmental licences to be requested in parallel with procurement; an environmental management system is planned. Resettlement plan to be prepared; leverages first-phase experience.
- Counterpart: to be secured; authorising law pending and LDO/LOA provision planned for the following year.
- A.4 Implementation: ADEQUATE-to-STRONG. UGP to be created inside the executing secretariat, but the team of the first phase is to be retained; detailed role profiles; special bidding committee; borrower has a long record with multilateral loans.
