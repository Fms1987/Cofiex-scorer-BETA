## Resumo
- Modality: institutional-strengthening investment loan from IDB to a state government, executed by an autonomous justice-system body (a state prosecution service); counterpart at the 20% minimum; federal guarantee with state constitutional revenue shares as counter-guarantee.
- Finances an integrated data and intelligence platform, modernisation of legacy case-management systems with training and infrastructure, specialised units for asset-tracing against organised crime, and an environmental geoprocessing unit.
- Delivery model: direct execution through a new project management unit (UGP) inside the institution, with existing internal units operating the delivered assets.
- Two components: institutional modernisation (four products, nearly all of the cost) and administration, evaluation and audit.

## Teoria da mudança e indicadores
- A.1 Theory of Change: ADEQUATE. Four specific objectives (information alignment across justice and security bodies, process efficiency, asset-tracing effectiveness, environmental-crime detection) each map to a product and to one or more indicators and PPA areas; assumptions (data-sharing agreements, user adoption) surface in the risk section rather than in an explicit results chain.
- A.2 Results Framework: ADEQUATE. Verified 9 indicators; approximately five outcome/effectiveness (data-crossing time, share of cases with parallel asset-tracing, median illicit assets identified, share of assets frozen, share of environmental cases monitored) and four output/capacity (data sources integrated, proactive investigations, geospatial reports, waste sites monitored). All have a stated baseline and reference year or period (shares 1.0 / 1.0). Counted zero baselines: six, all explained as capabilities that do not exist before the project (new platform or unit). One baseline is a multi-year average because the metric was not recorded earlier; another is a median over the previous five years.
- Verification: present (platform and case-management system data, partner agreements, internal laboratory data); annual and intermediate targets are defined for years 2 to 5 with explicit SMART matrix annex.
- Weaknesses: some targets are small absolute counts, and a share-of-area indicator for environmental monitoring has a modest ceiling; no external benchmarks cited.

## Sustentabilidade e riscos
- A.5 Financial Sustainability: ADEQUATE. No itemised OPEX table; argument rests on the institution's own budget (recurring IT maintenance already funded, a regular modernisation investment line, an in-house IT department) and on a permanent competence unit. Software licences are flagged as the critical recurring cost, expected to fall with in-house expertise and covered partly from the institution's budget. Recurring costs to be absorbed into the regular IT budget after the project.
- A.3 Risk Assessment: ADEQUATE (matrix with impact/probability and mitigation for a long list of risks): delay in data-sharing agreements, low user adoption, equipment delivery, legacy-system complexity (high probability), obsolescence, cyber exposure from inter-agency data flows, licence funding, procurement delays, skills for asset-tracing, institutional change, staffing. Cybersecurity mitigation is a budget line in the project.
- Counterpart: 20% of cost; funding source is the institution's budget via the state; legal backing and budget line not described in detail. Alignment with the state multi-year plan and the institution's own strategic plan is documented.
- A.4 Implementation: ADEQUATE. A new UGP with finance, procurement, monitoring and legal coordinators plus a bid committee; an IT coordination unit and operational units described; the UGP still has to be created and has no multilateral-project track record shown, although the institution has prior federal-credit portfolios.
