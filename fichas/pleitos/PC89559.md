## Resumo
- Investment operation (direct credit, subnational borrower) for a capital municipality recovering from a major flood disaster; financed activity is climate-resilience and flood-protection infrastructure plus related social and institutional measures.
- Delivery is direct: a program management unit inside the planning secretariat coordinates execution, with line agencies (water/drainage utility, housing agency, public-works and environment secretariats) as co-executors for their respective products.
- Main components: resilient infrastructure (flood-protection/drainage works, access-road and mobility works, housing for the vulnerable population), risk-management and innovation studies (contingency plan, flood monitoring, water-security plan, mobility observatory/lab), social infrastructure and workforce qualification, and program management/supervision/audit.
- Entity type: capital-municipality government with prior track record of external-credit operations.

## Teoria da mudança e indicadores
- A.1 Theory of Change: STRONG. Explicit chain from diagnosed problem (flood-protection/drainage failure, housing and road damage, missing risk-management tools) to proposed solutions to objectives to expected results; coherence between these layers was rated near the maximum in the underlying review.
- A.2 Results Framework: ADEQUATE. 11 verified indicators; most are outcome-type (system efficiency %, infrastructure capacity, access-road extension) but an approximate third are pure output/completion indicators (plan "homologated and implemented" =100%), which the review itself flagged as not clearly reflecting the expected result.
- Baseline: 6/11 indicators (55%) have a stated non-zero baseline value, all with a reference year — same share for `baseline_with_year_share`. The other 5 indicators carry an unexplained zero baseline (flood-protection efficiency; two "plan homologated" output indicators; two gender/youth participation-increase indicators whose formula structurally yields a zero base term); none of these zeros comes with an explicit CC justification, so all were counted as undefined.
- Verification mechanism: absent from the indicator table (only description/formula/unit/baseline/target columns; no stated data source or frequency); NT is silent on this point too.
- No external benchmark (e.g. SNIS, ISO 37120) is used to calibrate indicator targets. No wide-range or deferred indicators were found; all targets are point values.
- Internal inconsistency noted by the NT itself: at least one indicator (water-treatment-station efficiency) has vague "adequate functioning" parameters, and another measures product delivery (plan approval) rather than the outcome it is meant to proxy.

## Sustentabilidade e riscos
- A.5 Financial sustainability: ADEQUATE. O&M is not presented as a single multi-year cost table, but funding sources are identified by agency (drainage/sanitation utility funded by water/sewage tariff revenue; roads secretariat funded by its own annual maintenance budget line; housing actions funded via federal programs), each backed by an annual budget-law (LOA) appropriation. The CC itself cautions that post-disaster years cannot guarantee full annual maintenance funding because of expected revenue losses, but argues resilience investment pays off via reduced future damage.
- A.3 Risk assessment: STRONG. Dedicated risk sections cover legal/institutional acts, procurement, expropriation, environmental impact, social impact and involuntary resettlement, each with explicit mitigating actions (e.g. early title-identification for expropriations, approach to environmental licensing bodies, grievance-redress channel for resettlement, use of an emergency decree to allow direct contracting for urgent studies).
- Counterpart: secured in principle — funded from constitutionally-assigned municipal tax revenue under the COFIEX counterpart resolution, and the broader program is stated to be inserted in the municipal multi-year investment plan and annual budget laws; however no dedicated, dated counterpart budget line or schedule is shown, so this reads closer to "legally grounded but not itemized" than a specific appropriated line.
- A.4 Implementation (UGP): STRONG. A program management unit sits within the planning/strategic-affairs secretariat with dedicated coordination tracks (general, technical-administrative, procurement, fiduciary) plus specialized consultancy support; the borrower has a long track record of executing external-credit operations with multiple multilateral and regional lenders.
