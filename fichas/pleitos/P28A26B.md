## Resumo
- Investment loan to a capital-region municipality (small city government) for a multi-theme urban programme, executed directly by the municipality; no intermediation.
- Five components: studies/designs/licensing (a few % of cost), sustainable urban infrastructure with paving, drainage and territorial upgrading (roughly 40%), public and community facilities such as squares, sports courts and a gymnasium (roughly a quarter), renewable energy plus an industrial-eco-district (roughly a fifth), programme management (small share).
- Counterpart at the 20% minimum; part of the counterpart works was already completed or under way when the CC was filed.
- Heterogeneous scope (mobility, green infrastructure, sport, municipal solar generation, burial infrastructure, industrial land) makes the programme thematically scattered.

## Teoria da mudança e indicadores
- A.1 Theory of Change: ADEQUATE. Problem tree with nine diagnosed problems, causes and consequences, mapped to axes and objectives; assumptions are not made explicit or testable.
- A.2 Results Framework: ADEQUATE-to-WEAK. 11 indicators (verified), all SMART-templated; approximate split about 8 outcome / 3 output-type (area requalified, installed capacity, burial capacity). Several are national PPA key indicators.
- Baselines: 9 of 11 have a stated value (share 0.82), with reference year or period for the same 9 (mostly a 2025 T0, others a 2023 or 2022 series). Zero baselines counted as defined because they refer to new assets (green-area share, access to new public spaces, capacity of the requalified facility). Undefined: installed PV capacity ("to be surveyed in initial studies") and burial capacity (qualitative description, no value).
- Internal issues: the sanitation index is a composite of three sub-measures with a single combined baseline; the water-body quality target, the sanitation targets and the productivity target are loosely tied to what the programme builds; the productivity target value is left to be set from future statistics (deferred); the first indicator cites PPA objectives not in the declared set.
- Verification: engineering surveys, municipal records, georeferenced data, SNIS/SINISA and IBGE series are named as sources, but no independent verification schedule is given. Benchmarks: SNIS/SINISA.

O&M is assigned to the infrastructure secretariat and funded from the annual budget (LOA) under the municipal PPA; annual upkeep is estimated as 1-3% of invested value (quantified as a percentage rule, not a line-item table). Fiscal capacity is asserted, not demonstrated; no revenue model for the solar plant or industrial district.
- A.3 Risk Assessment: ADEQUATE. Seven risks with impact/probability and mitigation (procurement delay, failed tenders, contractor default, intersectoral coordination, works disruption, expropriation/resettlement, environmental licensing). Licensing is treated as a generic risk; no per-component licensing status.
- Counterpart: municipal law authorising the external credit and budget allocation is cited; counterpart is front-loaded in year 1 and partly already executed as works; LOA/PPA lines for the counterpart itself are not evidenced.
- A.4 Implementation: ADEQUATE-to-WEAK. A programme management unit is described but still "to be created" under the resource-raising secretariat; execution split among several secretariats with a central finance function; external audit and supervision consultancies foreseen.
