## Resumo
Subnational PPP-based investment operation (direct lending to a state government) financing the civil works and systems for an additional phase of an existing mass-transit concession, plus an institutional-strengthening component for the state's PPP/concession oversight bodies. Delivery is intermediated through the incumbent private concessionaire for the physical works (under the existing PPP contract, with contractual rebalancing covering the concessionaire's share) and direct for the institutional component (staff certification, process digitalization/BI tooling). Three components: civil works, systems, and technical/institutional strengthening. Entity type: state government with an established multi-contract PPP/concessions programme and prior external-credit experience. This is a second attempt for the same project (first attempt also approved in an earlier quarter) and is a separate, earlier, larger-scope tranche of the same underlying extension later topped up by an operations-only pleito.

## Teoria da mudança e indicadores
A.1 Theory of Change: STRONG — same clear causal chain as the sister operation (unserved corridor to a capital-adjacent municipality → physical extension + systems → ridership/time/emissions outcomes), plus a second, distinct chain for the institutional-strengthening component (manual, under-resourced PPP/concession oversight → staff certification + process digitalization/BI → faster, more transparent contract monitoring). Both chains are explicit and the design score is again near-maximum.
A.2 Results Framework: ADEQUATE/STRONG (approximate) — VERIFIED n=7 indicators: 4 mirror the sister operation (ridership, travel time, emissions, social time gain) plus 3 new ones tied to the institutional component (certified staff, frequency of tariff-review reports, time to grant case-file access). Outcome/output split is roughly even to output-leaning once the 3 institutional indicators (output-type: counts, counts, days) are added to the 1 output + 3 outcome transit indicators. Baseline rule applied: the 2 transit indicators with an explicit non-zero baseline, plus the 2 transit indicators with an explained counterfactual zero, plus the "periodicidade de relatórios" indicator with a stated non-zero baseline (1/month) count as defined; the ridership indicator here has an unexplained zero baseline (no counterfactual framing given, unlike its value in the sister CC) and the "funcionários certificados" indicator has an unexplained zero baseline — both counted as undefined. That gives 5 of 7 defined ≈ 0.71 (not quite the ≥80% SMART threshold on its own, so A.2 is called ADEQUATE/STRONG rather than flatly STRONG). No indicator states a separate baseline reference year, so the year-qualified share is 0. Verification mechanisms are mixed: direct registries/reports for 3 indicators, demand/emissions modelling for 2, and unspecified internal tracking for the institutional indicators. No external benchmark used. No wide-range or deferred indicators.

## Sustentabilidade e riscos
A.5 Financial Sustainability: STRONG — same contractual-rebalancing mechanism as the sister operation (added fare revenue, tariff increase, concession-term extension), with a multi-year cash-flow/income projection presented covering the whole concession horizon and a stated internal rate of return; no public-budget impact asserted; legally embedded in the existing concession contract.
A.3 Risk Assessment: STRONG — dedicated risk matrix covering legal/institutional risk (pending contract addendum, expropriation decree, authorizing law), contracting risk (low, delegated to the incumbent concessionaire), geotechnical/construction risk for the tunnel works, and environmental/social risk (soil/groundwater, air quality, vegetation removal, expropriation impact, temporary service disruption), each with stated mitigations; no resettlement or impact on traditional/indigenous populations identified; same risk-sharing logic as the sister CC, now for civil works (which carry materially larger construction risk than the systems-only tranche).
Counterpart: to be secured — counterpart financing is committed at the regulatory minimum with a legal basis, but the CC states the specific budget line only appears in the following fiscal year's budget-law proposal, so it is not yet enacted.
A.4 Implementation (UGP quality): ADEQUATE — institutional/RACI matrix presented, executing agency familiar with lender safeguards, delivery split between the incumbent concessionaire (civil works, systems) and the state's own PPP-oversight bodies (institutional component); however the CC itself diagnoses those oversight bodies as currently under-resourced and reliant on manual processes, which is the explicit rationale for the institutional component — a self-identified capacity gap being addressed, not yet resolved. Prior external-credit track record again rated only "regular" (mid-tier) by the NT.
Environmental licensing: in progress, not yet issued, same status as the sister operation.
