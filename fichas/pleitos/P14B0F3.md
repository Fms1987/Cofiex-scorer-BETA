## Resumo
- Investment operation (external credit), direct modality: borrower is a capital municipality executing through its own works/infrastructure secretariat, not an intermediated on-lending scheme.
- Finances urban mobility and public-space requalification in a central district: exclusive bus lanes and a bus-integration terminal, public-space/sidewalk and micro-drainage requalification, cycle-path works, plus a resettlement/compensation line tied to the terminal footprint.
- Complementary components: a gender-focused workforce-qualification program for construction jobs, air-quality monitoring, external audit, and a support team for the program management unit (UGP).
- Entity type: capital municipality, infrastructure/urban-mobility sector; delivery is direct through municipal secretariats and co-executing sectoral agencies (no PPP/concession involved).

- A.1 Theory of Change: STRONG. CC lays an explicit diagnosis (mode-share imbalance favoring private motorized transport, poor pedestrian/cycling conditions, long bus dwell times) linked causally to the proposed works and to stated specific objectives (bus-speed gains, wait-time reduction, emissions mitigation, accessibility, female workforce share); NT's own design sub-scores confirm coherence between problem, solution and expected results.

## Sustentabilidade e riscos
- A.5 Financial sustainability: OPEX is quantified (unit maintenance costs per m² for roads and plazas, rolled up to an approximate multi-year maintenance total in the <50M USD band) and the funding source is identified as the municipal operating budget; no dedicated maintenance fund or earmarking law is cited beyond that budget commitment.
- Counterpart (20% of cost, per header): status "to be secured" — the authorizing municipal law for the credit operation is still being drafted for submission to the municipal legislature, and the UGP itself is only to be formally created by municipal decree/ordinance after approval.
- A.3 Risk assessment: a structured set of risk categories is presented (legal acts/authorizations, procurement, expropriation/resettlement, environmental impact, social impact), each with identified mitigation measures; the financier's procurement and safeguard requirements (comparable to those of a major multilateral) are explicitly accepted by the proponent given prior credit-operation experience.
- Licensing: environmental and legal authorizations are stated to be the municipality's own responsibility, with no inter-agency agreements required; no itemized per-component licensing-status table is shown.
- Resettlement: a non-trivial number of households/mixed-use units within the terminal footprint require resettlement or indemnification; the exact count is still preliminary (desk/aerial estimate), with the proponent expecting to reduce the affected-household count materially through footprint revision, and legal instruments (land regularization laws, municipal housing policy) are cited as the basis for compensation.
- A.4 Implementation: UGP led by the municipal works/infrastructure secretariat with several co-executing sectoral units (transit, urbanization, drainage/zoning agencies); institutional arrangement and responsibility matrix rated adequate by the NT, and the proponent's average performance record with external financiers is cited as satisfactory.
