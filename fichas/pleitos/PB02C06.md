## Resumo
Investment operation with a municipal government borrower, financed by NDB, delivered directly by the municipality through a newly created municipal operating company rather than an intermediated structure. Core intervention is a restructuring of the public-transport system: direct municipal management of the service, acquisition of a mostly-electric bus fleet with associated charging infrastructure, complementary road/urban-mobility works, a mobility-innovation hub run jointly with a local technology park, and a monitoring/control system for the mobility network. Components cover fleet and charging-infrastructure acquisition, mobility infrastructure, system monitoring, the innovation hub, road works, and program management/oversight.

## Teoria da mudança e indicadores
A.1 Theory of Change: STRONG — the NT highlights a clear, research-backed (university-partnered study) causal chain from the diagnosed problem (passenger evasion and the system's financial unsustainability under the prior model) to the proposed solution (direct municipal management, fleet electrification, complementary infrastructure and a mobility hub) to the expected outcomes (service quality, emissions, cost and transparency gains), explicitly rated with the NT's top score band for this criterion.
A.2 Results Framework: ADEQUATE-to-STRONG — VERIFIED count: 9 indicators, matching the header. Outcome-vs-output split is approximately two-thirds outcome/effectiveness indicators (ridership, travel time, satisfaction/quality scores, accessibility and comfort perception, emissions reduction, fleet operating-cost ratio) and about a third output indicators (hub workshops held, a road-corridor travel-time target that is itself close to an output). Benchmarks used: an internationally recognised GHG-accounting protocol for the emissions indicator; periodic satisfaction surveys with an established methodology for perception indicators. All 9 indicators carry an explicit baseline year (2022–2024) and a numeric baseline value; one indicator (annual workshops at the new mobility hub) has a zero baseline, which we counted as defined/new-asset since the hub itself is a program deliverable with no prior activity to measure — flagged as a judgment call. Recomputed baseline_defined_share = 1.0 (9/9); baseline_with_year_share = 1.0. No wide-range targets were found. NT is silent on a distinct verification-mechanism/data-source column, though several indicators cite their own data source (survey series, GHG calculation tool, market cost comparison) inline.

## Sustentabilidade e riscos
A.5 Financial Sustainability: STRONG — operating costs after restructuring are quantified and split between fare revenue and a municipal budget subsidy, with the subsidy portion stated to be provided for in the 2022-2025 multi-year plan (PPA); maintenance/operation costs attributable to the program itself are also quantified separately. The new municipal operating company created to run the system gives a clear institutional/legal home for O&M funding.
A.3 Risk Assessment: STRONG — a detailed risk matrix covers legal/institutional acts, procurement, expropriation and resettlement, and environmental/social impacts, each with mitigation measures, and the NT gives this criterion its top score. Environmental licensing status is reported per individual work/component rather than generically. Resettlement is explicitly triggered for households occupying a federal-land easement; negotiations with the federal land agency are underway and two resettlement options are offered (replacement municipal housing or a rental subsidy) — a flagged resettlement/social-safeguard item.
Counterpart: matches the CC exactly (header and CC both ~25%); already backed by the municipality's own multi-year budget plan (PPA 2022-2025) for the operating-subsidy side, while the authorising law for the external-credit contract itself is still pending Cofiex approval before being finalised — i.e. "mostly secured, loan-authorisation step still to be completed," not yet fully secured in the Q6 sense.
A.4 Implementation: ADEQUATE — institutional arrangement includes a responsibility matrix, an organisational chart, and a UGP; the proponent explicitly lacks prior experience executing external-credit operations (per the NT's own system check), which the applicable scoring rule treats as a neutral/full-marks case for "quality of requirement" rather than a penalty, but it remains a first-time-borrower execution-capacity flag. Preparatory work is advanced: the redesign has been under study since a multi-year-old research partnership, procurement processes are underway, and a pilot electrified-corridor project is already operating.
