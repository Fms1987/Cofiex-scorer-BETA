## Resumo
Investment loan (direct, not PBL/DPL and not on-lending) for a subnational entity. Finances a new urban arterial avenue (including bridges, macro-drainage and paving) designed to divert heavy regional through-traffic away from a saturated federal-highway corridor, plus community facilities (public leisure/sports spaces, multiuse rooms, smart LED street lighting with public wifi) along the new corridor. The operation is explicitly one segment of a larger, multi-municipality avenue concept: a prior segment is already built and a further segment was already authorized by COFIEX in an earlier cycle. Components: studies and works, expropriations, and program management. Delivery is direct execution by the municipal government through a program management unit (UGP) to be created by decree, with external technical/operational support.

## Teoria da mudança e indicadores
A.1 Theory of Change: STRONG — the CC builds an explicit, traffic-study-backed causal chain (diagnosed corridor saturation and projected traffic growth → new avenue diverts a quantified share of traffic → reduced travel time/congestion/accidents; separately, lack of community facilities → new public leisure/multiuse spaces → access and economic-activity benefits).
A.2 Results Framework: ADEQUATE. VERIFIED indicator count: 5, all SMART-formulated with explicit formulas, units, a stated baseline year (2023) and target year (2028) for every indicator — baseline_with_year_share = 1.0. One indicator's baseline is zero (diverted traffic volume), but the CC explicitly explains it as a flow that does not exist today on the new, not-yet-built avenue, so it is counted as a defined (explained) baseline — baseline_defined_share = 1.0. Outcome-vs-output split: approximately outcome-focused (travel-time reduction, diverted-traffic volume, cycling and public-space usage, peak-hour travel time are all behavioral/use outcomes rather than simple physical-output counts). No explicit verification/monitoring mechanism (data source or responsible party for post-implementation measurement) is stated, which keeps the rating at ADEQUATE rather than STRONG. No external benchmarks used; alignment is cited to SDG targets, not to indicator benchmarks. No wide-range or deferred indicators.

## Sustentabilidade e riscos
A.5 Financial Sustainability: STRONG — the CC includes a dedicated sustainability annex with a quantified annual O&M estimate for the avenue, lighting and community facilities, funded from the municipality's own tax revenue and an urban-service contribution, channeled through existing infrastructure-maintenance contracts and current staff (no new hiring planned).
A.3 Risk Assessment: STRONG. Category-by-category risk sections (legal acts, procurement, expropriation, environmental, social, resettlement) each with concrete mitigation measures, and the CC explicitly leverages precedent from an already-built, technically similar segment of the same avenue concept (no major licensing issues faced there). Expropriation affects a moderate number of properties (no occupied residences), with a dedicated expropriation plan already underway. Environmental licensing follows a phased, agency-assigned roadmap (preliminary and installation licenses), with jurisdiction already resolved in favor of the state environmental agency rather than the federal one. No resettlement is expected; no indigenous or traditional populations identified in the intervention area.
Counterpart: to be secured but with a defined legislative pathway — the authorizing borrowing law is to be submitted to the municipal council only after COFIEX's recommendation, with the CC citing a favorable legislative relationship as mitigation; CAPAG is stated as top tier.
A.4 Implementation (UGP): STRONG — the UGP is to be created by decree, linked to the infrastructure secretariat, with a dedicated technical/legal team for procurement and expropriation oversight plus external operational support, and the CC notes coordination with the neighboring capital municipality executing the adjoining avenue segment.
