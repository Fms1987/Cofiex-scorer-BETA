## Resumo
- Modality: single investment loan from IDB to a state government with Union guarantee; counterpart at the 20% minimum; 4-year disbursement plus a long grace period.
- Finances international internet connectivity infrastructure with its terrestrial landing and interconnection station (the dominant share of cost, roughly 60%), expansion and modernisation of the state fibre backbone and public data centre, interconnection with small telecom providers, connection of public establishments and schools, and community hotspots plus digital-skills training for low-income, traditional and indigenous communities.
- Three components: international connectivity, state network modernisation/expansion/inclusion (seven products), and project management with technical studies.
- Delivery is direct: the state ICT company is the executor and technical lead, with the planning secretariat coordinating the credit operation; a project management unit is to be set up. Part of the school and management products is financed by counterpart.

## Teoria da mudança e indicadores
- A.1 Theory of Change: ADEQUATE. Diagnosis of coverage, price and quality gaps leads to infrastructure plus inclusion products and to expected effects on prices, adoption and digital skills; the chain is narrative, with no explicit testable assumptions.
- A.2 Results Framework: ADEQUATE-to-WEAK (NT scored the item low). 15 indicators verified; approximately 5-6 outcome-type (affordability of fixed and mobile broadband, coverage in traditional and indigenous communities, share of households served via small providers, network reach) and approximately 9-10 output or service-level (installed capacity, service-level availability, connected establishments, school bandwidth, hotspots, training hours).
- All 15 have a baseline value; zero baselines for hotspots and training hours were counted (new assets/programme). No reference year is stated for any baseline, hence the year share is 0; no target dates or intermediate milestones (NT notes the gap), and no externality indicators.
- Verification: formulas given, with national survey and regulator data named for the affordability indicators only; otherwise no source or verification mechanism.
- Inconsistencies: the data-centre and fibre service-level indicators share identical baseline and target; the mobile-affordability formula appears copied from the fixed-broadband one; the connectivity indicators measure capacity, with a single school/establishment-connection output standing in for last-mile use.

## Sustentabilidade e riscos
- A.5 Financial Sustainability: WEAK-to-ADEQUATE. CC argues the state ICT company will earn from selling surplus capacity, avoid third-party capacity purchases and the state will gain tax base; a 10-year NPV/IRR scenario is in an annex. NT calls the treatment vague (customer growth, market share and revenue not substantiated). Annual O&M of the new fibre and landing infrastructure is not quantified and no legal instrument assigns who pays.
- A.3 Risk Assessment: ADEQUATE-to-WEAK. Legal, procurement and environmental risks are discussed; procurement in a market with very few suppliers is flagged with a legal study deferred to the future; social and resettlement sections are blank. Environmental licensing is required for the international link and is delegated largely to the equipment supplier, with the state giving "support" (NT criticises the diluted responsibility).
- Counterpart: 20% shown; legal basis cited in state law, LOA line not shown.
- A.4 Implementation: executor is a state ICT company with experience in network projects; the responsibility matrix is shallow (a single executor for all components) and the project team is not described; NT SIGS score for past credit operations is 53.5/100 (mid-range).
- Disbursement timing: massive disbursement of the dominant component in year 1 (NT flag).
