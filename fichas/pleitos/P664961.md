## Resumo
- Investment loan (CAF, USD band below 50M, counterpart at the 20% minimum) to a mid-sized municipality that is a regional services and cultural hub; direct execution by the municipality with a federal guarantee. Floating SOFR-based rate with a modest spread, 5-year grace, 17.5-year total term, semiannual payments, commitment and one-off fees.
- Multi-sector urban programme with 4 components: (1) drainage, road and sidewalk rehabilitation, a new nature park on a hillside, and LED street lighting plus rooftop solar on public buildings (roughly half of cost); (2) heritage restoration and tourism infrastructure plus a tourism training centre (roughly a sixth); (3) social infrastructure: expansion of primary-care units and municipal schools, and a sports arena with its surroundings (roughly a fifth); (4) management, supervision, audit, evaluation and financing fee (under a tenth).
- Delivery: a programme management unit within the infrastructure secretariat, supported by supervision and management consultancies, with many sector secretariats operating the assets afterwards.
- Two attempts: the first was not approved for an Annex I information gap; the second (same cost structure) was approved.

## Teoria da mudança e indicadores
- A.1 Theory of Change: ADEQUATE. Each problem is linked to a cause-consequence list, a proposed solution and an expected result, grouped by axis; there is no explicit causal chain with testable assumptions, and the packaging of drainage, culture, health and schools under one goal is loosely integrated.
- A.2 Results Framework: ADEQUATE. 11 indicators (verified), approximately 6 outcome-type (flood points, two travel times, visitors/participants, energy cost, people with access) and 5 output-type (road length, green area, cultural spaces, trained people, sports facility count). Each has a source agency and annual or semiannual reporting; no external benchmarks.
- Baselines: all 11 carry a baseline value and a reference year (2024 or 2025). Four zero baselines were counted as defined because the CC explains them (new botanical park, no prior restoration, no prior sports works, no prior training actions). Hence defined share and with-year share are both 1.0.
- Weaknesses: very aggressive travel-time targets (a drop of roughly three quarters versus baseline); several formulas written as "before + after" rather than a difference; sports target is a single facility; the beneficiary indicator relies on population-base estimates; no indicators for the health-unit and school works despite their weight in the component; some indicators tagged to rights/inclusion areas without a matching result.
- Wide-range or deferred indicators: none.

## Sustentabilidade e riscos
- A.5 Financial Sustainability: ADEQUATE to WEAK. O&M responsibility is assigned per asset to the relevant secretariat; health units draw on the national health system and schools on the basic-education fund, everything else on the municipal treasury. No OPEX table is given; instead the CC claims maintenance savings (about 60-70% on roads and drainage) and an energy-cost reduction of about 30%. Operating plans and possible private-sector involvement for the tourism and cultural venues are still being studied.
- A.3 Risk Assessment: ADEQUATE. A matrix of 8 risks with impact and probability, mitigations and owners (legal/authorising law and debt capacity, procurement, environmental, social, expropriation, resettlement, compensation, construction). Environmental and social risks are rated low/low, which looks optimistic given hillside slope works and heritage buildings. Expropriation and resettlement are declared not planned.
- Licensing status: preliminary licences requested for the drainage works; the culture and tourism licences wait for finished designs; basic designs are in progress, and tender documents are expected only after the loan is signed.
- Counterpart (20%): to be secured. The municipal PPA is in force and the annual budget bill was submitted; a specific authorising law for the loan is still listed as a mitigation item.
- A.4 Implementation: ADEQUATE to WEAK. A programme management unit sits in the infrastructure secretariat with a small technical team; the CC itself says the team has no experience with multilateral loans and leans on supervision and management consultancies. Responsibility is spread over about ten secretariats.
