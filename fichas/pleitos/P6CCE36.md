## Resumo
- Modality: investment loan from IBRD to a state government, 8-year disbursement, long total term with a multi-year grace; counterpart at the 20% minimum.
- Finances performance-based road rehabilitation and maintenance under design-build-maintenance (DBM) contracts, plus studies and structuring support for PPP-type road maintenance concessions (including a financed counter-payment product).
- Four components: a dominant road maintenance/construction component (~90% of cost), then institutional strengthening (climate resilience, road-safety plan and enforcement equipment, a pilot training programme for women and Black workers), targeted road-safety works near schools and health facilities, and project management/M&E.
- Delivery model is direct: the state roads department runs the UGP; the state planning, finance and legal bodies support. Cross-cutting themes: road safety (Vision Zero, iRAP), climate resilience, rainwater capture in drainage.

## Teoria da mudança e indicadores
- A.1 Theory of Change: ADEQUATE. Six problem-solution pairs (reactive input-based maintenance, access, crashes, climate, water, social exclusion) map to 15 specific objectives and 12 expected results; assumptions are not made explicit and many objectives are tagged to several PPA areas.
- A.2 Results Framework: ADEQUATE/WEAK. 15 indicators, roughly 4 outcome-type (road condition, deaths, plan coverage, share under long-term contracts) and the rest outputs or counts of activities (approximate judgement). Sources and responsible bodies are stated for most; formulas are given.
- Baselines (strict count): only 2 of 15 have a stated baseline value with year (road deaths; share of network under long-term contracts) = 0.13. Eleven indicators carry an unexplained zero baseline (mostly a 2026 reference), not counted; two safety-rate indicators say "to be defined during preparation" and also defer their targets (deferred_indicators).
- Inconsistencies: the crash-rate indicator is worded as crashes with victims but its formula uses deaths; several targets are round numbers (100% road condition, one coverage target repeated for two indicators) without derivation; the NT notes some indicators are not fully SMART.
- Benchmarks: iRAP star rating, IRI roughness, and another state's DBM/PPP cost savings (a more conservative saving is used for the target).

## Sustentabilidade e riscos
- A.5 Financial Sustainability: ADEQUATE. Post-project maintenance is to be carried by a dedicated state infrastructure fund; unit costs per road kilometre are compared for traditional, first-cycle and second-cycle contracts (savings roughly a quarter, rising slightly in cycle 2). During the project, PPP counter-payments are paid from loan proceeds; afterwards from own resources. No multi-year OPEX table or legal instrument for the fund is described.
- A.3 Risk Assessment: ADEQUATE/STRONG. Nine risks with impact/probability and mitigations: legal acts, non-payment (state CAPAG B), procurement disputes and failed tenders, contractor undercapitalisation (bid and performance guarantees), expropriation/resettlement (ESS-5 referenced), environmental and social impacts. Licensing: DBM-type works treated as low impact with exemption; ESF instruments (environmental and social framework, stakeholder and labour plans) to be prepared.
- Counterpart: 20% minimum stated; LOA/PPA backing not demonstrated beyond alignment of the programme with the state multi-year plan; state legislative authorisation for the loan still required.
- A.4 Implementation: ADEQUATE. UGP inside the roads department with procurement, financial, safeguards and engineering staff; RACI matrix annexed; supporting state bodies have prior multilateral project experience; staff trained by the lender on the contract model.
