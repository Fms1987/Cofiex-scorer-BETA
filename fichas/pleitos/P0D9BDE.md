## Resumo
- Modality: investment loan from IDB to a mid-sized municipality, 5-year implementation, 25-year total term with a 5-year grace; counterpart at the 20% minimum.
- Finances an integrated urban upgrading programme in several low-income hillside communities: socioeconomic development, housing improvements with technical assistance, integrated urbanisation and urban resilience, institutional strengthening, and programme governance.
- Five components: housing improvements (roughly 40%) and urbanisation/resilience (roughly 45%) dominate; socioeconomic, institutional and governance components are small shares.
- Delivery model is direct and multi-secretariat under a programme decree and a working-group decree; utilities and service concessionaires are partners for operation of networks.

## Teoria da mudança e indicadores
- A.1 Theory of Change: ADEQUATE. Five diagnosed problems (inequality, housing deficit, incomplete infrastructure, risk areas, fragmented policy) with causes and consequences map to four specific objectives and component-level expected results; assumptions and external dependencies are not made explicit.
- A.2 Results Framework: ADEQUATE/STRONG. 9 indicators, roughly 8 outcome-type (income inclusion, women's productive inclusion, precarious housing share, sanitation adequacy, youth productive insertion, transparency index, green cover, violent-death rate) and about 1 process/output (effectiveness of technical-assistance service). Each is documented along SMART headings with data, formula and verification; several are adapted national PPA key indicators and the NT found the set sufficient.
- Baselines: 7 of 9 have a stated baseline value = 0.78. Two zero baselines counted because the CC explains them (no consolidated data for youth insertion; a new index for the communities). Not counted: the technical-assistance indicator (new service, no baseline set) and the green-cover indicator (baseline to be built from initial mapping). Only 3 of 9 state a reference year (housing 2023, sanitation census year, violence 2025) = 0.33.
- Targets are mostly relative changes (for example a 70% cut in precarious housing, a 10% gain in sanitation) over 5 years; the housing baseline is given as counts of dwellings rather than a rate; the income-inclusion baseline relies on an approximate proxy.
- Benchmarks: PPA key national indicators, census sanitation data, a transparency-governance index methodology, national social-registry data.

## Sustentabilidade e riscos
- A.5 Financial Sustainability: ADEQUATE. Annual post-project maintenance values are quantified per component (socioeconomic, housing with a multi-year works warranty, urban infrastructure and facilities, institutional); recurring costs are to be absorbed by the municipal multi-year plan and by partner secretariats and utilities. No explicit budget-line or legal instrument by year is shown.
- A.3 Risk Assessment: ADEQUATE. Nine risks with impact/probability and mitigations: licensing delays, environmental (slopes, erosion), resettlement/indemnity, inter-secretariat cooperation, contracting, community resistance, parallel-power access to territories, macro-economic/FX, engineering on unstable terrain. Several rated high impact and medium or high probability.
- Counterpart: 20% stated with yearly schedule; secured through PPA absorption only claimed, LOA not cited.
- A.4 Implementation: ADEQUATE. Dedicated programme management component and decree-based working group; responsibility matrix annexed; multi-secretariat dependence is itself a flagged risk.
