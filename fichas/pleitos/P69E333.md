## Resumo
- Modality: investment loan from NDB to a capital municipality (Res. 17/2021 regime), with federal guarantee; counterpart at the 20% minimum, ticket in the 100-200M USD band, 5-year disbursement, 20-year total term with a long grace period.
- Financed: a two-theme urban programme: (i) urban sanitation/stormwater (drainage gallery rehabilitation, an interceptor collector and a physical-chemical treatment station to remove polluted discharges to beaches) and (ii) urban mobility (a BRT corridor with terminal and stations, plus road-junction improvements on a second arterial).
- Delivery: direct execution by the municipal infrastructure secretariat through a UEP reused from a running CAF-financed programme; works are roughly 95% of cost, with small shares for studies, supervision, audit and the bank fee. BRT buses are outside scope (to be supplied by concessionaires).
- Entity: municipal government; four components (studies, works, programme management, financing fee).

## Teoria da mudança e indicadores
- A.1 Theory of Change: ADEQUATE. Problem-solution-result chain is clear for each theme (illegal sewage connections into stormwater galleries -> intercept and treat -> cleaner bathing water -> tourism; congested bus corridor -> BRT and junction works -> faster trips, fewer buses, lower GHG), but assumptions (modal shift, concessionaire fleet delivery, tourism uptake) are not made explicit or testable.
- A.2 Results Framework: ADEQUATE. 7 indicators verified; about 6 are outcome-type (bathing-water quality, monthly ridership, trip times, bus flow, emissions) and 1 is closer to output (count of polluting discharge points).
- All 7 have a stated baseline value and target (7/7 with reference year; none is a zero baseline); baseline years are mixed (mostly one or two years before the target, one is a decade old) and bathing quality is pinned to a single month.
- Verification: named sources for most (state environmental agency lab results under the CONAMA bathing-water resolution, ticketing data, municipal secretariat data); the trip-time indicator is an estimate and the GHG indicator has a loose formula and unit.
- Weaknesses: no deferred or wide-range indicators; some targets are modest vs the investment (e.g. ridership); sanitation objectives on tourist stays/visitors have no indicator; indicator ownership and periodicity not stated.

## Sustentabilidade e riscos
- A.5 Financial Sustainability: ADEQUATE. CC defers the topic to an annex; the NT (which cites the annex content) shows O&M estimated as a share of investment (about 2-3% a year for the treatment station, about 1.5% for mobility works), carried by existing municipal drainage, roads, lighting and traffic units under annual LOA lines and routine maintenance contracts; contractor operates the station for the first 12 months. No dedicated legal backing for the BRT operating model beyond an existing transit concession.
- A.3 Risk Assessment: ADEQUATE. Narrative risk sections cover legal acts, procurement, environment, social impact, expropriation and resettlement (none expected, no indigenous or quilombola groups); no risk matrix with likelihood/impact. Environmental licensing: preliminary licence stage, installation licence expected in a few months, municipal agency is the licensor.
- Counterpart: not yet secured. Budget lines to be included in the LOA only after COFIEX recommendation; municipal authorising law to be sent to the council after approval; counterpart origin argued to comply with Res. COFIEX 3/2019 (tax revenue).
- A.4 Implementation: ADEQUATE to STRONG on paper. Existing UEP and multi-secretariat responsibility matrix, prior MDB experience; proponent execution track record rated only "regular" in the NT. Readiness is mixed: drainage studies and basic design done, BRT executive design under way, road tunnel pre-design done.
