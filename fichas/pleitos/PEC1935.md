## Resumo
Direct investment operation (external credit), subnational municipal borrower, multilateral lender (IDB), ticket band 100-200M USD, counterpart at the 20% minimum, already included in the municipality's own annual budget law. Finances a large informal-settlement (slum) urbanization program combining integrated infrastructure upgrading (water, sanitation, drainage, street lighting, paving, sports/leisure areas, geotechnical-risk mitigation) at the settlement scale with a regional-scale component integrating settlements with the surrounding formal city along a transit corridor, plus housing-improvement works, resettlement/housing production for relocated families, land-tenure regularization, digital-inclusion public spaces, and transversal social-work/community-engagement programs. This is the fourth phase of a long-running continuity program with the same lender. Delivery is direct to the municipal government, executed by the municipal housing secretariat with a program management unit to be created by decree, and financial/budgetary interface handled by the finance secretariat. Entity type: large coastal capital municipality.

## Teoria da mudança e indicadores

- A.2 Results Framework: WEAK — the evaluating opinion itself is critical, scoring indicator coherence at little over half its weight (~0.16/0.30), describing indicators as "quite simplistic," missing timelines, lacking a future impact assessment, and in one case (the multidimensional-poverty-reduction indicator) lacking any clear calculation method.

- Verification mechanism: largely absent — the evaluating opinion explicitly states there is no concrete information on deadlines for reaching the indicator targets.
- Benchmarks used: none of the listed sector benchmarks (no IDEB/SNIS/CNJ/iRAP/ISO 37120 equivalent).

## Sustentabilidade e riscos
- A.5 Financial Sustainability: WEAK-to-adequate border — the CC itself states maintenance will be absorbed by existing municipal agencies "with no incremental expenditure foreseen," i.e. no dedicated new budget line; the evaluating opinion scores this item at roughly two-thirds of its weight (~0.19/0.30), noting that while the borrower shows "concern" about post-project upkeep (citing lessons-learned from an earlier phase of the same continuity program), there is "little concrete data."
- A.3 Risk Assessment: comprehensive matrix covering legal/authorization, procurement, expropriation, environmental, social and resettlement dimensions, scored high by the evaluating opinion (~0.89 of its weight). Resettlement is explicitly addressed (unlike a typical greenfield project) because housing production/relocation for displaced families is itself a program component; risks of community resistance, bidding-schedule/social-work mismatches, and urban violence are flagged with named mitigation measures.
- Licensing: treated briefly — the evaluating opinion only notes that the involved secretariats "appear aware" of further licenses/authorizations needed, without detailing status per component.
- Counterpart: secured — at the 20% minimum, backed by constitutionally-earmarked municipal tax revenue and already included in the municipality's annual budget bill for the relevant fiscal year, not "to be secured."
- A.4 Implementation: a program management unit is to be created by decree within the lead housing secretariat, with a separate finance secretariat handling budgetary/disbursement interface; the executor cites experience from three earlier phases of the same continuity program with this lender, but the evaluating opinion also reports a prior-portfolio performance score of only ~54/100 — a materially weaker track record than other cases with this same lender.
