## Resumo
Investment operation for a capital municipal government (direct, non-intermediated lending), financing an urban-mobility and river-margin requalification programme. Main lines: a structuring public-transport corridor network (trunk corridors, integration terminals, an intelligent-transport-system layer, intermodal integration), central-area accessibility and street-network improvements (complete-streets concept, active-mobility infrastructure, informal-commerce ordering), and urbanisation/recovery of an urban river's margins (channel desilting, a riverside road, a linear park, resettlement of at-risk households, land-tenure regularisation). Executed directly by the municipality through a dedicated programme-management unit, with operations handed to the mobility authority after construction.

## Sustentabilidade e riscos
- A.5 Financial Sustainability: ADEQUATE — O&M costs are to be embedded in the transit fare structure, with possible top-up from a municipal urban-mobility fund; additional post-project maintenance costs are quantified at roughly 1.5% of total investment; responsibility for upkeep is split across the mobility, environment and infrastructure secretariats after handover from the programme unit.
- A.3 Risk Assessment: STRONG — detailed legal/institutional risk list (municipal authorising law still pending, a decree to create the programme-management unit, heritage/urban-planning authorisations, fire-department approval for terminals) each paired with a mitigation; procurement risk addressed under general public-procurement law plus the lender's own procurement policy, with no additional staff needed except possible specialised training for the intelligent-transport-system component; no land expropriation needed (sites already municipally owned); resettlement of at-risk households is quantified (per-unit and aggregate cost) with an assisted-purchase/rental-subsidy mechanism and community-leadership buy-in noted; environmental licensing is to be obtained for all interventions, with an internal climate-governance structure already created.
- Counterpart: to be secured — financed from the municipal treasury per Brazilian law, but a municipal authorising law for the credit operation itself was still pending at CC stage and is listed among the project's own legal risks.
- A.4 Implementation: dedicated programme-management unit under the municipal government office, with an institutional responsibility matrix rated adequate; however, the proponent's historical portfolio-quality score under the credit-tracking system (SIGS) is reported as low (just over one-fifth, rated "poor" under the regime's own classification) despite having prior signed external-credit contracts, capping that specific NT sub-score.
