## Resumo
- Investment-project financing (IBRD, direct execution) by a non-dependent state-owned port-industrial development company, with a federal guarantee and state counter-guarantee.
- Finances 100% civil works: shared utility corridors (pipelines, power lines, water mains, access roads) plus port berth expansions for project cargo and for liquid-bulk export of green-hydrogen derivatives; one component/subcomponent with three works products (roughly 20/40/40% split).
- Purpose: de-risk private investment in a nascent green-hydrogen hub; investments already sit in the borrower's business plan. Counterpart at the 10% minimum from recurring company revenue; 3-year disbursement, 5-year grace, 20-year amortization.
- Executed by an in-house UGP of the borrower, with a state technical group (legal, planning, finance) as adviser.

## Teoria da mudança e indicadores
- A.1 Theory of Change: ADEQUATE (narrative chain from shared infrastructure to hub scale-up to emissions and jobs; main assumptions about private final investment decisions and end-use demand are acknowledged but not testable).
- A.2 Results Framework: ADEQUATE (6 indicators in the final version; the earlier cycle had only 3). All are outcome/impact-level (hub throughput, renewable and electrolyser capacity, avoided GHG, private co-financing leveraged, direct jobs); none measures the works outputs themselves. No verification mechanism or data source stated.
- Baselines: all 6 are zero; counted as defined because the CC describes a nascent industry with no existing hub production (new-asset logic), so baseline_defined_share = 1.0 on that reading. No baseline reference year is stated (only target years), hence 0.0 with year.
- Weaknesses: the flagship indicator is labelled hydrogen and derivatives but its formula measures only ammonia handled at the port (internal inconsistency); the renewable-capacity and electrolyser indicators overlap; the GHG indicator rests on an assumed end-use mix; targets depend on third-party investors.

## Sustentabilidade e riscos
- A.5 Financial Sustainability: ADEQUATE in the final version. OPEX stated as a share of capex (about 5%), borne by the company's own revenue, with long-term and operational maintenance plans and cash-flow projections annexed; revenue from the hub is the funding logic. Earlier cycle lacked quantified maintenance costs.
- A.3 Risk Assessment: WEAK-ADEQUATE. Qualitative treatment by category (legal, procurement, land, environmental, social, resettlement) mostly rated low/none, plus a demand risk with mitigants (pre-contracts, MoUs) and an FX-conversion option. No risk matrix; environmental licensing described by phases (preliminary licence stage for the hub area, works licensed); social section does not address nearby traditional/fishing communities.
- Counterpart: 10% from recurring company revenue, in the business plan (no LOA needed for a non-dependent state company); land already ceded, no expropriation or resettlement.
- A.4 Implementation: in-house UGP of the borrower with an organogram and RACI chart; capacity (team, experience with external credit) not detailed; borrower had no prior IBRD loan record in the CC.
