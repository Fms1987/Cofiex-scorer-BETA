## Resumo
- Modality: investment-type operation in local currency (BRL-only sublimit), IDB-funded, with the counterpart at the 20% minimum; 4-year disbursement window, 25-year total term with a 2-year grace period, variable CDI-based rate.
- Finances non-reimbursable housing subsidies that cover the down payment required by formal mortgage lenders, for low-income families (up to four minimum wages) and a higher-value tier for older persons; the vast majority of the cost sits in this component.
- Delivery is intermediated: a state housing company (executing entity) accredits developers and projects, approves subsidies, while a federal-programme financial agent underwrites the mortgage and releases funds.
- Two small supporting components (institutional strengthening of the executor; evaluation, audit and programme management); a state government is the borrower.

## Teoria da mudança e indicadores
- A.1 Theory of Change: ADEQUATE. Problem (down-payment barrier, older-person term limits, peripheral informal settlement) maps to subsidy tiers, accreditation criteria and urban/environmental quality levels; assumptions (mortgage approval, supply of eligible units) appear only as risks, not as testable assumptions.
- A.2 Results Framework: ADEQUATE. Verified 6 indicators, roughly 3 outcome-type (deficit reduction, targeting shares, leverage multiplier) and 3 output/process-type (share of projects meeting quality tiers, sports/leisure access); approximate split. Verification by administrative records and financial-agent reports, plus an official housing-deficit series for the impact indicator.
- Baselines: 5 of 6 stated (0.83), all with a reference year or period. A 0% baseline for the project-quality indicator was counted as defined because the CC explains it as a new requirement; the deficit-reduction indicator has no baseline value stated.
- Targets are percentage shares, a leverage multiplier and a deficit reduction tied to the volume of subsidies. The leisure-access target is deliberately below 100% (small projects lack space); the older-person share starts from a very low baseline, so its target looks modest.
- Inconsistency: the deficit-reduction indicator is attributed to the programme although other drivers exist (CC acknowledges this); indicator-to-objective cross-references in the CC are partly mislabelled.

## Sustentabilidade e riscos
- A.5 Financial Sustainability: ADEQUATE for a grant-type programme. No O&M burden (subsidies are one-off); fiscal sustainability argued through leverage (public subsidy mobilises many times its value in federal and private mortgage finance); no OPEX table, which is largely not applicable.
- Counterpart: provided from the state budget and said to build on resources already in the LOA, with PPA/LDO/LOA linkage in the mitigation plan; an authorizing law and a state housing fund backstop are mentioned. Status: partly secured / to be formalised.
- A.3 Risks: 3 risks with impact/probability and mitigation (mortgage rejection of beneficiaries, shortage or delay of eligible units, counterpart and fiscal-space risk); no licensing risk discussed, though project-level environmental conformity is a programme criterion. Policy-level flexibility to adjust subsidy values to budget availability is cited.
- A.4 Implementation: ADEQUATE to STRONG. Experienced executing company with a multidisciplinary team, an existing digital platform and management system, a programme running for several years, and parallel experience with an IDB project; governance through the state credit committee. No dedicated UGP described.
