## Resumo
Investment operation, direct lending to a capital municipality, executed directly by the municipal government (not intermediated). Finances an urban drainage-resilience program covering: macro- and micro-drainage works and watercourse requalification across a handful of urban watersheds; sanitation-network expansion and integrated urbanization in the same areas; involuntary resettlement and housing assistance for families in non-mitigable risk areas; technical-social support for relocation and socio-territorial development (with youth/women/ethnic-minority income-generation actions); and a risk-management/innovation component (disaster-control center, early-warning system, GIS tools, staff and community risk-management training). A smaller administration component funds the program-management unit, works supervision, and audit.

## Sustentabilidade e riscos
A.5 Financial Sustainability: improved from WEAK (scored "informação inexistente" on O&M/maintenance measures and cost projections in the first two NT passes) to ADEQUATE in the approved version, which cites a municipal law assigning stormwater-infrastructure management and its own revenue stream to a dedicated water/sanitation autarchy, plus a stated annual budget-law (LOA) allocation for its maintenance work; the NT still flags that this sustainability case covers mainly the drainage subcomponent and not the program as a whole (resettlement/housing and risk-management components are not separately costed for post-project upkeep). A.3 Risk Assessment: ADEQUATE-STRONG and stable across all three passes — a structured risk matrix covers legal/institutional acts, procurement, expropriation/resettlement (with reference to the municipality's own prior large-scale resettlement experience), and environmental/social impacts, each with named mitigation measures; licensing is addressed via a described decentralized state/municipal environmental-licensing process.
