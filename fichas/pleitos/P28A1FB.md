## Resumo
- Modality: intermediated on-lending investment loan (IBRD, sovereign-guaranteed, no counterpart) to a federal state-owned regional development bank.
- Finances a credit line for renewable-energy infrastructure (generation, transmission, distribution) plus a dedicated small-business product with simplified rules, in a remote macro-region with isolated systems and access gaps.
- Components: roughly 98% credit line (split about 80/18 between general renewable projects and small businesses) and roughly 2% institutional capacity building and training.
- Delivery model: the bank itself executes via its strategy and sustainability units and credit network; final subprojects are not yet identified. Two funding scenarios are sketched (blended with a constitutional development fund for large clients vs. 100% lender funds for small and medium clients).

## Teoria da mudança e indicadores
- A.1 Theory of Change: ADEQUATE. Diagnosis (access gaps, diesel-dependent isolated systems, funding constraints) leads coherently to a credit line and small-business product; assumptions on demand, eligibility and cost pass-through are not made explicit.
- A.2 Results Framework: WEAK. Verified 4 indicators, all approximately output/reach measures (households served, low-income municipalities reached, border municipalities reached, small-firm contracts); no outcome on energy access quality, cost reduction or renewable share.
- Baselines: all 4 have a stated baseline value, none states a reference year (baseline_defined 1.0, with-year 0.0). No zero baselines.
- Internal inconsistencies flagged by the NT: indicator 1 target below its baseline; border-municipality target apparently exceeds the plausible universe; small-firm indicator formula is a ratio of contracts but target is stated as a count. NT concluded indicators were not measurable as written.
- Verification mechanism and intermediate targets: absent from the CC (intermediate targets only mentioned as intent every few years); no benchmarks used.

## Sustentabilidade e riscos
- A.5 Financial Sustainability: ADEQUATE-to-WEAK. No O&M for the financed assets (credit line); sustainability argued through the bank's own income-statement projections and continued indicator monitoring. Lender terms: floating SOFR-based rate plus a variable spread, long term with a multi-year grace period, front-end and commitment fees; projected end-client rates are high, which may limit uptake among small borrowers.
- A.3 Risk Assessment: ADEQUATE. No formal matrix; narrative treatment of demand risk (with reallocation between sectors), currency risk (conversion options), environmental licensing at contracting and at each disbursement, and an exclusion of resettlement-causing projects. Legal-acts risk left without listed risks or mitigation.
- Counterpart: none (federal guarantee, no counterpart percentage); the bank must still provide a counter-guarantee from its own instruments.
- A.4 Implementation: ADEQUATE. Established institutional capacity and a RACI-type matrix, but no experience with external credit operations in the prior decade and no UGP-style unit; staff and consultant hiring not needed.
