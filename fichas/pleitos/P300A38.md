## Resumo
- Multi-sector territorial investment programme financed by NDB with federal guarantee, borrower a state government; direct execution by a state urban-development secretariat with a state water/irrigation company as co-executor and a dedicated programme coordination unit.
- Three thematic axes plus programme administration: (1) rural sanitation and water security (simplified water systems, household sanitation, access paving, small water reservoirs, dam safety, irrigation-scheme rehabilitation), roughly 40% of cost; (2) productive-sector infrastructure (waterfront tourism urbanisation, a state broadband network, state road rehabilitation), roughly 30%; (3) social and urban facilities (sports and early-childhood play spaces), roughly a quarter; administration and supervision a few percent.
- Counterpart at the 20% level from state resources; 5-year disbursement, 5-year grace, 15-year amortization. Works handed to municipalities for operation after completion.

## Teoria da mudança e indicadores
- A.1 Theory of Change: ADEQUATE (three axes mapped to problems and to territorial-inequality goal; links from works to outcomes asserted rather than tested; no explicit assumptions). Objectives are numerous (about 14 physical targets) for an 8-indicator framework.
- A.2 Results Framework: ADEQUATE-to-WEAK. 8 indicators, with baseline and target values; roughly 5 are outcome/coverage level (rural sanitation deficit, municipal broadband coverage, road condition, dam safety plans, small reservoirs) and about 3 are facility-coverage outputs (share of municipalities or territories receiving each facility type). Several objectives (paving, irrigation schemes, waterfront urbanisation, water systems) have no indicator of their own; no verification mechanism, data source or baseline year is given.
- Baselines: all 8 have a stated value; 3 zero baselines (new sports/play facilities) counted as defined because they are new assets. baseline_with_year_share = 0.0. Some targets move little (e.g. modest shifts in sanitation deficit and road condition shares).
- Concern: benefit localities for rural sanitation are to be chosen later by technical visits and criteria; the programme bundles unrelated sectors.

## Sustentabilidade e riscos
- A.5 Financial Sustainability: WEAK-ADEQUATE. Argued as essential, permanent state policies; O&M assigned to municipalities (water systems, waterfronts, sports and play facilities) via legal instruments whose drafts exist, to the water company for irrigation schemes, and to private partners for the broadband network (hybrid model). No O&M cost quantification or funding source is stated.
- A.3 Risk Assessment: ADEQUATE (qualitative; no matrix). Covers legislative authorisation of the loan (state assembly law needed, majority assumed), political transition, municipal land provision, FX risk (CAPAG B cited), price volatility and failed tenders, simplified environmental licensing for small works, no resettlement.
- Counterpart: 20% from state treasury, also covering consultants; stated as unrelated to recent domestic loans; legal/budget backing (LOA/PPA) not detailed, alignment with state PPA stated.
- A.4 Implementation: UCP to be created by decree, with a bidding committee and consulting support; co-executor has long experience in rural water works; many agencies involved across sectors.
