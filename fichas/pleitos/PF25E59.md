## Resumo
- Modality: conventional investment loan to the Union (federal borrower, 200-400M USD band, no counterpart because the borrower is a federal body), disbursed over 5 years with semiannual amortization and a SOFR-based rate.
- Finances a national rural/small-town sanitation programme executed centrally by a federal health foundation: simplified sewerage systems, simplified water supply systems and sustainable solid-waste systems (landfills with thermochemical energy units, selective collection), plus socio-environmental capacity building.
- Delivery: direct federal execution through a UGP, with a supervision consultancy and works contracted by public tender; municipalities and states to join through technical cooperation terms; several hundred municipalities selected by social-vulnerability and deficit criteria in two macro-regions.
- Components: integrated sanitation (about 90% of cost, of which roughly 45% sewerage, 25% water, 15% waste), programme management (about 10%), and other costs (lender fees). Second submission after a prior budget-related setback.

## Teoria da mudança e indicadores
- A.1 Theory of Change: ADEQUATE. Strong diagnosis (SNIS coverage gaps, waterborne-disease burden, costly water-truck emergency operation) and a 14-item expected-results list linking sanitation to health and poverty; no explicit assumptions, and eleven specific objectives are not matched by the results framework.
- A.2 Results Framework: WEAK. 6 indicators (verified): 2 coverage outcomes (share of municipalities served by water and by sewerage), 3 waste-treatment outputs/volumes (consortia served, two time-phased treated-volume indicators), 1 outreach share (health-environment education). Approximate split: 2 outcome, 4 output.
- Baselines: values stated for 4 of 6 (share 0.67); the consortia indicator and the first treated-volume indicator carry a baseline of zero that the CC does not explain, so counted as undefined. No reference year or period for any baseline (year share 0.0). The later treated-volume indicator uses the earlier one's target as its baseline.
- Verification: formulas and units given, no data source, frequency or responsible unit; the formula of the education indicator repeats its denominator. The coverage indicators use a municipality-share denominator that does not match the selected subset of municipalities (reading uncertain).
- Gaps: no indicator for household connections or health outcomes although these dominate cost and the stated purpose; the NT also found only about half the indicators reflect the objectives and some hard to reach within the programme period.

## Sustentabilidade e riscos
- A.5 Financial Sustainability: WEAK. Two-tier model: urban systems left to municipalities or autonomous utilities/concessionaires charging users; rural systems run by community associations with cost sharing among users; waste services usually carried by municipal budgets (only a minority levy a fee). No O&M cost estimate; the CC itself flags tariff collection from a vulnerable population as a risk, mitigated only by awareness work.
- Counterpart: none (federal borrower). Budget adequacy is the central issue: the CC says the first-year disbursement is a fraction of the loan and the ministry would ask for a LOA adjustment; it also records that the previous version was not approved for budget inadequacy (spending cap, golden rule, constitutional health floor).
- A.3 Risk Assessment: WEAK. No risk matrix; contracting risk covered in two lines; expropriation and resettlement "not foreseen" pending studies; environmental and social risk self-rated low or near null; licensing to be handled by cooperation terms with states and municipalities, with licences issued locally as works are defined.
- A.4 Implementation: UGP to be created by decree inside the executing foundation, with four management units; centralised execution with a supervision consultancy. Basic designs exist for a few hundred interventions but need review and executive designs; procurement packaging (by state or by block) and cooperation-term content undefined; management cost above typical.
