## Resumo
- Modality: investment programme in BRL from IDB to a state government, executed directly by the state health secretariat through a project management unit (UGP), with no transfers to municipalities; counterpart at 20% of cost, 60-month execution.
- Finances a state-wide strengthening of the public health system: works and equipment for regional hospitals, maternal-child, emergency and outpatient/diagnostic facilities; digital health, regulation and management modernisation; clinical care lines; reference centres for older people, people with intellectual disability and mental-health needs.
- Four components (access/services network, management and innovation, care models, programme administration with impact evaluation and supervision); the largest component by far is the physical network.

## Teoria da mudança e indicadores
- A.1 Theory of Change: ADEQUATE. Five problem areas (care gaps, inadequate hospital infrastructure, vulnerable groups, fragmented management, missing care lines) map to five specific objectives, each cross-referenced to components, PPA objectives and indicators; no explicit testable assumptions.
- A.2 Results Framework: ADEQUATE. 9 indicators verified; approximately 6 outcome-type (elective surgery volume, waiting time, neonatal mortality, maternal mortality, child malnutrition, in-hospital mortality) and 3 output/coverage (reference-centre attendances, electronic-record coverage, telemedicine consultations). Each has formula, SMART narrative, data sources (national health information systems, regulation system, hospital census) and a link to objective and component.
- Baselines: all 9 stated with a reference year (2023, 2024 or 2025), so both shares are 1.0; the one zero baseline (reference centres) is explained as nonexistent today and counted as defined.
- Internal inconsistencies: elective-surgery baseline and targets appear in several incompatible versions across description and target fields; maternal-mortality baseline and target differ between description and target; malnutrition target contradicts itself (state plan target above the baseline versus an eradication goal); waiting-time unit stated as minutes and seconds although measured in days; the mortality-in-beds target is very ambitious (fivefold fall). Several targets are aspirational relative to the lever.
- Benchmarks: national health information systems and PPA key indicators; no international comparators. No wide-range indicators.

## Sustentabilidade e riscos
- A.5 Financial Sustainability: ADEQUATE. Recurrent costs of new facilities are to be met from the state health budget, with a stated allocation of about 10% of the secretariat's annual budget to O&M, supplemented by federal transfers and habilitation of new facilities in federal financing schemes; a phased costing plan is promised but no per-facility OPEX table.
- A.3 Risk Assessment: ADEQUATE. Three risks with impact/probability, causes, effects and responsible units: recurrent funding of new units, delays in bidding and works, change of state administration (mitigated by institutionalising the programme in the secretariat). No environmental licensing risk surfaced for the works (NT silent).
- Counterpart: 20%, source in the state budget; LOA/PPA inclusion planned but not evidenced as secured.
- A.4 Implementation: ADEQUATE. Direct execution by the health secretariat with a UGP (technical team described as a few dozen professionals), prior experience with multilateral operations claimed, supervision firm contracted, RACI matrix annexed.
