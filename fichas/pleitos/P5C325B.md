## Resumo
- Investment project financed by IBRD for a state road agency; counterpart at the 20% minimum; executed directly by the road agency through a UGP, with the finance and planning secretariats in support.
- Finances proactive, safe and climate-resilient road asset management: long-term performance-based rehabilitation-and-maintenance contracts on the paved state network, with a PPP-preparation track.
- Four components: (1) proactive maintenance (roughly 75% of cost), (2) institutional strengthening and knowledge agenda, (3) road works and services (urban crossings, rural access, critical-point treatment, roughly 20%), (4) management support.
- Cross-cutting add-ons: road safety, climate-response plans, water-security drainage solutions, a small women/minorities job-training pilot, and creation of a maintenance investment fund.

## Teoria da mudança e indicadores
- A.1 Theory of Change: ADEQUATE. Problem tree (reactive maintenance, degradation, crash rates, climate vulnerability, inequality) maps to lettered specific objectives and each indicator cites its objective; assumptions are not explicit, and the objective list is very broad (a dozen objectives).
- A.2 Results Framework: ADEQUATE. 13 indicators (verified count); approximately 4 outcome-type (network share under long-term contracts, share of pavement in good condition, fatality reduction, communities with year-round access) and approximately 9 output-type (crossings, plans, studies, jobs, trainings, treated points, fund created).
- Verification present for every indicator (responsible body, data source, calculation, periodicity, objective link); IRI is used as the pavement benchmark.
- Baselines: all 13 state a value, but almost all are "0" or "No". Counted as defined (10 of 13): zeros of new contract model, new fund, new plans/studies, trainings, jobs and treated points (incremental outputs). Not counted: fatality reduction (0% expressed as a ratio), pavement-condition share and communities with access (before/after measures with unexplained 0). A reference year appears only for the pavement-condition indicator.
- Inconsistencies: the same 36% target on two different indicators (contract coverage and good-condition share); a percentage-type indicator with a bare numeric target; fatality target is a relative reduction with no absolute baseline; several targets in km or counts (not copied here).

## Sustentabilidade e riscos
- A.5 Financial Sustainability: ADEQUATE. Argument rests on lifecycle-cost savings of performance-based contracts (restoration cost roughly halved in a first cycle per the proponent's cited studies), per-km cost comparison table, and a planned infrastructure/maintenance fund plus institutional capacity for later PPP-type contracts. No total annual O&M budget line or legal backing for the fund yet; the fund is itself an indicator (to be created).
- A.3 Risk Assessment: ADEQUATE. 9 risks with impact/probability and mitigation (legal acts, payment, litigation over new contract types, bidder capacity, failed tender, expropriation, land occupation, environmental, social). Rehabilitation works are described as low-impact and licensing-exempt; World Bank ESF instruments (resettlement standard, gender action plan, stakeholder engagement) to be prepared.
- Counterpart: to be secured; only the loan-authorising law is needed, and the proponent cites a legislative majority and a top CAPAG rating; the state commits to finance contract tails beyond the disbursement period.
- A.4 Implementation: ADEQUATE-to-STRONG. UGP inside the road agency with procurement, financial, environmental, social and engineering staff; finance secretariat with a long track record with the lender; lender training planned; a PPP-structuring agency as PMO.
