## Resumo
Investment loan (direct external credit operation, two co-financing external lenders) funding the second edition of a state-level sustainable rural-development program targeting family farming, replicating and scaling up a first edition. Delivery is intermediated/direct hybrid: a state secretariat for family agriculture/semi-arid development is the lead executing agency, supported by several sub-executing state secretariats and a specialized rural-extension agency, coordinated through a project management unit built on the structure used in the first edition. Main components: water-access/sanitation and energy infrastructure and technologies for resilience, resilient productive systems and market diversification for poverty reduction, institutional strengthening/capacity building, and project management/M&E. Entity type: state government (subnational).

## Teoria da mudança e indicadores
A.1 Theory of Change: STRONG — NT scores problem-solution-expected-results coherence near full marks (0.658/0.700), crediting an adequate socioeconomic diagnosis and a causal narrative explicitly built on solutions tested in the program's first edition.
A.2 Results Framework: ADEQUATE — 12 verified indicators across three tiers (product, result/outcome, impact), described by the NT as "bastante diversificado" and satisfactory on clarity, measurability and relevance (coherence sub-item scored 0.240/0.300), enabling an approximate split of roughly a third output-type (family counts reached) to two-thirds outcome/impact-type (adoption rates, income/poverty-line transitions, food-security improvement). However, every one of the 12 indicators starts from a baseline value of zero with no stated explanation (new-asset/counterfactual framing is not spelled out in the text) — so baseline_defined_share=0.0 and baseline_with_year_share=0.0 (no reference year is given either). NT additionally flags the absence of an interim-milestone/indicator-evolution schedule, to be mitigated by a planned M&E subcomponent.
No internal inconsistencies noted beyond the above; verification mechanism is named (a dedicated M&A/impact-evaluation system, drawing on a prior short-form food-insecurity scale for one indicator).

## Sustentabilidade e riscos
A.5 Financial Sustainability: WEAK/ABSENT — NT scores this sub-item only 0.106/0.300, stating no structured post-project financial-sustainability strategy was presented; it credits only partially the proponent's argument that continued access to productive assets, food/water security and strengthened farmer capacity will itself sustain results, since no maintenance-cost projection was provided (header mentions_opex_sustain=0 is broadly consistent here, though the topic is in fact discussed and explicitly marked as insufficient — flagged as a borderline case).
A.3 Risk Assessment: a legal/procurement/environmental/social risk matrix is present but the NT repeatedly notes it lacks precision — legal-act risks are mapped but not assessed for likelihood/impact; procurement risk categories are listed without a risk analysis; environmental risk (climate hazards and unsustainable farming practices) is adequately covered; social-impact risk analysis is thin, limited to describing two indigenous ethnic groups without identifying concrete risks or mitigations for traditional communities. Expropriation and resettlement are both marked "not applicable" by the proponent, accepted as adequate given the project's nature (no physical works requiring land acquisition).
Counterpart: secured — contrapartida at roughly a quarter of total cost, sourced from earmarked state tax revenues, included in the state's multi-year investment plan under a named sectoral program/objective.
A.4 Implementation: ADEQUATE — project management unit built on the structure and experienced staff of the program's first edition, with sub-executing secretariats and a specialized rural-extension agency; the NT notes the executing agency's external-financier track record is rated at a modest quality level, and flags that the disbursement timeframe is demanding given project complexity (roughly double the prior edition), partly mitigated by lessons learned and a long grace period that would make a schedule extension feasible if needed.
