## Resumo
Investment operation for a capital municipality, direct delivery through the municipal government via a dedicated project-management unit under the planning secretariat. Finances a territorially targeted social-development program: integrated multi-service community facilities (health, culture, recreation, training, innovation) in lower-HDI districts, inclusive/sustainable infrastructure works (risk-area interventions, land-tenure regularization, green-energy retrofits), and a management/innovation component (investment-project bank, risk studies, project-management unit). Entity type: municipal government (direct borrower with sovereign guarantee).

A.2 Results Framework: WEAK-ADEQUATE — verified count of 14 indicators, an even mix of new-asset/activity counters (facilities built, photovoltaic systems installed, trainings held, plans adopted) and outcome-style percentage-change indicators (youth participation, land-tenure regularization, risk-area population, drainage complaints); outcome-vs-output split roughly even (approximate). Baselines: only about 36% of indicators carry a non-zero, strictly "defined" baseline value under the rule applied here — the remaining indicators state a zero baseline that is plausibly explained by their being new-asset/new-activity counters (nothing built/run yet) but the CC text reviewed does not explicitly state that counterfactual reasoning, so those zeros are counted as undefined rather than assumed. Where a baseline value is stated, it also carries an explicit reference year (2023, with a 2028 target year), so the year-share equals the defined share (about 36%) rather than being lower. No explicit indicator-verification mechanism is described; the NT specifically calls out one user-satisfaction indicator as lacking a defined measurement scale. No wide-range indicators found.

## Sustentabilidade e riscos
A.5 Financial Sustainability: OPEX not quantified — the proponent describes institutionalizing the program via multi-level government budgets and civil-society partnerships, but the NT explicitly notes there is no cost provision for maintenance after execution ends. A.3 Risk Assessment: risks are addressed by category rather than a consolidated matrix — legal risk (municipal legislative authorization still pending, to be sought after a favorable committee decision) with a coordination-based mitigation; procurement risk (bidding delays) with a legal-support mitigation; environmental risk is described but the NT notes no corresponding mitigating actions were identified for it; social-acceptance risk among stakeholders is named without a concrete mitigation; resettlement risk is detailed with several mitigation instruments (prior impact analysis, housing/rental-subsidy instruments, land regularization) tied to a road-system expansion component. Counterpart (20% of cost) is to be secured partly through land/property contributions valued as an in-kind product, which the proponent states is compliant with the regulatory framework; no explicit annual-budget-law (LOA) citation found in the reviewed text, so counterpart should be read as "to be secured" via budget programming rather than legislatively confirmed at CC stage. A.4 Implementation: a responsibility matrix was presented, but the NT notes it lacks a detailed technical-team description, relying instead on an organizational chart; the executing agency cites prior experience with the same lender on an unrelated program as a positive signal.
