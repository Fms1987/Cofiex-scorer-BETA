## Resumo
Investment loan to a state government (direct execution), financing requalification of tourism-service facilities on a beach frontage, decentralized sanitation collection/treatment for those facilities, and a professional-training component targeting women and youth. Delivery is direct (state UGP under a state public-works secretariat, supported by an external management consultancy), not intermediated. Main components: studies/projects, civil works (facility requalification + sanitation), program management/supervision/audit, and financing costs. Entity type: state government (subnational).

## Teoria da mudança e indicadores
- A.1 Theory of Change: WEAK. A causal chain links facility upgrade + sanitation + training to tourism, health and socioeconomic outcomes, but both NTs found the geographic concentration (a single beach frontage) poorly justified against the program's state-wide "sustainable municipalities" framing, and flagged a scale/beneficiary-definition inconsistency (direct beneficiaries framed as the whole municipality's population although the intervention is concentrated on one beach).
- A.2 Results Framework: WEAK. VERIFIED indicator count = 6. Outcome-vs-output split approximate: ~2 outcome-type (visitor count, fecal-coliform contamination index) vs ~4 output/physical-target indicators (facilities served, energy generated, training-quota shares for women/youth) — the NT itself states that four of the six proposed indicators are physical targets rather than true indicators.
- baseline_defined_share = 1.0: all 6 indicators state a baseline value; the 4 zero baselines (facilities served, energy generated, women's/youth training-quota shares) are counted as defined because the CC's diagnostic section explicitly frames the underlying service/program as previously absent (new-asset/counterfactual case).
- baseline_with_year_share = 1.0: all baselines are dated to the same reference year, with distinct target years per indicator.
- Verification mechanism: NT silent on an indicator-level verification/M&E methodology; only a generic program-monitoring consultancy function is referenced in the CC.
- Benchmarks used: none (no sector benchmark index cited for any indicator).
- Wide-range or deferred indicators: none identified.
- Internal inconsistencies: both NTs flag a unit-area mismatch for the main facility-upgrade product between the CC narrative and an annex, and a budgeted structural product (towers) present in the cost annex but omitted from the CC's narrative description.

## Sustentabilidade e riscos
- A.5 Financial Sustainability: WEAK. OPEX for the sanitation system is quantified (a band-range annual figure) and funded from the state budget; the facility/kiosk portion is expected to be self-funded by the authorized commercial operators. However, the legal instruments cited (adhesion/commitment/maintenance-declaration terms) cover only the implementation phase, not post-completion O&M — both NTs score this sub-criterion well below half marks and conclude long-term financial sustainability could not be confirmed.
- A.3 Risk Assessment: ADEQUATE. A matrix covers authorizing-legislation risk, procurement, environmental licensing (including a renewal risk on the installation license) and resettlement/compensation mitigations (relocation/compensation plan, social-work project). Gap flagged by NT: no assessment of post-upgrade demand risk for the commercial facilities.
- Counterpart: to be secured. Sourced from constitutionally-assigned state tax revenue; to be appropriated in the annual budget law only after a COFIEX recommendation — not yet in the LOA at CC stage.
- A.4 Implementation (UGP quality): ADEQUATE/STRONG. UGP created by state decree, under a state public-works secretariat, multidisciplinary, supported by an external management consultancy; an institutional-responsibility (RACI) matrix spans several state bodies and one municipal body, scored well by NT.
