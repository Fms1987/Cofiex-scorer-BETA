## Resumo
- Modality: investment loan from IDB to a state government, counterpart at the 20% minimum; disbursement over about 5 years.
- Finances a poverty-reduction programme built on intensive, personalised family follow-up (home visits, a family development plan, three sequential modules over up to 24 months) in dozens of pilot municipalities, with two beneficiary tracks (social protection; poverty exit with productive inclusion), plus links to existing state services (food security, education, health, vocational training, microcredit).
- Four components: family follow-up and development (roughly 90% of cost, field agents hired through civil-society organisations), technical assistance for a case-management information system, institutional strengthening/governance and training, and programme management with impact evaluation and audit.
- Delivery: state social-development secretariat as sole main executor with a large partner network of sector secretariats and municipalities; no transfers between secretariats.

## Teoria da mudança e indicadores
- A.1 Theory of Change: ADEQUATE. Problem, solution, ten expected results and objective-component-indicator mapping are explicit, and the track/module logic is a clear causal chain; assumptions (municipal uptake, take-up by families, availability of the existing state services) are discussed as risks rather than testable assumptions.
- A.2 Results Framework: ADEQUATE. 10 indicators (verified), approximately 6 outcome-type and 4 output/process-type (follow-up coverage, system use, plan completion, course completion, satisfaction); every indicator has a SMART justification, formula and PPA link.
- Baselines: 9 of 10 counted as defined. Counted zero baselines (new programme, explained): 5 indicators (follow-up coverage, plans drawn up, module completion, course completion, system-registration rate). Non-zero baselines: food insecurity (2023), early-childhood enrolment (2025), income share (2025) and social-security coverage (2019), mostly state-level proxies because municipal data are not disaggregated. The satisfaction indicator states a 0% baseline whose justification was copied from the system-use indicator, so it is counted as undefined. Baseline_with_year share is 0.9.
- Weaknesses: formula for the food-insecurity indicator does not match its definition or the state-level baseline; the early-childhood indicator is titled as a count but defined as a percentage, with 0-5 vs 0-6 age ranges; the social-security target mixes a 20% rise with a 1.5% expected change; income indicator only starts in the later years and uses a very small target step.
- Verification: EBIA, national household survey, national social registry and system records named; evaluation design funded in the management component. Benchmarks: national surveys; no LAC comparator.

## Sustentabilidade e riscos
- A.5 Financial Sustainability: ADEQUATE/PARTIAL. Recurring costs (field teams, system, municipal support) to be absorbed in state annual budgets from the year after approval, with a promised law/decree/resolution institutionalising the programme and a transition clause with the executing entity; no OPEX table or projected post-loan cost; the partner services already run on their own budgets.
- A.3 Risk Assessment: ADEQUATE. 5 risks with impact/probability and mitigation (municipal uptake, recruitment/turnover of field agents, system rollout, post-loan financing, family drop-out); family cash incentives are noted as outside the loan scope. No licensing issue (no works).
- Counterpart: budget guidelines/LOA 2026 forecast attached for recurring costs; counterpart at the minimum share, secured by annex rather than by dedicated law at CC stage.
- A.4 Implementation: ADEQUATE. Experienced secretariat with regional offices; programme-specific team, three-level management nuclei with monthly meetings, and an inter-secretariat steering committee chaired by the governor's office; RACI matrix annexed.
