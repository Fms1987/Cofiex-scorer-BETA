## Resumo
Investment loan (direct external credit operation) financing the second stage of a multi-year urban-environmental/linear-park program along a flood-prone watercourse that cuts through a capital municipality. Delivery is direct: the municipal government executes through a dedicated program coordination unit under the urban-planning/housing secretariat, with multiple co-executing municipal bodies (environment agency, infrastructure, sports, mobility, finance, administration, communication, innovation/technology). Main components: program management, land/resettlement release, environment-mobility-infrastructure works (green infrastructure, drainage, linear park, roads), and social/environmental sustainability actions (flood monitoring/alert tech, communication and environmental education). Entity type: capital municipality (subnational), continuing a first stage financed by the same lender.

## Teoria da mudança e indicadores
A.1 Theory of Change: STRONG — NT scored problem/solution/expected-results coherence and objective-component alignment near full marks, describing a clear logical chain from basin-wide flooding/occupation problems (continuing a first program stage) to targeted works and relocation, to reduced flood risk and improved quality of life.
A.2 Results Framework: WEAK — 9 indicators, but the NT explicitly criticizes indicator design (coherence sub-item scored only 0.120–0.240/0.300 depending on the cycle): formulas are reduced to "baseline plus units to be delivered," i.e. cumulative output counts rather than measures of effectiveness; the NT states they fail to reflect "resultados de efetividade." baseline_defined_share=0.33 (3 of 9 indicators carry a non-zero, stated baseline — reabilitated-canal length, paved-road length, and completed bridge/art works; the other 6 start at an unexplained zero baseline and are treated as undefined). No indicator states a baseline reference year/period (baseline_with_year_share=0.0). Indicators are overwhelmingly output-type (physical units delivered: families relocated, flood-prone spots eliminated, canal km, park hectares, gallery km, road km, environmental-education actions, monitoring-system rollout %).

## Sustentabilidade e riscos
A.5 Financial Sustainability: ADEQUATE/STRONG — the proponent names the specific agencies that will sign maintenance commitment-terms for each asset type (green areas, road/lighting/drainage infrastructure, sports equipment, public-space monitoring, street cleaning, flood-alert system) and estimates an annual O&M cost band, to be funded from the municipal budget, possible PPP arrangements, or a betterment-contribution charge; the NT scores this sub-item at or near the ceiling.
A.3 Risk Assessment: matrix present covering legal acts, procurement, land acquisition/resettlement, environmental and social impacts. Environmental licensing is PARTIAL/in progress: renewal of the prior environmental license was pending with the municipal environment agency at CC stage, with the installation license to follow after detailed-design adjustments. Involuntary resettlement is material to this program (several hundred households/parcels, mix of public and private land), mitigated via the lender's resettlement-safeguard policy, negotiated compensation instruments (replacement unit, indemnity, relocation incentive) and a dedicated resettlement/relocation plan; the NT credits this experience to a successful first program stage.
Counterpart: secured via earmarked municipal tax revenues and included in the multi-year investment plan and municipal master plan; land-acquisition costs are to be covered by the municipality while indemnities are covered by the lender.
A.4 Implementation: ADEQUATE — a program coordination unit under the urban-planning/housing secretariat, staffed with multidisciplinary career and appointed personnel experienced from the first program stage, with a responsibility matrix across co-executing secretariats; the NT notes the executing agency's familiarity with the lender's procurement/safeguard policies from that prior stage, though the comparison of financing terms against alternative external creditors was limited to a single additional institution.
