## Resumo
- Modality: sovereign-backed guarantee (IBRD contingent-credit payment guarantee) under the dedicated PPP sublimit; no disbursement, grace or amortisation, instrument term of 20 years, annual guarantee fee of about 1.4% plus one-off fees; it converts into a loan with federal guarantee only if the guarantee is called.
- Purpose: back the recurring public counter-payments and contract-risk compensation of a state-level metropolitan passenger-rail PPP concession (modernisation of existing lines, new stations, signalling and rolling stock, line extensions), replacing the guarantee capacity the state had exhausted; sized at roughly six months of annual counter-payments, a small share of project CAPEX.
- Structure: single component and single product (the guarantee); the physical programme is delivered and financed by a future concessionaire, with the state contributing about half of CAPEX outside this operation. Entity type: subnational government, with a state PPP guarantor company as custodian/executor.

## Teoria da mudança e indicadores
- A.1 Theory of Change: ADEQUATE. Implicit chain (AAA-rated guarantee lowers perceived payment risk, then a more competitive tender and cheaper private capital, then PPP investment, then capacity/reliability gains) is coherent but assumptions are not stated or testable; most benefits are quantified for the whole PPP programme rather than for the guarantee itself.
- A.2 Results Framework: ADEQUATE. 7 indicators verified; approximately 5 outcome/service-level (ridership on the extended line, line capacity, peak headway, service uptime, rolling-stock reliability) and 2 output counts (new stations, accessible stations).
- Baselines: all 7 have a stated value (baseline_defined_share 1.0); one zero baseline (new stations) counted as defined because it is a count of assets that do not yet exist. No baseline or target carries a reference year or date (baseline_with_year_share 0.0); the NT scored the time-bound sub-criterion at zero for this reason.
- Verification: formulas are given and tied to operator records and concession service-level measures, but no independent verification or evaluation arrangement; no wide-range or deferred indicators.
- Gaps: no indicator captures the guarantee's own effect (bidder competition, financing cost, private capital mobilised); the NT also noted one indicator merely repeats a product. Internal inconsistency: counterpart stated as none while a state contribution of about half of CAPEX is described elsewhere.

## Sustentabilidade e riscos
- A.5 Financial Sustainability: ADEQUATE. OPEX is quantified at programme level (current public operating cost versus projected concession cost, cost per passenger falling roughly a quarter) and O&M passes to the concessionaire; funding of counter-payments rests on the state budget under the PPP law; no year-by-year OPEX table. Guarantee administrative costs are borne by the state guarantor company.
- Counterpart: none formally required for a guarantee; the state contribution (about half of CAPEX) and the authorizing law are still "to be secured" (law in internal processing, not yet sent to the legislature); project is in the state PPA and sector transport plan.
- A.3 Risk Assessment: STRONG on environmental/social (IFC Performance Standards screening, several higher-significance risks incl. involuntary resettlement of thousands of families, cultural heritage, community safety, GBV); existing operating licences valid for the current lines, licensing of new works left to the concessionaire. Expropriation cost-sharing bands between grantor and concessionaire are described; NT noted mitigation measures not clearly stated. No formal risk matrix table in the CC (contract risk matrix referenced).
- A.4 Implementation: ADEQUATE. Responsibility matrix provided; monitoring commission with specialised staff (a few dozen) already oversees other concessions; project still in structuring (tender documents pending), borrower portfolio performance rated "good" by the NT.
