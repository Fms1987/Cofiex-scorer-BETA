## Resumo
- Modality: IDB investment loan to a state government (federal guarantee) to create and capitalise a subnational strategic investment fund; counterpart at one third of cost (roughly a third of total); long tenor (about 24 years) with a six-year grace period.
- Dual purpose: (i) a countercyclical liquidity buffer for fiscal resilience; (ii) concessional credit, equity, guarantees and co-investment that leverage private investment in strategic sectors (logistics concessions and PPPs, low-carbon agribusiness, innovation, clean energy, advanced industry).
- Delivery: intermediated; a financial institution to be selected will operate the fund, with demand-driven private borrowers whose projects are not yet identified; the state finance department executes the loan and sets up the legal framework and governance.
- Two components: capital injection into the fund (nearly all of cost) and fund governance/management instruments (small technical-assistance slice).

## Teoria da mudança e indicadores
- A.1 Theory of Change: ADEQUATE. Clear logic from tax-reform-driven loss of fiscal incentives, to a fund that crowds in private capital and acts as buffer, to seven specific objectives each mapped to PPA objectives and products; supported by literature on development-bank credit effects, but assumptions on leverage, pipeline and legal approval are not tested.
- A.2 Results Framework: ADEQUATE. 14 indicators verified; roughly 11 are macro or sector outcomes/impacts (per-capita growth, capital formation, value added of industry, agriculture and services, small-firm share of exporters, clean-energy share, road service level, CAPAG liquidity, disaster response time) and about 3 fund-level outputs (jobs created, private capital leveraged, ESG-compliant share of projects). Each has formula, SMART narrative, source and alignment; verification is by official statistics plus beneficiary reporting and an ex post evaluation with the lender.
- Baselines: 13 of 14 defined; zero baselines counted as defined for new-fund counters (jobs, leverage, ESG share, where the CC explains the fund starts from nothing); the incubated-firms indicator has a zero baseline stated for a percentage-change formula without explanation, counted as undefined. Reference years given for all stated baselines (0.93 for both shares).
- Deferred indicators: the CC says project-level baselines and targets will be supplied by each applicant at the time of financing; so many sector indicators are only monitored when projects exist.
- Inconsistencies: ESG share target is 80% in the SMART text but 60% in the target field; clean-energy indicator label (share of supply) differs from the formula (share of licensed capacity); several macro targets are small moves likely driven by non-fund factors (attribution weak); the CAPAG indicator only maintains the top tier; some SMART fields blank; the unit for one fixed-capital target is in BRL.
- Benchmarks: none international; PPA state and federal targets used.

## Sustentabilidade e riscos
- A.5 Financial Sustainability: ADEQUATE. CC states no extra recurrent cost to the state: O&M of financed projects falls on private beneficiaries, fund administration costs come from fixed fees defined in the fund's legal framework, and co-investment rather than public credit lowers default exposure. No quantified OPEX, and the fund law is not yet enacted.
- A.3 Risk Assessment: ADEQUATE. Seven risks with impact/probability, causes, scenarios and mitigation: governance, low socioeconomic return (rated high probability), non-approval of the enabling law, portfolio, executing-agency capacity (high impact, high probability), regulatory, environmental and social (licensing failure, protected areas). Safeguards via ESIA and ESG criteria.
- Counterpart: one third of cost, phased in the first two years; budget inclusion not evidenced, to be secured. Counter-guarantees given as constitutional revenue shares. No lender no-objection file attached.
- A.4 Implementation: WEAK-to-ADEQUATE. Finance department executes with a UGP and a ROP to be written, partners from planning and audit bodies; the CC itself admits technical and staffing limits of the executor (high-probability risk) and relies on consultants and the future operator.
