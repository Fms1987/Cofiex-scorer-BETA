## Resumo
- Investment programme for a mid-sized municipality, CAF-funded, with counterpart at the 20% minimum; direct execution by the municipality through a dedicated UGP attached to the planning secretariat, with many line secretariats as co-executors.
- Integrated urban package: sanitation/drainage and street requalification, social facilities (health, women's service centre, elderly centre, bus terminal, industrial park, early-childhood), and an environmental component (stream and riparian recovery, native-seedling nursery, fire/deforestation monitoring room, solid-waste plan); plus programme management and a separate "other expenses" component (five components in version used).
- Heavily multi-sector, with nine problem blocks and nine specific objectives mapped to three large finalistic components; the NT of the first attempt criticised exactly this aggregation.

## Teoria da mudança e indicadores
- A.1 Theory of Change: ADEQUATE. Problem-cause-consequence-solution-result chain written for each of nine problems, but no explicit testable assumptions and the many solutions are loosely tied to the three finalistic components.
- A.2 Results Framework: ADEQUATE-to-WEAK. 14 indicators verified (version 5); approximately 6 outcome-type (flooding hot-spots, hospital/elderly/women service volumes, waste-degraded areas, jobs) vs approximately 8 output-type (area restored, km of works, terminal flow, seats created, tonnes processed, trainees, seedlings, women's-centre attendances). Only the first 8 carry a SMART block; indicators 9-14 give formula and target but no SMART/verification text.
- Baselines: all 14 have a baseline value (share 1.0). Zero baselines counted because the text ties them to a new asset or pre-works situation (riparian area, street works, terminal, women's centre, cassava plant, trainees, seedlings, industrial-park jobs); service-volume indicators use "100% of current capacity" as baseline. Three baselines carry no reference year (terminal, seats, trainees), hence year share 0.79.
- Internal inconsistencies: indicator 1 is named as sewer collection coverage but its description, formula and target measure flood hot-spots; indicators 6 and 11 are two separate women's-centre indicators with different targets; component cost figures in one component description do not match its header; the first-attempt NT flagged conflicting target timelines in indicators 02, 03, 06, 07.
- Benchmarks: none cited (no external standard). Verification: present for the first 8 (GIS, works reports, ticket counters, enrolment, management system) and for jobs (RAIS/CAGED); absent for the other service-volume indicators.

## Sustentabilidade e riscos
- A.5 Financial Sustainability: ADEQUATE. O&M strategy with dedicated maintenance funds fed by percentages of tariff, tax and transfer revenue, an itemised annual O&M table by asset category with funding source per line, tariff revision, light-PPP/concession of terminal and industrial park, and a promise to enter the lines in PPA/LOA. Funds are proposed rather than legally created; some funding-source attributions look loose.
- A.3 Risk Assessment: ADEQUATE. Six risks with impact/probability and mitigation: regulatory (authorising law/LDO), tendering, environmental licensing, expropriation/resettlement, social, financial/FX (partial swap, contingency reserve). No per-component licensing status.
- Counterpart: at the 20% minimum, to be provided by the municipality; no explicit LOA/PPA line or authorising law shown ("to be secured"). Counter-guarantee via constitutional revenue shares. Bank no-objection field empty in the version used.
- A.4 Implementation: ADEQUATE. UGP with interdisciplinary team, PMBOK/EVM training, prior programmes cited, consultancy support; execution split across many secretariats via internal agreements, which raises coordination load. The municipal PPA was cited but its pages were not attached in the first attempt (per NT).
