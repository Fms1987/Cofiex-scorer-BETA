## Resumo
Investment-modality external credit operation for a state government, delivered directly through the state road agency, financed by a multilateral bank (IBRD) with contrapartida at the 20% minimum band. Finances performance-based recovery and long-term maintenance of paved state roads via design-build-maintain contracts, construction of urban bypasses, a road-safety rating methodology, and counterpart resources toward a pilot sub-national PPP/concession performance-payment stream. A secondary component funds PPP/concession structuring studies and program-management support (header `sector` of fund_intermediation appears to be a keyword-match artifact; the CC and NT describe a direct road-infrastructure/maintenance program, not a financial-intermediation fund, so `sector_verified` is set to infrastructure_mobility).

## Teoria da mudança e indicadores
- A.1 Theory of Change: ADEQUATE — the CC lays out five diagnosed problems each paired with a corresponding intervention and expected result; the NT judged the diagnosis-solution-result chain coherent, but assumptions are not tested explicitly (no stated counterfactual or risk-to-assumption linkage).
- A.2 Results Framework: WEAK — VERIFIED indicator count is 6; only 1 of 6 (17%) states a baseline value, and none state a baseline reference year (`baseline_with_year_share` = 0). The NT explicitly penalized this gap, noting the proponent's own long-standing experience with this maintenance model made the absence of defined baselines/targets unjustified.
- Outcome-vs-output split (approximate): all 6 indicators are outcome-type (pavement-condition share, a safety star-rating methodology score, accident-reduction rates, travel-time reduction, a roughness-index improvement share) — none are simple output/count indicators.
- Verification mechanism: present for the one defined indicator (an existing agency physical-assessment method) and for the safety-rating indicator (an international road-safety benchmark methodology); the four accident/travel-time indicators cite a named data source but defer baseline/target definition to post-design studies.
- Benchmarks used: an international road-safety star-rating methodology (iRAP-type).
- Deferred indicators: 5 of 6 indicators have "to be defined after project design conclusion" baselines and/or targets — unexplained deferrals, not justified as new-asset/counterfactual zeros, so counted as undefined per the baseline rule.
- Internal inconsistency: two of the deferred indicators (accident reduction at recovered segments; accident reduction at bypass locations) list a target of 0.00, which is likely a placeholder rather than a substantive zero target, but the NT does not flag this explicitly — UNCERTAIN.

## Sustentabilidade e riscos
- A.5 Financial Sustainability: ADEQUATE — O&M is planned to continue through the same long-term performance-based maintenance contracts funded by the state treasury budget line; the NT flagged a gap, noting the proponent does not state whether post-contract maintenance costs stay the same or require new procurement rounds.
- Counterpart: to be secured — sourced from state treasury revenue under the standing counterpart resolution and listed in the current multi-year investment plan (PPA), but the CC itself notes that legislative authorization for the executive branch to contract the financing is still pending, and the next PPA cycle covering the full execution period has not yet been enacted.
- A.3 Risk Assessment: ADEQUATE/STRONG — a structured risk section covers legal acts, procurement, land-acquisition, environmental and social impacts, with no resettlement expected (works described as being in rural, unoccupied areas for the new segments); licensing status is addressed (low-impact maintenance works qualify for a licensing exemption under the state environmental agency's own norm; new works' environmental studies are deferred to the engineering-design phase).
- A.4 Implementation (UGP quality): ADEQUATE — execution sits with an established state road agency with a dedicated program-management unit under an existing financing-coordination structure and over a decade of experience executing externally financed road programs (cited track record with another multilateral lender); the NT separately scored the agency's experience specifically with this lender's own procurement policies as only partial, since staff had received training but had not yet executed a contract under this lender's rules.
