## Resumo
- Modality: multi-sector investment loan (AFD, euro-denominated, floating-rate) to a state government, with a mixed disbursement design: traditional plus results-based disbursement tied to three disbursement indicators.
- Finances two roughly equal blocks (about 50% each) plus a small management block: primary-health strengthening (equipment/furniture kits for basic health units, vehicles, IT, telehealth diagnostics, oral-health for pregnant women) and water resources/rural sanitation (hydrometeorological and water-quality monitoring networks, river-basin plans and water-body classification, riparian reforestation, small dams, rural water-supply systems, household sanitation modules).
- Delivery: finance-secretariat project unit coordinating three co-executors (environment/water agency, state water-engineering company, health secretariat); entity type: state government.
- Counterpart at the 20% minimum; authorizing state law already exists but needs a wording update to the programme.

## Teoria da mudança e indicadores
- A.1 Theory of Change: ADEQUATE. The diagnosis links water-borne disease, rural water/sanitation gaps, weak water-resource management and primary-care resolution into one programme, with eight specific objectives and a stated health-water rationale; no explicit causal chain or testable assumptions.
- A.2 Results Framework: ADEQUATE. Verified 11 indicators: approximately 5 labelled outcome (health-service coverage, rural water/sanitation reach, diarrhoea hospitalisations in children under five, water-management coverage), 3 labelled disbursement indicators (telehealth municipalities, telehealth reports, oral-health for pregnant women) and the rest outputs (plans, monitoring network, water systems, sanitation modules). Verification mechanism not described.
- Baselines: all 11 have a stated value; 3 are zero baselines counted as defined because they are new-asset counts built by the programme (water systems, sanitation modules, rural families served); about 8 of 11 state a reference year (2018 or 2021), so year share about 0.73. Header share of 0.73 was revised accordingly.
- Benchmarks: national health-information and hospitalisation-rate definitions; water-resources instruments of the state policy. Inconsistencies: coverage baseline year (2018) differs from the more recent coverage figure quoted in the diagnosis; telehealth and report-count baselines are written in an unclear "x + y" format; targets mix "increase by %" with absolute levels.

## Sustentabilidade e riscos
- A.5 Financial Sustainability: WEAK. No O&M/OPEX quantification for rural water systems, dams, monitoring stations or telehealth services; sustainability is argued through programme alignment with the state multi-year plan (PPA) and prior sector-wide experience, not through a funding plan.
- A.3 Risk Assessment: ADEQUATE/WEAK. No risk matrix; procurement risk handled by trained staff following lender rules. Strongest part is safeguards: prior environmental analysis of water systems, dam-safety law compliance with a specific list of design practices, land-acquisition and involuntary-resettlement procedure with a public-utility decree and resettlement plans; environmental licensing by the state environment agency.
- Counterpart: sourced from the state treasury; the authorizing law exists (loan ceiling in euros, guarantee/counter-guarantee pledged via constitutional revenue shares) and is to be adjusted to the programme name after approval; programme is inserted in the PPA; no LOA line shown.
- A.4 Implementation: ADEQUATE. Finance-secretariat project unit with fiduciary role and three co-executors with long track records (water-engineering company, environment agency) and a prior World Bank sector-wide programme with results-based disbursement; responsibility matrix in an annex; coordination across three bodies is a risk. The CC notes an earlier withdrawal from the agenda at the state's request.
