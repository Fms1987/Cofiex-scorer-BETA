## Resumo
- Modality: conventional investment loan (IDB) with Union guarantee to a large capital-city municipal government; counterpart at about half of total cost, funded from municipal tax revenue. Direct execution by the municipal health secretariat through a programme management unit (UGP), a follow-on to an earlier primary-care and emergency-care programme with the same lender.
- Finances hospital-network modernisation: physical refurbishment and equipment of a subset of municipal hospitals, a few of them through PPP delivery (loan finances part of the concessionaire capital expenditure plus PPP modelling), plus an intra-hospital supply-logistics information system, quality certification of hospitals and laboratories, and staff training.
- Cost mix roughly half civil works, about a quarter the logistics system, 10-20% PPP capital contribution, remainder equipment, certification, management and evaluation consultancies. Disbursement horizon five years.

## Teoria da mudança e indicadores
- A.1 Theory of Change: ADEQUATE. Diagnosis (ageing, chronic disease burden, old hospitals not meeting sanitary and accessibility norms, no hospital role differentiation) maps to refurbishment, PPP, certification, logistics system and training, with an objectives-results-indicators alignment annex. Assumptions and sequencing risks (PPP timing, system adoption) are not spelled out.
- A.2 Results Framework: ADEQUATE. 10 indicators (verified): approximately 6 outcome/performance (elective surgeries, length of stay, institutional mortality, surgeries per theatre-day, discharges, user satisfaction) and approximately 4 outputs (share of facilities certified, share compliant on infrastructure, staff trained, PPP models delivered). Formulas are explicit, with data drawn from hospital-admission records and the facility registry (verification mechanism present for most), annual targets in an annex.
- Baselines: 5 non-zero baseline values; 4 zero baselines counted as defined because the CC presents the item as new in this programme (certification, infrastructure conformity after works, trained staff, PPP studies); the zero baseline on the 0-10 user-satisfaction score is not credible and counted as undefined. So baseline_defined_share is 0.9; no reference years stated, so baseline_with_year_share is 0.0.
- NT notes methodological limits on validity, reliability and feasibility of the indicators and asks lender-supported refinement of definitions. No wide-range or deferred indicators; no external benchmark index.

## Sustentabilidade e riscos
- A.5 Financial Sustainability: WEAK-ADEQUATE. CC says refurbishment does not materially raise operating cost; extra consumables are covered by budget growth and staff are reallocated from the PPP hospitals; incremental operating and maintenance costs are quantified in an annex and compared with hospital-area spending in the PPA and with expected net-revenue growth. No financing instrument or dedicated line shown; NT judged the measures insufficient.
- Counterpart: partly secured. Funds said to be already available for the works at a subset of hospitals to be tendered before loan signature; the rest comes from tax revenue with PPA alignment (hospital-care action); no LOA line detail shown.
- A.3 Risk Assessment: ADEQUATE-WEAK. No matrix; only the municipal election cycle is named as a risk (mitigation: sign the loan and start before it). Legal, environmental (biological waste, higher activity) and social/maintenance-cost risks judged imprecise or unexplored by NT. No environmental licensing needed per CC (works within existing hospital sites); no expropriation or resettlement. PPP structuring still at modelling stage.
- A.4 Implementation: STRONG-ADEQUATE. Same UGP structure as the prior programme with the lender, in-house engineering capacity built in the health secretariat, lessons learned listed; the NT scored borrower experience high but found the RACI matrix simplified and technical team not detailed.
