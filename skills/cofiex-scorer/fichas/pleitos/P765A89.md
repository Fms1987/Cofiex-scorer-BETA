## Resumo
Investment loan, direct execution by a state government, with an MDB lender. Finances a cross-sector programme combating food insecurity, forest-based environmental conservation/income generation, and accelerated learning outcomes, run jointly by social-assistance, environment, and education secretariats under a single component with three subcomponents, plus a management/M&E component. Budget is split roughly evenly across the anti-hunger (~36%), forest-conservation (~26%), and learning-acceleration (~36%) subcomponents, with dedicated targeting of a lower-IDH sub-region.

## Teoria da mudança e indicadores
A.1 Theory of Change: ADEQUATE — NT credits a clear, well-described three-front problem diagnosis (food insecurity, environmental degradation, learning gaps) with solutions and a management component framed as consistent with the magnitude of the problem; no explicit testable-assumptions chain is laid out.
A.2 Results Framework: WEAK — 17 verified indicators, well described and understandable per NT, but judged "simplistic": no stated timeline/milestones toward the targets and no foreseen future impact evaluation of beneficiaries. Mix leans output/process-heavy (adherence counts, centres/points installed, cash-transfer operations) against a smaller outcome set (food-insecurity rate, literacy rate, deforestation area). baseline_defined_share = 0.35 (6 of 17 indicators carry a non-zero stated baseline; the remaining zero baselines are not explained in the text reviewed as new-asset/counterfactual cases, so they count as undefined); baseline_with_year_share = 0.0 (no baseline carries a stated reference year). Independent technical verification of indicator achievement is explicitly deferred to the preparation phase. No standard external benchmark is cited; the proponent's own count of applicable UN strategic-sustainability areas is a self-assessment flagged by the NT as needing independent confirmation.

## Sustentabilidade e riscos
A.5 Financial Sustainability: WEAK — NT states the proponent gives no information on instruments or measures to guarantee financial sustainability of the programme's actions after execution; the requirement was scored low and marked incomplete. Counterpart (20%) is grounded in tax-revenue sources rather than an already-appropriated, dedicated budget line ("to be secured" in effect).
A.3 Risk Assessment: ADEQUATE on contracting/procurement risk (decentralised teams and technical/financial support as mitigation for local implementation-capacity risk) but WEAK on several fronts flagged directly by the NT: the proponent omitted the need for a state authorising law for the loan and for decree-based creation of the oversight commission/UGP; environmental risks from works/waste are acknowledged but not described or mitigated (deferred to lender review); social-impact risk toward vulnerable and traditional/indigenous populations is named but not detailed. No resettlement risk (land is state/municipal-owned).
Counterpart: tax-revenue sourced, not shown as a specific appropriated line — "to be secured".
A.4 Implementation: ADEQUATE — a joint-secretariat governance commission (decree-based) plus a dedicated, decentralised UGP with an infrastructure-specialist track is proposed; NT flags that team sizes are not specified and that the external lender's procurement/safeguards policies are not addressed. Prior cross-agent lending-execution experience scored at roughly half of the maximum reference benchmark (mixed track record, some delays reported with other creditors).
