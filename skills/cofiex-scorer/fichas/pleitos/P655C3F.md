## Resumo
- Modality: investment loan (IDB) to a state government, executed directly by the judiciary's own project unit; counterpart at the 20% minimum from the state treasury.
- Finances the digital transformation of a state court system: cloud migration, cybersecurity and data-protection tooling, equipment renewal and public-access digital spaces (about 60% of cost), judicial-system quality, data governance, automation/AI and social-justice digital services such as domestic-violence response and youth inclusion (about 35%), governance/HR management tools, and project management/audit.
- Four components, mostly goods and non-consulting IT services; floating-rate (SOFR-based) loan with a long grace period and a 25-year total term.

## Teoria da mudança e indicadores
- A.1 Theory of Change: ADEQUATE. Component-level expected results are listed and a diagnosis of problems (system downtime, scattered low-quality data, manual repetitive work, ageing equipment, weak governance) is linked to them, but there is no explicit causal chain or testable assumptions.
- A.2 Results Framework: ADEQUATE. Verified 5 indicators, all outcome-type institutional performance measures tied to CNJ benchmarks (quality award index, IT governance index, Meta 1, Meta 2, net congestion rate); no output indicators and none at component level, so the digital products (cloud, security, social-justice tools) are not directly measured.
- All 5 have a baseline value stated (none zero), none with a reference year or period (baseline_with_year_share 0.0). Targets are point values; formulas follow the CNJ glossary and a detail annex, but no separate verification/reporting mechanism is described beyond semiannual progress reports by the project unit.
- Benchmarks: CNJ Metas and CNJ governance resolution indices (strong, externally verifiable); indicators are broad judiciary-performance indices, so attribution to the project is an open weakness.

## Sustentabilidade e riscos
- A.5 Financial Sustainability: ADEQUATE/WEAK. Narrative states that cloud pay-per-use and equipment renewal lower maintenance cost and that evolving the governance tool will be absorbed in the court's budget; a sustainability annex and the state law creating the court's modernization fund are listed as annexes, but the main text has no quantified OPEX table or recurrent-cost projection.
- A.3 Risk Assessment: WEAK/ADEQUATE. Risk section covers procurement and contract-management risks with mitigations (late tenders, empty bids, incomplete specifications, vendor dependency, contract oversight); no matrix, no technology, cybersecurity or adoption risks; environmental, resettlement and licensing risks are declared not applicable.
- Counterpart: from the state treasury, to be secured; an amendment to the existing state authorizing law is yet to be sent to the legislature, and alignment with two programmes of the state multi-year plan (PPA) is argued; no LOA line shown.
- A.4 Implementation: ADEQUATE. Project unit inside the court, linked to the presidency, with coordinators for finance, procurement and M&E, product leaders and a legal adviser; responsibility matrix and semiannual reporting to the court plenary; prior steps (cloud pilot, equipment purchases, governance studies) show preparation.
