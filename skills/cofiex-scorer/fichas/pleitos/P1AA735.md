## Resumo
- Modality: investment loan from IBRD to a state government, with a 6-year (72-month) implementation and counterpart at the 20% minimum; execution by the state family-agriculture secretariat, which will delegate to a to-be-confirmed implementing agency hosting a UGP.
- Finances a rural development programme for family farmers and traditional communities: technical assistance and extension, agroecological and agroforestry production, food security support, agro-processing and collective business plans, market access (institutional purchasing programmes, popular fairs), land and environmental regularisation, and community fire brigades.
- Four components: collective economic structuring and marketing (roughly 35-40% of cost), family food production and food security (roughly 40%), territorial governance and sustainable landscape management (under 10%), and institutional strengthening/management/monitoring with gender and youth inclusion (about 20%, including a new agency headquarters, regional offices, vehicles and equipment).
- Delivery largely through grants/agreements to producer organisations on the basis of mandatory business plans.

## Teoria da mudança e indicadores
- A.1 Theory of Change: ADEQUATE. Three thematic axes with expected results (income and food security, access to markets, climate resilience and governance) and each indicator tied to results and PPA objectives; assumptions and causal links (for example how fire brigades or regularisation produce the stated environmental effects) are not made testable.
- A.2 Results Framework: ADEQUATE. 10 indicators (verified): about 3 outcome-type (families with improved food security and income, diversification of foods sold to public buyers, families linked to processing/marketing) and about 7 output/coverage counts (organisations supported, families served by extension, contracts, fairs, brigades, regularised holdings, women and youth participating); hence approximate output-heavy profile.
- Baselines: 7 of 10 stated (share 0.70): existing organisations, families already served by extension (also reused for the food-security indicator), families already in processing structures, existing institutional-purchase contracts, average products sold, structured fairs. Brigade, regularisation and women-and-youth indicators give no baseline (no zero explanation). No baseline reference year is given in any indicator, so the year share is 0.0 (a census year is cited only for the universe size).
- Verification: described for every indicator (technician records, public purchasing systems, field visits, land and environmental registries); no external benchmarks.
- Inconsistencies: the food-security outcome indicator reuses the extension baseline and its target of newly improved families is stated differently from the extension target; the regularisation target equals the number of new extension families, whereas the indicator says holdings fully regularised; two indicators combine groups (women plus youth).

## Sustentabilidade e riscos
- A.5 Financial Sustainability: ADEQUATE (qualitative). Asset O&M is transferred to the beneficiary organisations through cooperation agreements with co-responsibility clauses, conditioned on maintenance viability in mandatory business plans; the agency's digital systems, vehicles and offices go into its PPA budget; continuity relies on linkage to permanent policies (institutional purchasing, rural credit). No quantified O&M table or OPEX figures.
- A.3 Risk Assessment: ADEQUATE but optimistic. Six risks, all rated low impact/low probability (legal acts, procurement, expropriation, environment, social, resettlement); the CC itself admits institutional fragility of a recently created executing secretariat with staff still being formed; environmental category B; safeguard plan, consultation with traditional peoples and a gender/youth specialist promised; expropriation and resettlement stated as not applicable.
- Counterpart: to be secured. A state authorizing law is in preparation; part of the counterpart is to be met with the value of UGP civil-servant staff and other governmental programmes for the same beneficiaries, not new cash; PPA budgeting cited for recurring costs.
- A.4 Implementation: ADEQUATE with gaps. Secretariat as executing agency, a UGP hosted by an implementing agency still to be confirmed, with executive and technical directors, component managers and a monitoring unit; management council with social-control participation; many institutional partners; lender procurement rules and operations manual planned.
