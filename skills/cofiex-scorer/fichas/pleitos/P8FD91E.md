## Resumo
- Modality: conventional sovereign investment loan from NDB (USD, variable-spread SOFR-based, 5-year grace and 30-year total term), federal borrower, zero counterpart (federal entities are not bound by the 20% minimum under the resolution, per the NT).
- Finances a flagship high-complexity public hospital intended as a scalable, replicable "smart hospital" model: civil works (roughly 40% of cost), advanced medical equipment (about a third), sustainable-construction features, a technology/R&D/intelligent-systems component with a translational research centre, staff training, and a technical reserve.
- Delivery model: ministry as principal executor; a social organisation (OS) under a federal management contract builds, equips and trains; a state health secretariat is strategic partner and later contracts the OS to operate the hospital and bears annual operating costs. Site and cooperation agreement are still to be formalised.
- Entity type: federal ministry (national health policy manager).

## Teoria da mudança e indicadores
- A.1 Theory of Change: ADEQUATE. Four problems (fragmented data, fragmented patient journey, slow emergency/high-complexity response, weak translational research) map to seven specific objectives and five expected results; the replicability logic is stated but assumptions (adoption by other units, operator performance) are not tested explicitly.
- A.2 Results Framework: ADEQUATE at best. 12 indicators verified; approximately 8 are implementation-progress or output indicators (agreements signed, model elaborated, works progress, equipment, systems, staff trained, services enabled, innovation initiatives), approximately 4 outcome-type (emergency regulation time, digital-maturity level, efficiency index per bed, share of clinical decisions supported by digital tools).
- Baselines: only 1 of 12 states a baseline value and year (a national figure for emergency regulation time); the progress indicators have implicit starts from nothing but no stated zero, so they count as undefined. Efficiency index has no baseline and its formula is left "to be defined".
- Verification: sources and formulas named for most indicators; digital maturity is verified by an external international certification audit (a benchmark); several targets are 100% completion within a time window, weakly informative.
- Inconsistencies: the NT regards two of the indicators as execution indicators with little link to science/technology; indicator timelines are tied to operation start, which depends on an undefined site and unsigned agreements.

## Sustentabilidade e riscos
- A.5 Financial Sustainability: ADEQUATE. Annual operating budget estimated by category (staff, supplies, maintenance, support services, IT and cybersecurity, research, reserves) using high-complexity hospital cost benchmarks, plus depreciation and equipment-maintenance assumptions; to be paid by the state government through a management contract for services. This burden falls on a non-borrower entity, and the formal pact between the federal and state levels is still pending (single-year view, no multi-year table).
- A.3 Risk Assessment: STRONG on breadth. Matrix with impact/probability and mitigation for roughly two dozen risks (the NT counts 23): agreements and management-contract formalisation, financing delays, site definition, works, equipment, interoperability, cybersecurity, regulatory/accreditation, supply dependence, telehealth failure. Licensing is addressed through site/permit risk rather than per-component status.
- Counterpart: none required (federal borrower); the loan finances the whole programme, while the model design is funded by the ministry's own resources.
- A.4 Implementation: ADEQUATE. Internal working group across ministry secretariats and an OS executor; the OS is not yet qualified or contracted, and the ministry's experience with multilateral loans is asserted rather than evidenced.
