## Resumo
Investment loan, direct execution by a state government through its rural-extension agency, with an MDB lender. Finances a sustainable rural-development programme for small/medium farms: landscape/territorial planning, degraded-area recovery and sustainable production systems, environmental regularisation, water/sanitation support on farms, and market access for producer organisations and individual micro-entrepreneurs, plus an institutional-strengthening/management component. Two investment components split roughly 47%/39% of cost, with the remainder on institutional strengthening and management.

## Teoria da mudança e indicadores
A.1 Theory of Change: STRONG — NT credits a clear, satisfactorily demonstrated problem-to-solution chain (land-use pressure, soil/biodiversity degradation, low technical/credit access among small producers) leading to explicit resilience/inclusion/replicability goals.
A.2 Results Framework: ADEQUATE — 27 verified indicators (NT's own text cites a different count elsewhere, likely a transcription slip; the CC indicator table itself lists 27), output/process-heavy (farm- and territory-level counts: properties supported, area recovered, water/sanitation support, capacity-building headcounts) against one outcome-type sales-growth indicator. Almost all baselines are stated as zero, but the CC explicitly explains this: the intervention territories and the beneficiary producer associations/cooperatives "will be selected later according to technical criteria," which matches the spec's allowed zero-baseline exception for territory/beneficiary selection deferred to implementation. Applying that explanation, baseline_defined_share = 0.89 (24 of 27 indicators: 23 zero-baseline indicators tied to territory/association/beneficiary selection plus 1 indicator with a non-zero starting value); 3 indicators (capacity-building headcounts and a relative sales-growth measure) lack any such explanation and are counted as undefined. baseline_with_year_share = 0.0 (no baseline carries a stated reference year). NT itself flags scepticism about the zero-baseline convention, suggesting it may understate an already-existing production base — a caveat on an otherwise-covered exception. A time-series calculation memory per indicator is reported as attached in annex (verification mechanism present).

## Sustentabilidade e riscos
A.5 Financial Sustainability: ADEQUATE — NT credits a qualitative sustainability narrative (alignment with standing public policy, community adoption of sustainable practices, continued extension work, economic gains for beneficiary organisations, demonstration effect for neighbouring communities) and a cited state climate-action instrument, but flags the absence of post-project maintenance/replacement cost figures, so the item was only partially scored.
A.3 Risk Assessment: ADEQUATE — legal/institutional risk is flagged directly: the state authorising law for the loan and for the state counter-guarantees is still in the legislative pipeline ("to be secured," not yet enacted). Procurement risk is judged low because the long-standing executing agency already has an extensive field network and prior MDB-funded lending experience, with no new permanent hiring needed. No resettlement and no new environmental licensing/clearing of native vegetation is expected (interventions are on already-consolidated agricultural land); any licensing needs are left to individual beneficiary-level actions. Social risk toward traditional/indigenous/quilombola and other vulnerable communities is addressed via a cited international safeguard policy and the executing agency's own track record on social safeguards.
Counterpart: declared at 20%; the specific funding source is not detailed in the text reviewed — treated as "to be secured."
A.4 Implementation: STRONG — the executing agency is a long-established statewide rural-extension network (dozens of regional offices and local technical-assistance units, with a sizeable permanent and specialised staff) with prior experience executing MDB-funded rural-development operations, including on social/environmental safeguards with traditional and indigenous communities; prior-experience quality scored above the midpoint of the reference benchmark.
