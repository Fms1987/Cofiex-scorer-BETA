## Resumo
Investment operation for a coastal tourism-destination municipality, financed directly (not intermediated),
combining urban drainage and solid-waste management, tourism/urban-mobility infrastructure (roads, cycleways,
parking, street lighting with a renewable-energy component) and social equipment/income-generation actions (support
for individual micro-entrepreneurs, public digital-service systems), plus a programme-management component.
Delivery model is direct execution by the municipal administration, with a dedicated programme-coordination unit and
external management/supervision/audit consultancy support. Entity type: municipality (direct borrower), 20%
counterpart consistent with the subnational minimum.

## Teoria da mudança e indicadores
A.1 Theory of Change: ADEQUATE (improved across attempts) — the CC links disorderly tourism growth to multiple
urban-infrastructure deficits and proposes a correspondingly broad set of solutions and expected results; the first
attempt's NT found the diagnostic "somewhat confusing" and the general objective "too broad, such that many
unrelated solutions could fit it," while the final attempt's NT found problems, solutions and expected results
coherently linked, albeit still numerous/broad in scope.
A.2 Results Framework: ADEQUATE — 8 VERIFIED indicators, baseline_defined_share 0.75 (6/8): two
indicators (micro-entrepreneurs placed in the formal market; vulnerable-population/minority micro-entrepreneurs
installed) have zero baselines that read as "new activity" zeros but are not explicitly explained as such in the CC,
so per the baseline rule they were counted as undefined. baseline_with_year_share is also 0.75: every indicator in
the table states "Linha de base: Ano 2024 / Meta: Ano 2029," so all six baseline-defined indicators also carry an
explicit reference year. Outcome-vs-output split is approximate: about 3 read as outcome-type (flood-point count,
selective-collection coverage, degraded-area reduction) and about 5 as output/process-type (public-lighting cost,
irregular-parking count, micro-entrepreneurs placed, vulnerable-population entrepreneurs installed, digital-system
accesses). The NT flags recurring SMART weaknesses across both attempts: several indicators merely restate a
specific objective rather than measuring it, some formulas/baselines are internally confusing (e.g., the selective-
collection coverage indicator's calculation and 2029 target appear to imply a coverage decrease instead of the
intended increase; a digital-system-access indicator shows a non-zero baseline that contradicts the system not yet
being implemented at the baseline date), and an "area degradada" indicator lacks an operational definition. No
verification mechanism is stated for the indicators beyond the stated formulas.

## Sustentabilidade e riscos
A.5 Financial Sustainability: improved from PARTIAL in the first attempt to ADEQUATE in the final attempt — the
first NT credited the proponent with naming the responsible municipal secretariat/concessionaire for most assets'
O&M but scored this sub-item only partially (no clear cost figures for all actions); the final NT found annual
budget and estimated annual maintenance-cost figures presented for most actions (utility/concession arrangements for
new lighting and parking assets), with no need for new staffing cost (only incremental operating cost), earning full
marks on the cost-projection sub-item.
A.3 Risk Assessment: improved from PARTIAL (first attempt) to ADEQUATE (final attempt) — both NTs describe legal/
institutional risks (an authorizing municipal law is required), procurement risks (bidding delays, under-qualified
bidders) with mitigations (a dedicated procurement unit, advanced-stage bidding documents), and environmental/social
risks with mitigations (environmental licensing already underway with the municipal environmental agency, a social-
outreach technical plan). Both attempts explicitly state no resettlement is foreseen because intervention areas are
municipal public land, even though they also note some areas have irregular/informal occupation; the final NT
flags this as a social-impact gap that should have been assessed, without resolving it, and the first NT raised the
same concern about undocumented informal occupation on intervention sites. Licensing risk: environmental licensing
status tracked per work, under a named municipal environmental authority.
Counterpart: secured and verified — 20% counterpart matches both the ficha header and the CC text, sourced from
constitutional tax revenues and incorporated in the municipality's multi-year investment plan and annual budget law.
A.4 Implementation: ADEQUATE and improving — a dedicated programme-coordination unit (with a technical team:
general coordinator, works specialist, socio-environmental specialist, procurement specialist, financial-
administrative specialist) is described in both attempts, backed by external management/supervision/audit
consultancy support; the executing secretariat is credited with prior exposure to multilateral safeguards through a
previous nationally-financed tourism-development programme. The proponent has no prior external-credit-operation
experience in the applicable 10-year lookback window per the NT.
