## Resumo
- Modality: investment loan from a multilateral development bank under a standing sector line for subnational fiscal-administration modernization (third cycle for the borrower); counterpart at the 10% level, long grace period and total maturity of roughly 24 years.
- Finances institutional and technology modernization of a state treasury department, with a strong tax-reform adaptation theme: tax administration and litigation (data, AI, compliance, online services), planning/budget/spending systems, legal management of tax credit and judicial payment obligations, IT infrastructure and governance, people management with a gender/race/diversity policy, and small sustainable-building works on state-owned premises.
- Four components, roughly 40% / 30% / 20% / 10% of cost (fiscal management and transparency; tax administration and tax litigation; planning, budget and public spending; legal management for fiscal sustainability).
- Direct execution by the state fiscal authority through a coordination unit, with several partner state bodies under cooperation agreements.

## Teoria da mudança e indicadores
- A.1 Theory of Change: ADEQUATE. Problem diagnosis rests on a fiscal-management maturity assessment and workshops; objectives are split into two core and seven complementary specific objectives and link to products and PPA areas, but assumptions and causal pathways are not stated as testable, and the tax-reform dependency is treated mainly as a risk.
- A.2 Results Framework: ADEQUATE. 18 indicators verified; approximately 9 outcome-type (revenue-to-GDP, primary result, net-debt ratio, application availability, cost to collect, litigation return rate, online-service share, citizen interactions, strategic-plan target attainment) and 9 output-type (platform coverage, innovation mapping, training participation of women leaders, first-instance judgments in the new system, integrated planning model, recycling, water reuse, charging points, judicial-payment analysis).
- Baselines: all 18 state a baseline value and a reference year (2021 to 2024), so share defined 1.0 and with-year 1.0. Seven zero baselines counted as defined because the indicator measures a new system, platform or installation introduced by the project (judgement call: the letter implies this rather than saying it). One baseline text is garbled/ambiguous (women-leaders indicator).
- Every indicator carries a five-year target schedule, source and formula; verification relies on administrative systems and fiscal reports, no external evaluation mentioned.
- Inconsistencies: SMART text for availability claims a +10% gain while the target schedule moves a fraction of a point; the primary-result and deficit indicators are labelled "reduce the deficit" but expressed as a rising ratio; macro-fiscal ratios have weak attribution to the project; some indicators (recycling, water reuse, charging points) are peripheral to the core fiscal objective.

## Sustentabilidade e riscos
- A.5 Financial sustainability: ADEQUATE. Maintenance of technology and new systems is estimated as a low single-digit percentage of project value per year, with an annex forecasting the costs; during execution costs are covered by the loan, afterwards by state recurrent budget plus the fiscal authority's own modernization fund. No category-by-year table in the body text.
- Counterpart: 10% of total; the letter does not show LOA/PPA lines; the authorizing state law is to be sought after COFIEX approval, so counterpart and authorization are "to be secured".
- A.3 Risks: eight risks with impact, probability, mitigation and owner. Covers procurement delays, tax-reform transition delay, authorizing law, continuity of the data centre, environmental and resettlement (minor works on state-owned premises). The letter states no environmental licensing is required.
- A.4 Implementation: coordination unit with experienced staff from two earlier cycles of the same line; partner bodies also experienced; the bank will apply its institutional capacity assessment tool to the unit and partners. Strong UGP evidence.
