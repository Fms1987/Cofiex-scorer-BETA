## Resumo
- Modality: direct investment loan to a state government, co-financed by two lenders (USD and EUR tranches mirrored in a duplicated component structure); despite the header sector guess, it is not an intermediated fund.
- Finances international connectivity infrastructure operated by a neutral commercial operator, plus a state-owned backbone/metro network upgrade, public Wi-Fi access points, digital-skills training for low-income youth and women, a government data centre and digital-government platform, and institutional strengthening.
- 10 components in total (5 per lender, same scope); roughly 60% of cost sits in the international link and backbone, a small share in training, institutional support and project management. Counterpart at the 20% minimum.
- Executed by a newly created state IT agency with a project management unit (UGP) being set up; coordination by a state economic-development secretariat.

## Teoria da mudança e indicadores
- A.1 Theory of Change: STRONG. Five problems each map to causes, consequences, an action and a result; actions are linked to specific indicators and PPA areas; the price-competition-to-adoption chain is stated, though demand assumptions rest on a small provider survey.
- A.2 Results Framework: ADEQUATE to STRONG. 9 indicators verified, approximately 6 outcome and 3 output-type (access points, communities served, people trained); each states formula, source, annual monitoring and a 2032 target.
- Baselines: 8 of 9 stated with a 2024 reference (counted zero baselines for the new international link, the Wi-Fi points and the training programme, each explained as not existing yet); one indicator (communities served) has no baseline; one baseline is a speed range.
- Benchmarks: regulator-published broadband density, national averages, a research-institute elasticity of GDP to broadband; verification via the regulator, the neutral operator and the agency.
- Internal inconsistencies: the fixed-broadband baseline text attributes the value to the national figure; target horizon (2032) is long relative to disbursement.
- No indicator is gender- or ethnicity-disaggregated beyond the training and communities counts.

## Sustentabilidade e riscos
- A.5 Financial Sustainability: ADEQUATE. Annual maintenance of the international link is quantified (in BRL) with the first three years financed by the loan; the neutral operator pays operating costs and revenue is projected from contracted capacity (based on a small qualitative demand survey). Fibre-pair swaps with providers are proposed to cut route maintenance. State-owned network and inclusion programmes rely on state budget with no OPEX table; the agency is expected to become a public company (not yet done).
- A.3 Risk Assessment: 6 risks with impact/probability and mitigations: access agreement and fiscal space for the international link, environmental licensing and conditions (high impact, medium probability), agency coordination capacity, missing technical expertise, delay in setting up the neutral operator, stakeholder engagement with traditional communities. Mitigation largely via consultancies funded by the loan.
- Counterpart: described as the minimum 20%; state also funds a related enabling procurement from its own resources; LOA/PPA legal backing for counterpart not detailed.
- A.4 Implementation: new agency with legal mandate, UGP and procurement unit still being structured; institutional gap openly acknowledged and addressed with PMO and ESG consulting.
