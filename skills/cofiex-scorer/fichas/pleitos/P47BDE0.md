## Resumo
- Investment loan (IDB, 200-400M USD band, counterpart at the 20% minimum, five-year disbursement; floating SOFR-based rate of about 4.9%, 25-year term with 2-year grace) to a state government; a multi-sector social/security programme executed directly by the state through a planning secretariat acting as coordinating executor, with many partner secretariats.
- Six components: (1) social and situational youth violence prevention (evidence-based family/socio-emotional models, conditional cash transfer, LED lighting in low-income municipalities); (2) gender-based and minority violence (victimisation surveys, community prevention, protection services, electronic monitoring, aggressor pilot); (3) police and forensic modernisation plus environmental-crime response, roughly 40% of cost; (4) recidivism and reintegration in prison and juvenile-justice systems (work and education capacity, risk-need-responsivity model); (5) healthy ageing and care network (age-friendly facilities, home accessibility, caregiver support); (6) programme management and experimental evaluation.
- Strategic positioning claimed by the proponent is broad (many PPA objectives, most scored zero by the NT); the dominant weight is public security, with social protection as a secondary theme.

## Teoria da mudança e indicadores
- A.1 Theory of Change: ADEQUATE. Five interdependent fronts are tied to problems, objectives, components and indicators with international evidence cited for expected effect sizes; assumptions and risks to the causal chain are not stated as testable assumptions.
- A.2 Results Framework: STRONG (judged, approximate). 16 indicators, each with a SMART breakdown, numerator/denominator sources, mandatory disaggregations (sex, race, territory) and administrative registries as verification; roughly half are outcome rates (violence, recidivism, response time), the rest are output/coverage measures (access, lighting, facilities, training).
- Baselines: 14 of 16 have a stated baseline value with a reference year, including one zero counted as defined because the CC explains the interventions are not yet implemented. Two zero baselines for the ageing-network coverage indicators are counted as undefined because the CC does not explain them (they are programme-created activities, which arguably justifies the zero).
- Internal inconsistencies: one indicator states a 2023 baseline in the table but 2024 in its SMART text; the homicide indicator's numerator mixes homicide and femicide; target end dates differ (2030 vs 2031) among indicators; some targets are stated only as a year-5 value without annual milestones.
- Benchmarks: international evidence (programme models, effect-size ranges) is used to justify targets; no peer-lender cost benchmarks.
- Verification mechanism present; process and impact evaluations (experimental/quasi-experimental) are funded through the management component.

## Sustentabilidade e riscos
- A.5 Financial Sustainability: PARTIAL. A recurring-cost table by category (social offices, municipal facilities, women's service centres, digital systems, security equipment, prison workshops, cash-transfer schemes, behavioural-intervention models) is given in BRL, but the CC itself says final values will be detailed later; funding is declared as incorporation into state LOA/PPA and municipal adhesion terms for municipal facilities, with no specific budget line or legal act yet.
- A.3 Risk Assessment: ADEQUATE. Four risks (regulatory/institutional, environmental, contractual, social) with impact, probability, 6-8 mitigations each and responsible bodies; no explicit risk on technology/data protection or fiscal capacity. Environmental licensing risk is addressed through management plans and specialised supervision, without status per civil work.
- Counterpart: to be secured (schedule per component provided; counter-guarantees from constitutional state revenues, sufficiency to be assessed by STN in the PVL).
- A.4 Implementation: ADEQUATE to STRONG. A planning secretariat with a long multilateral portfolio and a coordinating unit already managing a similar security loan; roughly ten partner bodies formalised by cooperation agreements and transfers (to be signed); an existing integrated programme-monitoring system; responsibility matrix annexed.
