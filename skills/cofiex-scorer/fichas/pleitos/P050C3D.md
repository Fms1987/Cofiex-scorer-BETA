## Resumo
- Modality: conventional investment loan (CAF), direct execution by a mid-sized municipal government with counterpart at the 20% minimum; no intermediation.
- Financed: a broad multi-sector urban package rather than a single-theme programme: urban mobility corridors (roughly 40% of cost, the largest block), river-bank stabilisation and drainage, public squares/parks and recycling drop-off facilities, central-area urban requalification, urgent-care and specialist outpatient facilities, a school/sports centre rehabilitation, a municipal emergency operations centre and IT modernisation.
- Components: (1) environmental sustainability/urbanisation/social/institutional, (2) integrated and resilient infrastructure, (3) studies and designs, (4) land acquisition, (5) programme management, (6) financing fees. Health, education, security and environment items are each small shares of cost.
- Header sector label (environment_climate) is a keyword guess; mobility and urban requalification dominate.

## Teoria da mudança e indicadores
- A.1 Theory of Change: ADEQUATE. Problem tree by theme and four specific objectives mapped to components and to federal PPA strategic objectives; causal logic is mostly "asset delivered -> service capacity/time gain", with no explicit assumptions.
- A.2 Results Framework: ADEQUATE. 12 indicators (verified), each with name, formula, data to be collected, target, unit, target year and baseline with reference year; all targets share one end-year, and data collection is set for a period after operation starts (verification mechanism present, though no named source/system).
- Split (approximate): about 5 outcome-type (emergency response time, travel-time/level-of-service on corridors and bus route) and about 7 capacity/output-type (recycling capacity, urgent-care volume, number of specialties, school places, sports capacity, monitoring points).
- Baselines: all 12 state a value and a year (share 1.0 on both). One zero baseline counted as defined because the CC explains it (sports facilities currently closed).
- Weaknesses: three mobility indicators have formulas deferred to basic-design/traffic studies; several targets are expressed as "up to" reductions (range-like); mobility, health, education indicators are small relative to cost shares, and no indicator covers the drainage/bank-stabilisation or digital/IT components.

## Sustentabilidade e riscos
- A.5 Financial Sustainability: ADEQUATE. Annual O&M estimates are given per responsible secretariat (BRL, not reproduced here), with health facilities as by far the largest recurrent cost, to be met from municipal own resources plus SUS transfers; a current-year budget annex on O&M is attached; O&M plans per works package are promised at about 80% physical/financial execution (lender requirement). No multi-year projection.
- A.3 Risk Assessment: ADEQUATE. 11 risks with impact/probability and mitigation (utility interference, licensing, geotechnical cost overrun, contractor delay, expropriation resistance, legislative approval, system integration, digital skills, environmental). Licensing flagged; operating-licence process only starts at 80% execution.
- Resettlement: a few expropriations on the road works, with informal occupations on public land; compliance with social-protection norms stated, no resettlement plan described.
- Counterpart: not yet legally secured; the municipal multi-year plan for the next cycle is in final drafting and counter-guarantees are to be linked in the authorizing law (STN to assess at PVL stage).
- A.4 Implementation: ADEQUATE. A programme management unit (UGP) is yet to be created by decree under the finance secretariat, with three coordinations and external consultancies; experienced staff and prior domestic credit operations are cited. Execution split across many secretariats with centralised financial execution.
