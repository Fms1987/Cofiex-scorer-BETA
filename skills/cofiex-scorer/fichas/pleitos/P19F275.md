## Resumo
- Modality: investment loan (IBRD) to a state government under a fiscal recovery regime (RRF), with counterpart at about half of programme cost; guarantee needs RRF inclusion.
- Finances state road restoration (pavement, shoulders, drainage, bridges/structures, signage, third lanes at critical points), improvements on unpaved rural roads, environmental road measures (wildlife crossings, scenic road corridors, environmental liabilities) and an urban transit corridor component (clean-fleet renewal and transit infrastructure, tied to concession contract changes and a special purpose vehicle).
- Delivery: direct execution by the state roads agency through a UGP to be created, with several state secretariats as co-executors (monitoring, finance, environment); works selected through a pavement management system.
- Four components: transport modernisation (road restoration, rural mobility, environmental actions), urban mobility corridor, programme management (supervision, audit, studies, UGP support, institutional strengthening); roughly 60% of cost goes to road restoration.

## Teoria da mudança e indicadores
- A.1 Theory of Change: ADEQUATE. Diagnosis uses an audit-body road condition survey and a national road benchmark, results are listed (travel time, accidents, emissions, wildlife mortality, costs) and works are prioritised by a pavement management matrix; assumptions are not made explicit and the list of expected results is not tied to measurement.
- A.2 Results Framework: WEAK. Verified count: 4 indicators, all outputs (km restored, km of rural roads, corridor works, an emissions figure); 0 genuine outcomes. Formulas are percent-of-baseline constructions and units look mislabelled (a "children" quantity for roads, a currency sign for emissions).
- Baselines: all 4 have a stated value (100% defined, none a zero baseline) but no reference year (baseline_with_year_share 0). Baselines simply equal the planned physical quantities, so they are not true baselines. The NT, which may have judged an earlier version, says most indicators lacked a baseline.
- Targets are all 100% of the same quantity; no verification source or monitoring responsibility stated at indicator level; no benchmark except cost per km cited in the narrative.
- Inconsistencies: stated expected results (accidents, travel time, satisfaction) have no matching indicator; disbursement period is only a few years against a very large works scope.

## Sustentabilidade e riscos
- A.5 Financial Sustainability: WEAK-to-ADEQUATE. Road maintenance moves to performance-based contracts (routine upkeep included in early years, later repairs from the state treasury), a per-km maintenance cost spreadsheet is annexed; urban corridor running costs to be covered by transit fare revenue, tariff review and public subsidy backed by state law and LOA. NT still rates sustainability information incomplete.
- A.3 Risk Assessment: ADEQUATE-to-WEAK. Procurement, legal-act and environmental-licensing risks with mitigations; no formal risk matrix with probability/impact; expropriation, resettlement and social risks declared nil because works are on existing roads; licensing to be requested after executive designs; environmental sections lean on the argument that impacts are already absorbed.
- Counterpart: programme included in the multi-year plan; authorizing law for the loan still in preparation (the corridor component already has legislative authorisation); RRF inclusion pending, so counterpart is "to be secured".
- A.4 Implementation: ADEQUATE. UGP to be created under the roads agency with four coordinations and no new hiring planned; agency had one prior IBRD operation long ago but NT found no external-credit experience in the last decade; still learning the lender's procurement and safeguard policies.
- Fiscal context: CC states CAPAG B.
