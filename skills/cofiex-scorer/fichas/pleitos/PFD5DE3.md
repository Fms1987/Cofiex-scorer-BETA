## Resumo
- Modality: conventional investment loan (IDB, USD, long maturity with a 2-year grace period, variable SOFR-based rate), with a state counterpart at the 20% minimum; the new-hospital component is labelled as financed entirely by that counterpart.
- Finances a regional health-network programme: regionalisation governance and care-model training (small), physical rehabilitation and equipment modernisation of state-owned hospitals (roughly 60% of cost), digital health (EHR, telehealth; roughly 20%), a new regional general hospital (roughly 20%), and a small project-management component.
- Direct execution by the state health secretariat through a single coordination unit (UCP); no on-lending or transfers to other bodies. Municipal and regional offices take part only indirectly.
- Entity type: a large, high-capacity state government; population-wide target group (users dependent on the public system), with vulnerable groups cited narratively.

## Teoria da mudança e indicadores
- A.1 Theory of Change: ADEQUATE. Problem tree (regional service gaps, chronic-disease profile, fragmented governance, obsolete assets) maps to four solution blocks and expected results, each with a stated "how it addresses the problems" paragraph; no explicit testable assumptions or results chain diagram.
- A.2 Results Framework: ADEQUATE. 10 indicators verified; approximately 6 outcome (avoidable-admission share, two regional dependence indices, infant and maternal mortality, telehealth resolution rate) and approximately 4 output/volume (hospital admissions, outpatient volume, new-capacity throughput, telehealth growth).
- Baselines: all 10 state a baseline value and a year (2023-2025), so both shares are 1.0. One zero baseline counted as defined: throughput in new capacity, explained as a not-yet-existing asset.
- Verification: source systems named for most indicators (national hospital, outpatient, mortality and birth information systems); the two telehealth indicators cite only an internal portal; benchmarks are the national key indicators of the health objective.
- Inconsistencies: stated baseline year differs between description and target text on a few indicators (2024 vs 2025); the new-capacity indicator formula divides by beds while the target is per bed per month; the telehealth growth indicator mixes a programmed-vs-held ratio formula with an absolute-count baseline and a "per year" target; the volume target is phrased "up to" a percentage.
- Several indicators are state-wide or network-wide and only loosely attributable to the loan; the governance component is measured only by mortality and avoidable-admission outcomes (NT rated these as weakly representative of state capacity).

## Sustentabilidade e riscos
- A.5 Financial Sustainability: ADEQUATE. Recurrent costs for the new hospital, expanded services in existing hospitals and digital-system upkeep are estimated as monthly (BRL) figures, with the current monthly running cost of the existing hospitals given as a reference; funded through the state PPA, annual budget (LOA) and federal health transfers for new procedures. No multi-year O&M table by category and no dedicated legal instrument.
- A.3 Risk Assessment: ADEQUATE. Five risks with impact/probability ratings (governance and regional coordination, operating-cost shortfall, procurement/works delays, state government transition, legal/licensing) each with mitigation and responsible parties; licensing is treated generically, with no status per physical component.
- Counterpart: at the 20% minimum; the CC states there is no authorising law yet (to be secured) and cites PPA programme alignment; counter-guarantee drafted by reference to the standard manual wording.
- A.4 Implementation: ADEQUATE. Single UCP under the secretary's office, prior experience with an earlier IDB-financed management programme, safeguards familiarity; external audit and supervision products included; municipal and regional actors participate without formal execution roles.
