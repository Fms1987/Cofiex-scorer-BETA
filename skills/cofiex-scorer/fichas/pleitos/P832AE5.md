## Resumo
- Modality: investment loan from IDB to a capital municipality; direct execution by the municipal health secretariat (no on-lending), counterpart at the 20% minimum.
- Finances the reorganisation and expansion of a municipal health network: new and expanded hospitals including a specialised reference hospital, specialist outpatient units, an emergency-services base, a centralised regulation unit, a new secretariat headquarters and continuing training of staff.
- Three components; the first (infrastructure, equipment and new specialised services) takes roughly 70% of cost, the others cover regulation/management strengthening and services for priority groups.
- Delivery: works and equipment procurement under IDB-type bidding documents, run through the secretariat directorates; heavy on civil works, outcome indicators on access and regulation.

## Teoria da mudança e indicadores
- A.1 Theory of Change: ADEQUATE. Problem diagnosis (care, physical capacity, regulation/management, institutional) maps cleanly onto solution blocks and expected results, but assumptions and causal links to the indicators are not made explicit.
- A.2 Results Framework: ADEQUATE. 6 indicators verified; approximately 5 outcome-type (waiting time for specialist consultation, waiting time for elective surgery, bed-regulation time, share of elective admissions, share of own vs contracted production) and 1 management-process (budget execution rate).
- Baselines: all 6 state a baseline value with a reference year (2024 or 2025); no zero baselines counted. Hence baseline_defined_share 1.0 and baseline_with_year_share 1.0.
- Verification: national health information systems are named as sources for each indicator; no independent evaluation or verification arrangement described; no external benchmarks cited.
- Inconsistencies: the elective-surgery waiting-time target wording is contradictory (reduction size and end point do not reconcile); the elective-admissions indicator is labelled a percentage but its baseline and target are expressed as a count of surgeries; admissions and surgeries are conflated; the budget-execution indicator has a weak link to project outputs.
- NT view: indicators accepted as SMART for the access objective, but none targets the vulnerable groups the CC names.

## Sustentabilidade e riscos
- A.5 Financial Sustainability: ADEQUATE-but-thin. Recurrent costs are quantified annually in three categories (building upkeep, equipment maintenance, operating expenses), funded by the municipal treasury, with SUS custeio blocks mentioned as regular funding. The table has no multi-year horizon, no staffing or clinical running costs for the new hospitals, and looks small for a works-heavy portfolio (my judgement).
- Legal backing: new actions to be folded into the municipal health plan, annual programme and PPA; two PPA programmes cited; an annual budget line for upkeep is promised, not evidenced.
- A.3 Risk Assessment: 7 risks with impact, probability, mitigation and owner (licensing delays, cost escalation, staff shortage, procurement failures, design approval delays, equipment supply, works on a protected heritage site). Licensing is addressed in general terms; no fiscal, demand or operating-deficit risk.
- Counterpart (20% minimum): stated, with no LOA line, PPA action or authorising law cited, so "to be secured".
- A.4 Implementation: execution through the secretariat directorates, each with a small team; a project unit is referenced in risk mitigation but its composition is not distinct; the municipality cites prior IDB-financed experience.
