## Resumo
- Modality: investment loan from CAF to a large capital municipality, financing works and a public guarantee account inside a PPP concession for the requalification of a central riverside park and its transport interfaces; counterpart at about a quarter of cost.
- Six components: (1) new intermodal bus terminal, roughly 40% of cost; (2) underground stormwater reservoirs and gallery reinforcement; (3) requalification of spaces under viaducts, including a municipal guard base; (4) green areas and leisure/sport equipment; (5) road improvements, replacement of elevated structures by an at-grade bridge and transport complements, roughly 20-25%; (6) public guarantee account covering about one year of maximum monthly counter-payments, roughly 10-15%.
- Delivery model: private concessionaire builds, operates and maintains under a long-term PPP contract paid by monthly counter-payments; a municipal regulatory agency is the executing body, supported by the PPP secretariat, municipal technical and heritage bodies; the agency has no prior experience with international lenders.

## Teoria da mudança e indicadores
- A.1 Theory of Change: ADEQUATE. Four problems (fragmentation, unsafe active-mobility links, poor intermodal integration, flood vulnerability) lead to five specific objectives, seven thematic axes and one indicator per objective; the logic from physical works plus concession duties to outcomes is clear but assumptions are not stated as testable.
- A.2 Results Framework: STRONG (approximate judgment). 5 indicators verified, all outcome-type (walking time between transport nodes, perceived-safety index, flood-point count, green area per inhabitant, visitor numbers); each follows an explicit SMART breakdown with baseline value and year (all 5 counted), target horizon in the concession's seventh year, data sources and sampling described.
- Verification: periodic user surveys, field timing, municipal drainage mapping, geospatial green-area registry and census population, visitor counts; benchmark references to a WHO green-space range and to similar urban concessions.
- Inconsistencies: the green-area target is described as a 40% rise but the stated start and end values imply a considerably larger rise; the flood target is placed at year 5 in one place, and year 7 or a fixed calendar year in others; the safety target has two different values in the SMART text and in the target line; the visitor indicator is titled annual but baseline and target are daily; the green-area denominator is a district population that is small relative to users.

## Sustentabilidade e riscos
- A.5 Financial sustainability: ADEQUATE to STRONG. Annual operating and maintenance cost of the concession is quantified by sub-area (terminal, green areas, reservoirs, viaduct spaces, road works) and paid through monthly counter-payments; guarantee account sized at about twelve maximum monthly counter-payments, to be fully funded by year 6; step-in, substitution and service-continuity clauses; very low net debt-to-revenue ratio and PPP continuing obligations well below the 5% statutory ceiling cited for federal guarantees.
- A.3 Risk assessment: ADEQUATE. Six risks with impact/probability and mitigations (works effect on non-conceded public assets, operational conflict during terminal works, technical complexity of demolitions, riverbank environmental impact, irregular occupation and informal vendors, applicability of a clause in the municipal loan-authorising law). Environmental and heritage licensing responsibilities are mapped to municipal and state bodies; per-component licensing status not shown.
- Counterpart: described as a budget allocation directly to the executing agency, with no reallocation needed; the authorising municipal law exists but one clause is itself listed as a risk.
- A.4 Implementation: ADEQUATE. Clear multi-body institutional arrangement with RACI annex, but the executing agency lacks lender-financed project experience and no dedicated UGP is described.
