## Resumo
- Modality: investment loan from a regional development bank to a municipal government, direct execution by the municipality (a new project-management unit under the planning secretariat plus sectoral secretariats as co-executors), about 20% counterpart (the 20% minimum), 5-year disbursement, 5.5-year grace and about 14-year amortisation, floating rate plus a spread from 2%.
- Multi-sector urban development programme in four components: (1) digital government and fiscal efficiency (multipurpose cadastre, system interoperability, innovation hub); (2) sustainable urban infrastructure and climate resilience (industrial/logistics-zone roads, public-transport corridors, nature-based works in a flood-prone area, risk-sector interventions); (3) social infrastructure (early-childhood and primary schools, primary-care units, community sports/leisure spaces); (4) programme management.
- Resources are spread across many themes; roughly half goes to component 2, about a quarter to component 3 and about a tenth each to components 1 and 4 (rounded cost shares), so no single social or digital theme dominates.

## Teoria da mudança e indicadores
- A.1 Theory of Change: ADEQUATE. A general objective, three specific objectives and a component/product/result correlation table link each product to a result, but there is no explicit causal chain with testable assumptions; results are described as benefits and several are broad (systemic effect on the whole municipality).
- A.2 Results Framework: ADEQUATE. 14 indicators verified, each documented with a SMART narrative, formula, target, yearly trajectory and a mapped strategic objective; approximate split 6-7 outcome vs 7-8 output/progress indicators (physical execution percentages, a count of interventions, a cumulative count of trained people). Programme management (component 4) has no indicator.
- Baselines: 7 of 14 have a stated value with a reference year (2023-2025): cadastre coverage, education indices (two), primary-care coverage, and three where the zero is explained by the CC (no multipurpose modules exist; systems start non-integrated; business-count variation measured from a stated starting stock). I did not count the zeros for pavement/road works, fiscal-revenue variation, civil-defence calls, intervention count and trained people (unexplained 0). One indicator (maintenance-condition index) has no baseline, justified as first-time application. Hence both shares 0.5.
- Verification: partial. Most indicators cite a data source (national and state education assessments, health ministry coverage data, civil-defence reports, contract service reports, municipal tax data), but the output indicators rely on the project's own reports and no independent verification is described.
- Weaknesses: a highway-maintenance index borrowed from a federal road-concession methodology applied to urban streets; education targets stay flat for most years then jump in the last year; some indicators (business-count growth, tax revenue) sit far from the products; several roads/logistics indicators are linked to strategic objectives the NT later judged not met. The NT itself found the sports and disaster-risk indicators not SMART.
- Benchmarks used: IDEB (plus a state-level education index), national primary-care coverage metric, federal road-maintenance index.

## Sustentabilidade e riscos
- A.5 Financial Sustainability: PARTIAL. O&M is assigned secretariat by secretariat (urban upkeep, public lighting, health, education, sports/innovation hub) with funds said to be in each secretariat's budget under the municipal multi-year plan, plus a promise of an O&M plan at design stage; no O&M cost table. Funding logic relies on higher property-tax yield from the cadastre, transfers triggered by new schools/health units, partner-shared operation of the hub, and environmental compensation funds for the first years of logistics-zone upkeep.
- A.3 Risk Assessment: matrix of 16 risks with impact/probability and mitigation, linked to products. Includes sector risks (aerial-imagery weather limits, cybersecurity, land availability for health units, school calendar vs works, rainy-season works, staffing for new facilities, maintenance funding) and licensing/land/resettlement risks (one rated medium impact, high probability). Weak points: the maintenance-funding risk appears twice, one resettlement risk text refers to another municipality (copy-paste), a mitigation date for school delivery is inconsistent, and no resettlement plan or licence status per component is given.
- Counterpart: at the 20% minimum; supported by annexed budget-law excerpts per secretariat and alignment to the municipal multi-year plan, but not shown as secured line by line. Counter-guarantees pledged from municipal tax and transfer revenues; sufficiency to be assessed by STN.
- A.4 Implementation: ADEQUATE. Project-management unit still to be created under the planning secretariat (organogram annexed), with sectoral managers per product, a collegiate governance body already created by decree, a digital monitoring system, and prior experience with credit-funded programmes; execution centralised in the unit, details deferred to preparation. Bank consent letter annexed.
