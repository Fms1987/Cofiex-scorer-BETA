## Resumo
- Modality: investment loan (FONPLATA) to a mid-sized municipality, executed directly by the municipal infrastructure secretariat through a project unit still to be created by decree; counterpart at the 20% minimum from tax revenue.
- Note: the header sector keyword (water) is a mismatch; this is urban mobility/road infrastructure.
- Finances one urban waterfront road corridor built on reclaimed (hydraulic landfill) land: a multi-lane road linking to a federal highway interchange, service road, cycle path, sidewalks, parking, leisure/sports areas, LED lighting, drainage and special structures. Civil works are the large majority (about 85%) of cost; the rest is studies, small expropriation, supervision, project unit and audit.
- Part of a longer corridor delivered in segments by two neighbouring municipalities, one segment already built and another in negotiation.

## Teoria da mudança e indicadores
- A.1 Theory of Change: ADEQUATE. Diagnosis (saturated expressway, fast vehicle-fleet growth, traffic forecasts) leads to a bypass-and-public-space solution with a long list of expected benefits (shorter trips, fewer jams, lower operating cost, fewer accidents, cycling and walking use, leisure space); benefits are listed rather than linked by testable assumptions, and some are "unplanned".
- A.2 Results Framework: ADEQUATE/WEAK. Verified 4 indicators, all outcome-type (travel time on the link, cycle-path users, users of public/leisure spaces, share of current traffic diverted); no output indicators. Road safety is a stated objective and benefit but has no indicator (internal inconsistency).
- Baselines: all 4 have a stated value; the one zero baseline (traffic share diverted to a road that does not yet exist) is counted as defined because the CC explains it as a new asset; no baseline states a reference year (baseline_with_year_share 0.0). Calculation memory is in an annex; the main text names no verification source or survey method, and the travel-time unit is written ambiguously.
- Benchmarks: none cited; targets are point values. Traffic-forecast studies from a federal road study are used for the diagnosis.

## Sustentabilidade e riscos
- A.5 Financial Sustainability: ADEQUATE. Maintenance of pavement, drainage, sidewalks, squares and lighting is to be covered by existing municipal maintenance contracts funded from own tax revenue and the public-lighting contribution; no new staff planned. Not quantified (no O&M cost figures).
- A.3 Risk Assessment: ADEQUATE/WEAK. No risk matrix. Detailed coverage of environmental impacts (marine works, dredging, fishing, hydrodynamics) with a list of mitigation programmes, licensing sequence (preliminary then installation licence by the state agency) with a long timeline, and procurement risk mitigation. Weak points: federal road agency approval of the interchange not yet started (design not final), maritime and federal property authorisations pending, expropriation (non-residential, no families) not yet negotiated; an earlier environmental licence relates to a prior, larger design.
- Counterpart: tax-revenue sources named under the COFIEX counterpart resolution; budget and counterpart to be set by a specific law after COFIEX recommendation (to be secured); alignment with the municipal PPA mobility programme and the master plan cited.
- A.4 Implementation: ADEQUATE/WEAK. Project unit with a small multi-disciplinary team (including a socio-environmental role) to be created by decree; supervision consultancy and external audit planned; engineering design and environmental studies were contracted but still under way, so the project is at an early preparation stage.
