## Resumo
- Investment loan to a very large municipality, co-financed in equal parts by IDB and IBRD (ticket in the top band, counterpart at the 20% minimum, Union guarantee requested, five-year disbursement). The header sector guess (fund intermediation) is wrong: it is urban public-transport decarbonisation.
- Component 1 (about 98% of cost) is a capital subsidy covering the price gap between battery-electric and diesel buses; the vehicles are bought and owned by private concessionaires, who fund the diesel-equivalent share and the charging infrastructure. The loan covers roughly 40% of the municipal electrification target; the rest is meant to come from other credit and treasury funds.
- Component 2: institutional-strengthening studies (electric operations and staff training, mode-shift incentives, financial sustainability and inclusive fares, governance, supply chain, gender inclusion incl. women drivers); Component 3: project management unit.
- Delivery is intermediated: municipal finance secretariat pays the differential, the transit authority (state-owned company) coordinates and amends concession contracts.

## Teoria da mudança e indicadores
- A.1 Theory of Change: ADEQUATE. Clear chain from legal emission-reduction mandates and a pilot with a small electric fleet to subsidised fleet replacement, lower emissions, lower operating cost and better service; supply-chain and grid-capacity assumptions were checked with manufacturers and the utility, a prior economic comparison of financing models supports the design. The link between fleet replacement and the modal-shift results is asserted but covered only by studies.
- A.2 Results Framework: ADEQUATE. 7 indicators verified; approximately 5 outcome-type (three emission reductions, service satisfaction, operating-cost reduction) and 2 output-type (vulnerable census sectors served, staff trained). Emission indicators cite a methodology annex (not visible in the CC text); the NT notes targets lack timing and data availability within the execution period.
- Baselines: 3 of 7 counted as defined (satisfaction score, vulnerable-sector count, staff trained at a new-capability zero). The four percentage-reduction indicators start at an unexplained 0 and were not counted. No reference year is stated for any baseline.
- Verification mechanism only implied (survey scale, annex formulas); benchmark cities used for operating-cost expectations; the operating-cost indicator is a ratio with a vague formula.

## Sustentabilidade e riscos
- A.5 Financial Sustainability: WEAK-to-ADEQUATE. Operating and ownership responsibility sits with concessionaires, remunerated through concession contracts; pilot data and benchmark cities suggest lower operating cost, and a financial-sustainability study is financed. No forecast of O&M or battery/vehicle replacement cost after the programme (flagged by the NT).
- A.3 Risk Assessment: STRONG. Detailed legal-risk treatment of a capital contribution to private concessionaires (budget-law restriction on aid to for-profit firms, concession-bidding equality, contract amendments), asset identification and disposal, loss/insurance, collateralisation of subsidised vehicles, reversibility of fleet assets, tax accounting; supply and grid capacity; road-safety (quiet vehicles) and battery supply-chain risks; no expropriation or resettlement; no licensing items.
- Counterpart/authorisation: external borrowing covered by a municipal authorising law within its ceiling and by the annual budget law's general clause; counterpart origin stated as compliant with Res. 3/2019, no specific budget line shown. External amount was later adjusted at approval under the Art. 5 paragraph 5 prerogative of Res. 17/2021.
- A.4 Implementation: ADEQUATE. Project management unit to be created by administrative act under the transit authority, with finance secretariat and focal points; responsibility matrix simplified and no technical team detail (NT), but strong lender-portfolio track record and prior World Bank/IFC technical assistance.
