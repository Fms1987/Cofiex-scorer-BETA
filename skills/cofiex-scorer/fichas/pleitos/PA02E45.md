## Resumo
- On-lending credit line (global credit programme) in which a regional development bank, acting as borrower and executor, passes multilateral funding through as sub-loans, with counterpart at the 20% minimum (about 80/20 lender/counterpart), after a major climate disaster in a state.
- Two products under a single component: working-capital and investment credit for micro, small and medium enterprises (about 80% of cost, including cooperatives and microcredit institutions) and credit to municipalities (about 20%), preferably small ones, for reconstruction and disaster-risk resilience subprojects.
- Sub-projects are not yet identified (menu of eligible investments only); disbursement period of two years; the CC text names a different multilateral lender than the ficha header.

## Teoria da mudança e indicadores
- A.1 Theory of Change: ADEQUATE. Clear diagnosis (disaster, liquidity shock, weak access to credit, municipal inability to borrow directly) and credit-for-recovery logic; no explicit assumptions or causal link to jobs preserved, only a disbursement condition tied to job maintenance.
- A.2 Results Framework: WEAK. 4 indicators verified, all portfolio-volume or client-count measures of the bank's book (essentially output/portfolio, no outcome on recovery, resilience or employment). Approximately 0 outcome indicators.
- Baselines: all 4 stated with non-zero values; no reference year or period stated (only a currency conversion date), and no target deadlines.
- Verification: indicators drawn from the bank's own portfolio data, but no explicit source/frequency; targets do not add up to the total programme amount (NT point).
- Internal inconsistency: currency of the SME product in the spending table differs from the rest of the CC; benchmarks absent.

## Sustentabilidade e riscos
- A.5 Financial Sustainability: ADEQUATE. Spread-based margin covers the commitment fee; cash flow annexed; no O&M issue as there are no owned assets; sub-borrower credit analysis and the bank's low delinquency (relative to system) are the main support, with an acknowledged expectation that delinquency will rise after the disaster and repayment moratoria.
- A.3 Risk Assessment: ADEQUATE. Narrative by category (legal, contracting, expropriation, environmental, social), no matrix; relies on an institutional environmental-social-climate risk system and exclusion of projects with involuntary resettlement; sub-project licensing deferred to selection stage (no cost estimate for mitigation yet).
- Counterpart: required at the lender's 20% share, to come from final beneficiaries and the bank's own funds (the bank cannot use own resources for the public sector); not tied to LOA/PPA since the borrower is a financial institution.
- A.4 Implementation: ADEQUATE. UGP inside the bank's financial superintendency with defined roles (procurement, financial, socio-environmental, technical analysis) and a responsibility matrix annexed; external-credit track record is substantial but portfolio disbursement performance was rated unsatisfactory in the NT.
