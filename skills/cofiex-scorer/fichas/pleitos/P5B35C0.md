## Resumo
Investment Project Financing (IPF) operation for the federal direct administration, financed without counterpart
(federal entity, no counterpart required under the applicable COFIEX resolution). Finances a pilot performance-based,
long-term road maintenance management model for part of the federal road network, with (i) a main infrastructure
investment component (performance/long-term maintenance contracts), (ii) a road-safety and environmental-sustainability
component, (iii) an institutional-strengthening/technical-studies component, and (iv) a programme-management/UGP
component. Delivery model is direct execution by a federal transport infrastructure agency.

## Teoria da mudança e indicadores
A.1 Theory of Change: ADEQUATE — the CC presents a clear diagnostic (deteriorated federal road-network maintenance
condition harming logistics/safety) and a causal link to the proposed performance-based, long-term maintenance model,
but the NT across all three attempts flagged that expected results are not addressed directly/precisely enough,
limiting full understanding of the causal chain (rated as an unsatisfactory sub-item in the NT's design section in
the first attempt, improving to satisfactory in the final attempt).
A.2 Results Framework: WEAK — 8 VERIFIED indicators. baseline_defined_share is 0.375 (3/8): only the long-term-contract
coverage, DNIT maintenance-condition index and Logistic Performance Index indicators carry an explicit non-zero
baseline value; the other five (intelligent system implemented, strategic plan developed, women's GRM-awareness
training, GBV-sensitization training, BIM-trained staff) have zero baselines that are plausibly "new activity" zeros
but are NOT explicitly explained as such in the CC text, so per the baseline rule they were counted as undefined.
baseline_with_year_share is 0.0 — no indicator baseline carries an explicit reference year in the table. Outcome-vs-
output split is approximately 2 outcome-type (maintenance-condition index, Logistic Performance Index) vs 6
output/process-type (contract-coverage share, system implementation, plan development, the two training indicators,
staff trained). The NT repeatedly notes indicators are "efetividade e de produto" (effectiveness/output type) and
flags that no indicator measures the programme's own maintenance-cost-reduction objective or road-safety improvement,
despite these being stated programme objectives — an internal inconsistency between objectives and the indicator set.
No verification mechanism is stated for any indicator. Benchmark used: the national Logistic Performance Index.

## Sustentabilidade e riscos
A.5 Financial Sustainability: WEAK across all three attempts per the NT — operation and maintenance of the resulting
assets is argued as a continuation of existing recurrent road-maintenance budget practice, with an expected cost
reduction (the final attempt cites an expected 20-40% maintenance-cost reduction), but no post-programme cost
projection (OPEX table) was ever presented; the NT scored this sub-item zero in the first attempt and only partially
in later attempts, explicitly noting the absence of maintenance-cost projections after programme completion.
A.3 Risk Assessment: ADEQUATE — the NT across attempts describes identified risks (legal/institutional acts,
procurement, expropriation/resettlement, environmental and social impacts) with corresponding mitigating actions;
environmental licensing is addressed (environmental regularization programme, EIA where needed); it notes
expropriation/resettlement is not expected since works would use existing road right-of-way, with contingency
procedures described if needed. A recurring gap flagged by the NT in all three attempts: no measures are mentioned
for the institution of the UGP itself as a legal/institutional risk item.
Counterpart: none required — the CC states no counterpart applies because the proponent is a federal direct-
administration entity (header counterpart_pct 0.0, consistent with the text).
A.4 Implementation: the NT's institutional-arrangement score improved across attempts (from unsatisfactory, due to
missing detail on the executing agency's operational structure, to acceptable in the final attempt, once the
executing federal transport-infrastructure agency's experience and regional structure were detailed), but every
attempt notes the responsibility matrix leaves gaps (e.g., it does not name a specific accountable actor for
"internalization of financing resources" and does not address UGP-institution-related acts). The proponent entity has
no prior external-credit-operation experience in the relevant 10-year lookback window, per the NT.
