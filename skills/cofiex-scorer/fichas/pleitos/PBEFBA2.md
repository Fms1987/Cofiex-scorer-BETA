## Resumo
- Investment loan (CAF, USD band 100-200M, counterpart at the 20% minimum) to a capital municipality acting directly as borrower and executor, with a federal guarantee; floating SOFR-based rate with a modest spread and a possible compensatory-fund discount, semiannual payments, long grace period.
- Integrated urban climate-resilience package, with 4 components: (1) resilient city (about 90% of cost): urban drainage and channel rehabilitation, linear parks and green infrastructure with restoration of a sensitive mangrove area, a gender-safety facility, and tourism-oriented road corridor works including grade-separated structures, plus studies and designs; (2) institutional strengthening and training for operation and maintenance (very small); (3) management support, works supervision and audit; (4) financing costs.
- Delivery: a programme execution unit housed in the municipal infrastructure secretariat and its public works company, with partner secretariats; no transfers between agencies.
- The pitch frames road works as tourism-access infrastructure, which sits awkwardly with the climate-resilience core.

## Teoria da mudança e indicadores
- A.1 Theory of Change: ADEQUATE. Problem-cause-consequence tables by thematic axis (flooding and drainage, green infrastructure and safety, mobility, institutional) map to solutions and numbered products, but there is no explicit results chain and no testable assumptions; assumptions surface only as risks.
- A.2 Results Framework: WEAK. 13 indicators (verified), roughly 6 outcome-type and 7 output-type (approximate judgement); each has a stated formula, source agency and periodicity, so verification channels are present, but several are delivery counters (km of roads, share of crossings, trained staff, share of assets with a maintenance plan).
- Baselines: only 5 of 13 carry a baseline value (all with a reference year): maintenance-cost, urban-tree-cover, public-space violence, and two travel-time indicators. Eight are undefined: 7 zero baselines are justified only as "no comparison data" (not as new asset or counterfactual), so none were counted as defined, and one more states zero for visitors with no explanation. Share with year = same 5/13.
- Internal inconsistencies: the cost indicator's baseline equals its own target; the sewage-points indicator has a zero baseline yet a target of eliminating all points; the green-area indicator's rationale speaks of hectares while the target is in m2; the tree-cover formula differs from its stated rationale (parks); two travel-time indicators overlap; the results section cites indicator numbers beyond the 13 listed.
- No outcome indicator directly measures flood incidence or flooded area (only the municipal cost proxy), despite flooding being the central problem. No international benchmarks; all targets fixed to 2030.
- Wide-range or deferred indicators: none, but the travel-time reduction target is ambitious versus its baseline.

## Sustentabilidade e riscos
- A.5 Financial Sustainability: ADEQUATE. Annual maintenance cost is quantified by responsible agency and the municipality points to an existing budget line covering routine upkeep; operation-and-maintenance plans per work are deferred until about 80% disbursement (lender requirement), and operating licences are also sought only from that point. A municipal conservation/maintenance law is annexed. Component 2 (institutional) is funded only by counterpart.
- A.3 Risk Assessment: ADEQUATE to STRONG. A matrix of 10 risks with impact, probability, 3 mitigations each and named owners: licensing and inter-agency delay, utility interferences (high/high), environmental conditionality, land and expropriation, procurement, social acceptance, gender-safety, exchange rate and counterpart shortfall (high/high), indicator-data governance, and O&M deterioration. Licensing risk is explicit; the NT judged the matrix comprehensive.
- Counterpart (20% of total cost): to be secured; the municipality only commits to include it in future LDO/LOA. The municipal PPA already in force is cited; federal PPA strategic objectives are claimed for 8 areas.
- Counter-guarantee: municipal own revenues and constitutional transfers, sufficiency to be assessed by STN at the PVL stage; lender no-objection letter is annexed.
- A.4 Implementation: ADEQUATE to STRONG. UGP-type unit with 7 coordinations in the infrastructure secretariat, a bidding committee, a responsibility matrix, prior experience with several external loans (including multilateral), external consultants for supervision; designs for the drainage works are described as advanced, but several other products (parks, lighting/CCTV, road works) are still at the stage of defining polygons, quantities and terms of reference.
