## Resumo
- Investment loan (IBRD) to a capital municipality for an integrated social-assistance model: modernisation and expansion of the social-assistance facility network (including food-security kitchens and a food bank), integrated service protocols and digital management systems, productive-inclusion workshops for vulnerable people, and institutional strengthening of the social-assistance secretariat.
- Delivery: direct execution by a municipal planning secretariat through a project management unit (UGP), with the social-assistance secretariat as co-executor; no on-lending.
- Four components with sub-components; counterpart about a quarter of cost, partly in-kind (municipal land independently appraised) and partly financial.

## Teoria da mudança e indicadores
- A.1 Theory of Change: ADEQUATE. Problem (fragmented network, rented and precarious facilities, falling federal co-financing) leads to solutions and results per component, but assumptions are not explicit or testable.
- A.2 Results Framework: ADEQUATE. 4 indicators verified, approximately 3 outcome-type (service integration, productive insertion, food-security capacity) and 1 output-like (share of modernised facilities). Each has formula, target, target year and data-to-collect list; no explicit verification source or independent evaluation mechanism.
- Baselines: all 4 stated as 0% with a year (2024 or 2025); I counted all as defined because the CC explains each (no integrated service today, new model, no facility meeting standard, growth measured over a to-be-documented existing capacity). The food-security baseline is a relative-growth framing, so the absolute baseline is still pending.
- Targets are very ambitious (100% integration and modernisation, several-fold growth in meal capacity); no benchmarks used.
- Internal consistency: one target is expressed as a ratio over an undocumented base; the horizon is set at the end of the execution period.

## Sustentabilidade e riscos
- A.5 Financial Sustainability: ADEQUATE. Additional recurrent costs are quantified and shown as a modest share of the secretariat's projected budget; savings expected from ending rent and from energy/water-efficiency measures (claimed operating-cost reduction around 40%). Federal co-financing cuts are acknowledged as a pressure. Productive-inclusion units are expected to become self-sustaining via sales or PPP arrangements, flagged as a risk in the CC itself. No dedicated legal instrument for O&M funding.
- A.3 Risk Assessment: 4 risks with impact/probability and mitigation (integration across components, financial sustainability of productive units, political change, operational/works risks). Sector risks present: land availability, heritage-body approval of works, parallel works supervision, service continuity during works. Mitigation: land already identified and appraised, standard federal designs.
- Counterpart: land in kind plus a financial share, with the municipal multi-year plan cited; budget securing (LOA) not detailed ("to be secured").
- A.4 Implementation: UGP in the planning secretariat with specialised staff (finance, procurement, engineering, safeguards, M&E) and a management-support firm; co-executor is the sector secretariat. Staff not yet hired.
