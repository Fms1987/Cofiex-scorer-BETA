## Resumo
- Investment operation (external credit), direct modality: borrower is a capital municipality, executed through its own planning/strategic-affairs secretariat with a program management unit (UGP) housed in the resource-mobilization directorate.
- Finances an integrated logistics-mobility-drainage program: road duplication/widening, exclusive/preferential public-transport lanes, cycle-infrastructure, pumping-station capacity expansion for stormwater drainage, plus a logistics/mobility management-innovation observatory and laboratory, and a social-infrastructure/competency-building component for vulnerable populations.
- Components: structuring logistics/mobility/sanitation works (the bulk of cost), management-innovation (observatory/laboratory), social infrastructure and workforce/income generation for vulnerable groups, and program-management-unit support.
- Entity type: capital municipality, infrastructure/mobility sector with a logistics and social-inclusion secondary emphasis; delivery is direct through municipal secretariats (no PPP/concession or intermediated on-lending).

## Sustentabilidade e riscos
- A.5 Financial sustainability: evolved markedly across the three attempts. The first version gave only a generic description of road/drainage maintenance funded by tariff and own revenues with no cost figures (NT: "imprecisas"). The second added named maintenance-responsible agencies and one budget-law line for a broader municipal program, but still without a stated share earmarked for the new assets (still "imprecisas"). The final version quantifies multi-year budget appropriations for the two maintenance-responsible agencies (one funded from the municipal operating budget, one from water/sewage tariff revenue) and mentions a possible future PPP arrangement for maintaining some new assets — rated adequately met. (Note: the ficha header's `mentions_opex_sustain` field is 0, which appears inconsistent with this O&M discussion found across all three NTs; flagged for review.)
- Counterpart (20% of cost, confirmed in the CC per header `counterpart_check: match`): backed by multi-year budget-law appropriations and alignment with the municipal multi-year plan (PPA 2022-2025); however, the municipal law specifically authorizing this credit operation with federal guarantee still requires approval by the municipal legislature and had not been enacted as of the versions reviewed.
- A.3 Risk assessment: a consistent risk-category structure (legal acts/authorizations, procurement, expropriation/resettlement, environmental impact, social impact) with mitigation measures appears in all three attempts; NT repeatedly notes some clarity issues (legal/institutional risk narrative overlaps with procurement-risk narrative; the social-impact write-up does not fully convey its own mitigations) and calls some resettlement mitigation proposals "incipient."
- Licensing: the proponent does not consider environmental/operating-license issuance to be a high risk given its institutional experience, though a schedule risk is flagged — delay in obtaining asset title and installation/operating licenses, both preconditions for works — without a full per-component licensing-status table.
- A.4 Implementation: UGP housed in the municipal strategic-planning/resource-mobilization structure, with defined staffing profiles (general coordination, technical-administrative, procurement, fiduciary/financial) and a dedicated preparation unit (UPP) planned ahead of full UGP stand-up; NT calls the institutional-responsibility matrix "not very detailed" but adequate overall, and cites the proponent's average external-financing portfolio performance as good (around 72%).
