## Resumo
Investment operation with a subnational government borrower, financed by IBRD, delivered directly by state executing agencies (no intermediated on-lending). Program organised in two axes: integrated water-resource governance/management instruments, and multiple-use water security in priority areas, spanning rural water-supply and sanitation, agricultural water-use efficiency, flood/drought risk reduction and environmental recovery (APPs, basin revitalisation). Executed through several sector secretariats/entities coordinated by a programme management unit (UGP) with a steering committee.

## Teoria da mudança e indicadores
A.1 Theory of Change: ADEQUATE — the CC lays out a clear problem-to-solution chain (water scarcity/flood risk and rural sanitation gaps → governance instruments + multiple-use infrastructure and rural WASH → water-security outcomes), but causal assumptions linking capacity-building/governance outputs to resilience outcomes are implicit rather than spelled out, so it falls short of STRONG.
A.2 Results Framework: ADEQUATE — VERIFIED count: 22 indicators, matching the header. Mix of outcome (water-quality/turbidity improvement, flood-loss reduction, rural access to water/sanitation) and output indicators (studies, instruments, systems and hectares implemented); approximate split is roughly half outcome, half output, with the NT itself flagging that some indicators resemble delivered products rather than impact measures. Benchmarks used: a historical agricultural-loss series (DERAL/SEAB) as the baseline for the loss-reduction indicator; a 2010 census baseline for two rural-access indicators (dated, pre-project). No explicit verification-mechanism/data-source column is stated for the indicator set — NT silent on this and the CC text does not show one — so treat A.2 verification as largely ABSENT. All indicators carry a stated baseline year/period label (a specific project year, "2024", or "census 2010"), but about half show a baseline value of exactly zero. We treated zero baselines tied to a brand-new physical/environmental deliverable (reservoir volume, green/grey infrastructure works, recovered APP area, turbidity-improved basins, sustainable-practice area, rural waste-collection systems) as explained by the indicator's own "new asset" framing and counted them as defined; zero baselines for capacity-building head-counts and a satisfaction survey were not explicitly explained and were counted as undefined. Recomputed baseline_defined_share ≈0.73 (16/22); baseline_with_year_share ≈0.73 (every indicator with a defined baseline also states its reference period). No wide-range targets were found.

## Sustentabilidade e riscos
A.5 Financial Sustainability: ADEQUATE — O&M is not separately quantified as a line item, but the CC states that no new hires are needed and that continuing activities will be absorbed into the regular budget of the executing bodies; several state sector funds plus a dedicated water-security program are named as the funding source for post-project continuity, and an annex reportedly details sustainability costs where applicable.
A.3 Risk Assessment: ADEQUATE — a risk matrix is presented covering legal/institutional acts, procurement, resettlement and environmental/social risks, each with mitigation measures; the NT rates this the strongest part of the risk analysis. Environmental licensing is explicitly deferred: licences "will be obtained during execution" rather than already in hand — a licensing-risk flag. No expropriation/resettlement is foreseen, as works are said to run on public land or land already acquired by municipalities via agreements.
Counterpart: stated at roughly 29% of total cost, above the 20% floor, and its origin is said to comply with the governing COFIEX counterpart resolution — but legally still "to be secured": the CC lists an authorising law and a regulatory decree as pending, plus future inclusion in the PPA/LDO/LOA planning instruments, i.e. not yet enacted at CC stage.
A.4 Implementation: ADEQUATE — institutional arrangement with a lead executing secretariat and several co-executing agencies is detailed with a responsibility matrix and named UGP functions (coordination, safeguards, procurement, finance, M&E); the borrower is shown in-system to have a portfolio of prior external-credit operations with an average performance rated "Bom" by the NT's own methodology, supporting execution-capacity credibility.
