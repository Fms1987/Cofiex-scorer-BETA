## Resumo
- Investment loan, directly executed by a state government (direct delivery model, dedicated program-management unit under the state agriculture secretariat).
- Finances rural development: family-farming productivity/competitiveness support (technical assistance, calls for subprojects and business plans), sustainable/climate-resilient production practices, applied research/extension/innovation (modernizing public agricultural research and extension institutes), and performance-based rural-road rehabilitation and maintenance (plus complementary structures such as bridges/culverts).
- Main components: (i) economic dynamization and sustainable/resilient production systems for family farming (calls/grants, technical assistance, applied research); (ii) complementary rural productive infrastructure modernization — rural-road rehabilitation/maintenance under performance-based contracts, by far the largest component; (iii) rural innovation, science, technology and knowledge-sharing (modernizing research/extension infrastructure); (iv) project management, institutional development and governance.
- Entity type: state government with an established rural extension/research network and some prior experience with multilateral lenders.

## Teoria da mudança e indicadores
- A.1 Theory of Change: ADEQUATE — the reviewing NTs consistently scored the top design sub-criterion (problem-solution-result coherence) highly, but both flagged that many components are framed as open calls/edital mechanisms rather than pre-defined subprojects, and that most products lack detailed specification of what will be done and how, indicating a need for further interlocution with beneficiaries before implementation.
- A.2 Results Framework: WEAK — 10 verified indicators, all with a stated baseline of zero (0.0 defined share). The CC itself explains this is a reporting convention adopted to make the program's own attributable results more measurable, explicitly stating this does NOT mean the state has no pre-existing action in these areas — which is the opposite of a justified counterfactual, so none of the ten were counted as baseline-defined. Both reviewing NTs criticized the framework further: missing outcome-level indicators (e.g. income gains for family farmers), an unclear measurement methodology for the road-maintenance-condition indicator, one indicator with no stated calculation formula at all, and (in one review) no stated target dates within the program's timeframe.
- Outcome-vs-output split: almost entirely output-type (counts of organizations/members benefited, projects contracted, units/centers modernized, km of road intervened, reference units implemented), with at most one borderline outcome-type count (properties with a "sustainability curriculum" adopted).
- Benchmarks used: none of the typical cross-sector benchmarks; road condition is assessed via an existing state visual-inspection methodology (not otherwise named).
- Internal inconsistency flagged: the CC's own narrative undercuts its "zero baseline" framing by admitting ongoing prior action exists in the same areas, which both reviewing NTs treated as a results-framework weakness rather than an accepted design choice.

## Sustentabilidade e riscos
- A.5 Financial Sustainability: WEAK/ABSENT — both reviewed NTs scored this criterion very low; no specific post-project maintenance-cost figures or dedicated financial-sustainability instruments were presented, beyond a general statement that incremental costs from new research/extension facilities would be absorbed into the responsible agencies' ordinary budgets.
- Counterpart (20%, matches CC): sourced from the state treasury against cited constitutional tax-revenue articles; no counterpart-specific gap was flagged — the legal gap concerns the overall borrowing-authorization law, still to be submitted to the state legislature.
- A.3 Risk Assessment: a risk matrix is present. Legal/institutional risk is acknowledged (authorizing law to be prepared for the state legislature) but both reviewed NTs noted the absence of specific mitigating actions for that legal-approval risk itself. Procurement risk (undersubscribed/failed tenders) is identified with mitigations (feasible eligibility parameters, broad publication of notices). Environmental studies are explicitly deferred to a later engineering ante-project phase, to follow state/national rules and the lender's safeguards, with a promised environmental-and-social risk-management plan covering vulnerable/traditional populations. No resettlement or expropriation is expected, since works stay within the existing road right-of-way.
- A.4 Implementation: a dedicated program-management unit under the state agriculture secretariat, backed by an extensive existing field-office and research-station network; however, both reviewed NTs rated the proponent's historical lender-portfolio performance low (numeric ratings toward the bottom of the scale) based on registry data, and flagged missing information on the specific experience of the implementation team.
