## Resumo
- Modality: investment loan from CAF to a large capital municipality, with federal guarantee; counterpart at the 20% minimum; execution of works by a municipal public works company with the urban infrastructure secretariat as contract manager.
- Financed: a structural urban road link built around a major new bridge, plus requalification of the connecting avenues (pavement, drainage, bus stops and priority lanes, protected cycle paths, accessible sidewalks, LED lighting, landscaping).
- Three components: roughly two-thirds road infrastructure and sustainable mobility; roughly 30% socio-environmental management, expropriation and resettlement (including environmental monitoring and compensation); a small share (about 3%) for programme management, supervision and external audit.
- Rationale is intra-urban connectivity in a densely populated, lower-income periphery with chronic congestion on the one existing main avenue.

## Teoria da mudança e indicadores
- A.1 Theory of Change: ADEQUATE. Seven numbered problems are mapped to solutions, six specific objectives and indicators, with explicit PPA correlations; the chain from new link to travel-time, traffic and emissions gains is plausible and supported by technical traffic studies, but assumptions (traffic redistribution, modal shift, bus reliability) are not stated as testable.
- A.2 Results Framework: ADEQUATE. 8 indicators, roughly 7 outcome-type and 1 output/process-type (approximate judgement); each carries a formula, data sources and an explicit SMART self-assessment. Verification relies on operator and traffic-agency records, field surveys and air-quality network data.
- Baselines: 7 of 8 give a value (share 0.88) and each carries a year or study date. The resettlement baseline of zero is counted as defined because the CC explains that no resettlement has occurred yet. The public-space-access indicator reuses a cycling-frequency survey as baseline, which does not measure the indicator, so it is counted undefined.
- Internal inconsistencies: bus-punctuality target is 90% in the SMART text but 50% in the target field, with a perception-survey baseline; the active-mode indicator is defined as a share of trips (formula numerator lists motorised modes) but targeted as +20% cycling use; the accident indicator mixes an old cycle-lane study from another period as baseline with a target phrased as "80% of the population with safe access"; baseline year for the traffic indicator differs between the SMART text and the baseline field.
- Benchmarks: national air-quality classes and a city mobility survey; accessibility norm for sidewalks. Strategic-area mapping is very broad (about a dozen PPA objectives claimed).

## Sustentabilidade e riscos
- A.5 Financial Sustainability: ADEQUATE. Post-project O&M is assigned by asset (road and lighting maintenance to the infrastructure secretariat and subprefecture; bus stops to the transit operator under existing zero-cost shelter contracts; environmental monitoring to the environment secretariat), with annual cost estimates per category and funding through the annual LOA. Some figures are citywide or regional rather than project-specific, and there is no multi-year table.
- A.3 Risk Assessment: eight risks with impact and probability. Environmental, expropriation/compensation and social risks are rated high probability; regulatory, contractual, schedule and operational are medium. Licensing is addressed (environment secretariat as licensing body, conditions to be met) but status per component is not shown.
- Counterpart: at the 20% minimum; secured through the municipal budget cycle but no specific LOA/PPA line is cited here.
- A.4 Implementation: public works company with a sizeable in-house engineering staff and a project-management system, a partner secretariat, and a long list of utility and agency co-actors with a RACI matrix; prior CAF and IBRD operations of the borrower are cited. A dedicated UGP is supported through the programme management component.
