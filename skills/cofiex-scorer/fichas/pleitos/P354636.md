## Resumo
- Co-financed investment loan (two multilateral lenders in parallel) to a state government, executed centrally by the state water and sewerage utility through a dedicated programme management unit; Union guarantee requested; 5-year disbursement.
- Second stage of a wider urban sanitation programme whose first stage is funded by another multilateral lender; this stage completes the sewer "belt" around the raw-water reservoirs of a metropolitan supply system.
- Components: (1) urban sanitation infrastructure - collection networks, household connections, trunk sewers, pumping stations, treatment plant expansion (roughly 80% of cost); (2) environmental requalification of two supply reservoirs (dredging, aquatic-weed removal, studies, water-quality monitoring, environmental education); (3) institutional strengthening, innovation and gender (lab upgrade, sludge-to-energy plant, IoT-based water-loss control in pilot sectors, gender/diversity workshops; roughly 13%); (4) administration, audit and evaluation.
- Counterpart at the 20% minimum, drawn from state tax revenue.

## Teoria da mudança e indicadores
- A.1 Theory of Change: ADEQUATE. Clear problem tree (unsewered catchment -> raw-water degradation and silting -> higher treatment cost and supply risk) with a matching set of interventions and an explicit sequencing rule (lake works only after sewer and treatment capacity are in place); assumptions such as household connection uptake are acknowledged and addressed by education and a tariff-subsidy scheme, but not tested.
- A.2 Results Framework: ADEQUATE. 7 indicators verified; approximately 5 outcome-type (sewer coverage, connection uptake, water-loss rates, weed volume) and 2 output-type (monitoring points, sludge volume). All 7 have a baseline value and all state a baseline year and a target year in the indicator name, so both shares = 1.0; one zero baseline (connection uptake in the unserved sub-basins) counted as defined because the CC explains the area has no network yet.
- Verification: no data-source column, but the programme funds a monitoring and evaluation consultancy (mid-term and final) and a water-quality monitoring programme; no external benchmark cited beyond a national sanitation ranking in the diagnosis.
- Inconsistencies: objective text sets a connection-uptake floor in people that does not match the 70% indicator target; the monitoring-points formula uses a 60-point reference while the text speaks of about 50; the sludge indicator is named a reduction but expressed as absolute volumes whose target implies about a 95% cut; formulas of the "volume x (1 - reduction)" type embed the target.
- NT notes no indicators for several stated results (operating-cost reduction, gender/diversity workshops, lake water quality, dredging); no outcome indicator for the reservoirs themselves.

## Sustentabilidade e riscos
- A.5 Financial Sustainability: PARTIAL. CC argues extra billing from new connections (stated per month) will exceed the extra outsourced treatment O&M, backed by the first stage's expected revenue uplift and a state social-tariff law that subsidises poor households; but NT could not tell whether the utility's stated operating base already includes this stage, and there is no O&M plan or table for the new assets (energy plant, lake works, IoT systems). Default/arrears around a third of billing is acknowledged.
- A.3 Risk Assessment: narrative sections, no matrix. Covers legal acts (state authorising law only after COFIEX approval, draft agenda already shared with the government leader in the legislature), procurement (low bidder interest, weak contractors; mitigations by market-based cost estimates and technical qualification), land for pumping stations (all unoccupied, tenure being surveyed), environmental (EIA/RIMA to be financed for dredging and weed removal inside a protected park), social (connection resistance, temporary disruption). No resettlement.
- Counterpart: "to be secured" - budget line to be placed in the investment budget of the state utility in the next budget bill; authorising law pending.
- A.4 Implementation: programme management unit already constituted with a handful of utility staff and running the first stage; special bidding commission exists; a management/supervision firm will be hired; external-creditor portfolio found in SIGS with satisfactory performance. Basic designs complete and a preliminary licence for the sewer system in hand; installation licences and park-related studies pending.
