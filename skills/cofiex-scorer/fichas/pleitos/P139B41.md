## Resumo
- Modality: investment loan from IBRD with Union guarantee to a non-dependent state-owned port company (not a state or municipality), no counterpart (waiver invoked under Art. 34), requested under the environmental/climate sublimit.
- Finances port infrastructure for a low-carbon fuels and energy-transition hub: new multipurpose and bulk berths, structural recovery of existing berths, recovery and expansion of liquid-bulk piers, and a renewable-powered shore-power and charging component.
- Four components; the new berths take roughly 60% of cost, the electrification component roughly 20%, the rest goes to recovery works.
- Delivery: direct execution by the company through a project management unit set up in-house; procurement under IBRD rules, which the company has not used before. Dredging and part of the works are said to come from own resources.

## Teoria da mudança e indicadores
- A.1 Theory of Change: ADEQUATE. Narrative chain from infrastructure bottleneck to private investment, jobs and lower emissions is clear, but it rests on unsecured assumptions (offtakers reaching investment decisions, grid connection, international fuel regulation) that are treated as risks rather than as testable assumptions.
- A.2 Results Framework: WEAK. 4 indicators verified, all outcome or impact level (jobs created, average income change in surrounding municipalities, complementary private investment attracted, avoided emissions); no output indicator for berth capacity, cargo handled or shore-power connections, so the physical components are not measured.
- Baselines: jobs, investment attracted and income are incremental measures; the zero baselines for jobs and investment were counted as defined (net additions attributable to the project), the income baseline is given per municipality, and the emissions baseline is a monthly value. baseline_defined_share 1.0, but none states a clear reference year (the emissions "period" is the monitoring window), so baseline_with_year_share 0.0.
- Verification: official labour and income databases and port-sector statistics named; emissions follow a World Bank methodology or specialised assessment; the indirect-jobs and leverage figures depend on modelling assumptions.
- Inconsistencies: income indicator unit is a currency amount but the formula is a percentage change; emissions baseline unit/scale unclear against a percentage target; attribution of jobs and private investment to the project is not isolated; leverage target is very large relative to the loan.
- NT view: only the competitiveness-type indicator (investment attracted) and the emissions indicator supported area scoring; logistics and trade areas lacked indicators.

## Sustentabilidade e riscos
- A.5 Financial Sustainability: ADEQUATE. Post-project incremental operating cost stated as a share of capex (low single-digit percent per year, including maintenance, insurance, staff, security), borne by the company's own resources, with specific maintenance plans per asset still being prepared; no revenue or traffic projection provided in the text read (the cash flow is cited but not shown).
- Counterpart: none requested (waiver under Art. 34); the company says dredging and some works use own resources, which clashes with the loan-funded berth works description.
- A.3 Risk Assessment: 5 risks with impact, probability and mitigation: environmental/dredging and hazardous-fuel handling, social (worker influx, demobilisation), procurement under lender rules for a first-time borrower, power-transmission availability for green-fuel projects, and regulatory/international market uncertainty (rated high-high). Environmental licensing for the dredging is reported as already authorised; no resettlement.
- A.4 Implementation: in-house project management unit to be staffed from company personnel; lender training offered on procurement; development-assistance trust funds may cover supervision; no prior experience with this lender.
- Market dependency: private tenants' investment decisions are assumed, not contracted for the loan-funded assets; the central risk is demand for low-carbon cargo.
