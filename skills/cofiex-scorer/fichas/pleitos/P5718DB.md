## Resumo
- Investment operation, direct execution by a state government (not intermediated), blending two external co-lenders alongside a legally secured counterpart near the regulatory floor (about a fifth of cost).
- Finances a rural development / environmental-restoration program targeting high-poverty, low-IDH territories within a threatened biome: production-systems resilience, environmental protection/payment-for-services, water and sanitation access, rural roads/connectivity, land-tenure regularization and institutional strengthening.
- Delivery is direct, through an already-existing project-management unit reused from a prior sister program, co-executed with producer associations/cooperatives and partner municipalities under cooperation agreements.
- Entity type: state government, subnational-general subtype; targets include indigenous and traditional (quilombola) communities within the intervention area.

## Teoria da mudança e indicadores
- A.1 Theory of Change: STRONG — an explicit diagnosis (poverty incidence, soil/forest degradation, market-access failures, climate exposure, water/sanitation gaps) is mapped to four thematic solution axes and named products, with stated sustainability mechanisms (continued technical assistance, institutionalization via the state's payment-for-environmental-services program, capacity building of local organizations).
- A.2 Results Framework: ADEQUATE — 11 VERIFIED indicators cover most result areas (beneficiary reach, agricultural income, food security, land-tenure regularization, water/sanitation/connectivity access, environmental recovery), with clear attribution formulas, but all 11 carry a stated baseline value of zero with no stated rationale (new-asset/counterfactual) in the source text — counted here as UNDEFINED rather than explained (baseline_defined_share = 0.0); none state a reference year either (baseline_with_year_share = 0.0).
- Outcome-vs-output split (approximate): more outcome-leaning than typical — several indicators measure beneficiary-level welfare change (income increase, food-security improvement, land tenure, service access) rather than pure physical output; two indicators (agroforestry hectares, recovered degraded hectares) are output/physical-asset measures.
- Verification mechanism: present — CC states the project will use the two lenders' own standardized monitoring-and-evaluation systems.
- Benchmarks used: national/state socioeconomic references (IDHM, Census, SNIS, IBGE) are used for diagnosis/targeting, not as external indicator benchmarks.
- The NT available for this pleito is a meeting-roster/agenda document, not a dedicated analytical note: it is silent on indicator-level quality and on the Theory of Change narrative (see avaliação).

## Sustentabilidade e riscos
- A.5 Financial Sustainability: relies on continuity of technical assistance and institutionalization through the state's own payment-for-environmental-services program plus strengthened producer organizations/market access, rather than a quantified post-project O&M budget; no OPEX cost table is presented (consistent with no mention of a quantified sustainment budget in the source).
- A.3 Risk Assessment: addressed narratively by category rather than as a formal matrix — legal/institutional (pending state authorizing law as the first required act), procurement (lender procedures plus a long-standing track record of grant pass-through to producer associations), environmental (climate variability and land-use/agrochemical risks, mitigated through the agroecological components), and social (indigenous and quilombola communities, addressed via free-prior-informed-consultation and dedicated field-team training) risks; proponent self-rates the social risk to these communities as low (not independently verified in the sources reviewed).
- Expropriation and resettlement: explicitly ruled out by the proponent ("no expropriations," "no resettlements" in the Project).
- Counterpart: secured in amount — counterpart share (about a fifth of cost) sits just above the regulatory floor, to be funded from the state budget "with fiscal responsibility," per the CC; no LOA/budget-line citation is quoted in the source text reviewed, so the funding instrument itself is not fully specified here.
- A.4 Implementation: UGP already exists, reusing the structure/team of a prior, similar sister program, which the CC cites as already familiar with this project typology; co-execution is formalized with producer associations/cooperatives and partner municipalities via cooperation instruments.
